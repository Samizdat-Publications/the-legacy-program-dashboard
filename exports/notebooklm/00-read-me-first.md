# Read me first: what this board is and how to read the rest of these files

This is a research board about an alleged legacy programme to recover and reverse engineer non human craft, and about the modern fight to force that record into the open. Everything in these files comes out of the board's own data, with every claim carrying the tier that says how strongly it is evidenced and the source string it rests on.

## What the board holds

The board carries 1125 nodes: 338 people and 787 entities, which are institutions, programmes, documents, events, locations, domains and pieces of legislation. Those nodes are joined by 2661 hand written links and carry 5839 tiered claims between them. 1050 dated events sit on the timeline, 149 nodes are placed on the programme map, 5 patents are tracked in full prosecution detail, 8 authored threads walk a reader through the strongest sequences, and 17 Freedom of Information Act requests are drafted and waiting to be filed.

Of the claims, 3648 are Tier 1, 1618 are Tier 2 and 573 are Tier 3. That split is the point of the board. It is built so that nobody can read it without knowing which parts rest on a primary record and which parts rest on testimony or on inference.

## The claim tiers, in plain words

Every claim on this board carries a number from 1 to 3. That number is about evidence strength and nothing else.

- **Tier 1, documented, a primary record exists.** A document exists and has been located. The source string names it.
- **Tier 2, a named person on the record.** Somebody with a name said it, in testimony, in an interview or in print. The claim is evidence that they said it.
- **Tier 3, a stated inference with the reasoning given.** The board is reasoning from what it holds. The reasoning is written into the claim rather than hidden.

When a claim is worded as an allegation rather than as an established fact, these files say so next to the tier. That flag is generated from the wording the board already uses, words such as alleged, reportedly, purported, unsupported and unverified.

## The node tiers, which are not credibility

Nodes carry a letter, A, B or C. This is the single most misread thing on the board, so it is worth saying slowly: the letter measures proximity to the alleged programme, not how credible the node is. A tier C node can be entirely real and entirely well documented. It just sits further out.

- **Tier A.** Named in sworn testimony or primary documents as directly inside the programme.
- **Tier B.** Documented channel and capability, programme role indicated but not yet nailed to a primary source.
- **Tier C.** Contextual, real and relevant, with no direct programme linkage on the record yet.

Tiers are assigned globally rather than by the researcher who worked that subject, and a node with no sourced URL has its score cut hard, so nothing unsourced can hold tier A. Each node carries a written rationale showing its rank and inputs, and those rationales are reproduced in the band files.

## The three axes: C, A and D

Each node also carries three scores from 0 to 3. They are never summed, because collapsing them destroys the information: a contractor can sit at the centre of the custody chain and be completely absent from the disclosure fight.

- **C, custody proximity.** How close is this entity to alleged possession of recovered material or craft?
- **A, access level.** What special access did it hold: special access program, unacknowledged special access program, or waived unacknowledged?
- **D, disclosure role.** How central is it to forcing the record into the open?

So a node written as C3 A2 D0 is deep in the custody question, holds serious access, and plays no part in disclosure. A node written as C0 A0 D3 is a legislator or a journalist driving the whole fight with no programme access at all.

## Interested parties do not get to be their own best evidence

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, AARO, and the Department of Defense publishing domains including defense.gov are treated on this board as interested parties. A claim sourced to them caps at Tier 2 unless the claim is about what they said. The reasoning is simple: publishing something on aaro.mil makes that page a primary record of the statement, not a primary record of the fact asserted in it. AARO reporting what AARO found is testimony from a party to the dispute.

This is not a rhetorical stance, it is enforced in the data. 264 claims on this board carry a source string that records the demotion in writing, usually with the phrase DEMOTED from Tier 1 followed by the reason and a note that a primary replacement is still needed. Those strings are reproduced unchanged in these files.

The mirror of that rule is that denials are kept in full and sourced, treated as artefacts of the concealment effort rather than as counterweights to it. A denial is a dated, signed, attributable statement, which makes it evidence about the denier. There is a whole file of them here.
