## United States Department of Defense (secondary designation: Department of War)

United States Department of Defense (secondary designation: Department of War) is a institution on the board; board tier A, named in sworn testimony or primary documents as directly inside the programme; dates 1947-09-18; axes C3 A3 D1; 45 connections; in the retrieval band.

In one line: Executive department; owner of the SAP enterprise and of every alleged recovery compartment.

### What it is

The Department of Defense, renamed the Department of War by executive action in 2025, is the cabinet department that has run every official US government UAP investigation from Project SIGN in December 1947 to AARO today. It holds the special access programme architecture, the counterintelligence organisations that respond to incursions on military installations, and the security review office that clears what its personnel may say in public. It is the institution against which the legacy programme allegation is principally made.

### Why it is implicated

The Department has controlled every official UAP investigation for seventy-nine years and, by its own inspector general's finding, still had no coordinated approach and no comprehensive response plan eighteen months after Congress ordered it to build one. Its structure gives it the two things a concealed recovery programme requires: an unacknowledged special access programme system whose existence need not be disclosed, and counterintelligence organisations designated as the first responders to any anomalous incursion on any installation or range. Congress has twice legislated as though it believes unreported UAP compartments exist, first by giving AARO statutory access notwithstanding all access controls, then by forbidding the expenditure of funds on UAP-related special access activity not reported to the intelligence committees. A former DoD official who managed a Special Access Program on behalf of the White House and the National Security Council testified under oath that the United States is in possession of UAP technologies and that the effort is hidden from elected representatives. The Department's own Historical Record Report concedes that a controlled access compartment was expanded in 2021 to protect UAP reverse-engineering.

### The longer account

The Department's UAP history is documented by its own inspector general. The first official DoD UAP activity was PROJECT SIGN, established by the Air Force Chief of Staff in December 1947. From 1947 to 1949 Air Force personnel investigated 243 reported sightings. In 1952 the Air Force Director of Intelligence initiated PROJECT BLUE BOOK, which investigated more than 12,000 sightings before its termination in 1969. Then, in the inspector general's own words, the DoD did not officially look at UAP again until mid-2000, when select members of Congress initiated and funded a programme to study UAP under the direction of the Defense Intelligence Agency. That sentence is worth pausing on. The Department's own watchdog records that the resumption of UAP work was not a Departmental decision at all. It was a congressional earmark inserted over the Department's disinterest, and when the Senate Majority Leader asked for it to be made a Special Access Program in June 2009 the Deputy Secretary of Defense declined on the advice of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence.

That forty-year gap is the load-bearing claim of the official account and the weakest point in it. The Department asks the public to accept that between BLUE BOOK's closure in 1969 and AAWSAP's standup in 2008 it did nothing on the subject at all, in the same period in which it was operating an unacknowledged special access programme architecture whose whole purpose is that its contents cannot be inferred from budget documents or organisation charts.

Congress does not appear to accept it. Section 1683 of the FY2022 NDAA forced the creation of an office. Section 1673 of the FY2023 NDAA forced the creation of a reporting mechanism usable notwithstanding non-disclosure agreements and free from reprisal. Section 6802 of the FY2023 NDAA forced the historical report. Section 1687 of the FY2024 NDAA went further and prohibited the use of authorised funds for any special access or restricted access programme activity relating to UAP that has not been reported to the congressional committees by the Director of National Intelligence, and extended the restriction to contractor Independent Research and Development. Legislators do not write funding prohibitions against activities they believe do not exist.

The Deputy Secretary's July 2022 establishment memorandum is the Department's own consolidation instrument. It made AARO the authoritative office for all UAP and UAP-related activities, disestablished the Navy task force, and directed every DoD component holding UAP data, analysis, contracts or other material to synchronise immediately with AARO. If material and paperwork from a legacy programme existed anywhere in the Department, that memorandum is the order that would have moved it, and it moved it into an office reporting to the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security.

The inspector general's evaluation, DODIG-2023-109, is the best independent measure of the Department's conduct. Its unclassified summary, released 24 January 2024, found that the DoD had not used a coordinated approach to detect, report, collect, analyze, and identify UAP; that DoD components had largely excluded the geographic combatant commands, the organisations actually responsible for detecting and preventing threats against the United States; that components had developed varying informal processes; and that the Department had issued no comprehensive UAP response plan. The evaluation notes that the Military Services and counterintelligence organisations had sent some UAP incident reports to AARO but that the Department does not formally require them to do so. It also records that the Secretary of the Navy, the Secretary of the Air Force and the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff each declined to provide official comments for the final report. That is three of the Department's most senior officials refusing to go on the record with the inspector general on this subject.

The response architecture matters as much as the analytic one. AARO's FY2025 report states that the military Services' counterintelligence, law enforcement and security agencies are the cognizant authorities responsible for responding to UAP incidents and incursions on all installations and ranges, and that AARO aims to fully implement the Joint Staff's classified UAP GENADMIN from February 2025. The doctrine governing what the United States military does when an anomalous object comes down on its property is classified, and the organisations that would execute it are Army Counterintelligence, the Naval Criminal Investigative Service and the Air Force Office of Special Investigations. Those are the same organisations that, in the concealment argument, would administer the silence.

Luis Elizondo, who told the House Oversight Committee in November 2024 that in his last government position he managed a Special Access Program on behalf of the White House and the National Security Council, testified in writing that UAP are real, that the United States is in possession of UAP technologies, and that the country is in the midst of a multi-decade secretive arms race funded by misallocated taxpayer dollars and hidden from elected representatives and oversight bodies. He testified that a small cadre within the government has created a culture of suppression and intimidation including unwarranted criminal investigations and harassment, and that the Pentagon's public affairs office employs a professional psychological operations officer as the single point of contact for UAP inquiries from citizens and the media.

The Department's posture since February 2026 has been transparency by executive channel. Secretary Hegseth committed to full compliance with a presidential disclosure directive, and a War Department official told DefenseScoop that AARO was working in close coordination with the White House to consolidate existing UAP records collections and facilitate the expeditious release of never-before-seen UAP information. The releases run through a departmental website rather than through the statutory National Archives collection, which puts the pace and the redactions back inside the Department.

### What it has denied

The Department, through AARO, has denied since March 2024 that it has ever recovered, possessed or reverse-engineered non-human technology, and its annual reports state that all resolved cases are attributable to prosaic objects. It has never publicly enumerated its special access programme inventory in this area, and its response doctrine, the February 2025 Joint Staff UAP GENADMIN, remains classified. Three of its most senior officials declined to comment on the record for the inspector general's evaluation of its UAP conduct.

Roles the board assigns it: retrieval, reverse-engineering, concealment, funding, oversight, custody.

## Central Intelligence Agency

### What it is

The Central Intelligence Agency is the United States civilian foreign intelligence service. Its documented involvement with the UFO question runs from the Office of Scientific Intelligence study group of 1952 through its convening of the Robertson Panel in January 1953 to its management of the U-2 and OXCART cover stories through the 1950s and 1960s. Its own in-house historian published a detailed account of that record in Studies in Intelligence in 1997.

### Why it is implicated

The CIA is the only agency in this dossier set that has published, in its own classified-origin journal, a first-person institutional admission that it directed the government to debunk UFO reports, concealed its sponsorship of the panel that recommended doing so, and knowingly allowed the Air Force to issue misleading and deceptive statements about more than half of all UFO reports for over a decade. That is not an allegation; it is the CIA's own historian describing the machinery of a UFO cover story and calling it by name. Once an agency has demonstrably run one UFO cover story for a real classified programme, the argument that it would not run a second for a different one loses its force. The Agency also sits at the point in the concealment argument where a recovery capability would have to live if the operations occurred on foreign soil, and it has not appeared among the agencies transmitting records to the statutory National Archives collection.

### The longer account

The primary document here is Gerald K. Haines, CIA's Role in the Study of UFOs, 1947-90, published in Studies in Intelligence in 1997 and posted on the Agency's own website. Haines was a government historian with access to Agency files. What he wrote is more damaging to the Agency's credibility on this subject than anything a critic has written.

The Robertson Panel convened in January 1953 at CIA's instigation, following a recommendation from the Intelligence Advisory Committee to enlist selected scientists to review and appraise the available evidence in light of pertinent scientific theories. The panellists had expertise in nuclear physics, high-energy physics, radar, electronics and geophysics. Their formal conclusion was that there was no evidence of a direct threat to national security and no evidence of extraterrestrial visitation. Their operational recommendation was something else entirely. Haines writes that to meet these problems, the panel recommended that the National Security Council debunk UFO reports and institute a policy of public education to reassure the public of the lack of evidence behind UFOs. It suggested using the mass media, advertising, business clubs, schools, and even the Disney corporation to get the message across. In the same breath, reporting at the height of McCarthyism, it recommended that private UFO groups including the Civilian Flying Saucer Investigators in Los Angeles and the Aerial Phenomena Research Organization be monitored for subversive activities.

That is a US intelligence agency recommending, at national security council level, a domestic public-opinion management campaign on a scientific subject and the surveillance of American civil society organisations that took an interest in it. The Agency then concealed its own role. When public pressure built for release of the panel report in 1956, Philip Strong, Deputy Assistant Director of the Office of Scientific Intelligence, refused to declassify it and declined to disclose CIA sponsorship of the panel. Haines records that the Agency instead prepared a sanitized version which deleted any reference to CIA and avoided mention of any psychological warfare potential in the UFO controversy. ## United States Air Force

United States Air Force is a institution on the board; board tier A, named in sworn testimony or primary documents as directly inside the programme; dates 1947-09-18; axes C3 A3 D1; 22 connections; in the retrieval band.

### What it is

The United States Air Force, established as an independent military department on 18 September 1947, eleven weeks after the Roswell press release issued by the 509th Bomb Group at Roswell Army Air Field. It inherited and ran every official American UFO project -- Project SIGN in 1948, Project GRUDGE in 1949 and Project BLUE BOOK from 1952 to 1969 -- at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, Ohio, the installation every retrieval account since 1947 names as the destination of recovered material.

### Why it is implicated

The Air Force is the origin point of both the American UFO record and the American denial record, and it is the only institution on this board that is simultaneously the collector, the analyst, the custodian and the public voice. Under AFR 200-2 it required reports to flow to the Air Technical Intelligence Center -- the foreign-materiel exploitation shop -- and restricted public discussion to cases conclusively identified as conventional. It closed Blue Book publicly in December 1969 while its own units continued to run a Foreign Materiel Program whose 1967 unit history lists MOON DUST and BLUE FLY alongside MIG-17 exploitation and describes airlifting recovered materiel to Wright-Patterson. And in February 1985 an Air Force spokesman, reminded that his own department had published Aurora's figures in an unclassified procurement document, said his orders were still to say only that it was a classified program.

### The longer account

Everything this board argues about custody runs through one service, and the useful thing a dossier can do is set the public record and the internal record side by side using only documents.

The public record is well known and well sourced. From 1947 to 1969, 12,618 sightings were reported to Project BLUE BOOK, of which 701 remain unidentified; on 17 December 1969 the Secretary of the Air Force announced its termination, resting on the University of Colorado study and a National Academy of Sciences review; the files went to the National Archives as 94 rolls of microfilm, T-1206. Two later reports attributed the Roswell debris to Project MOGUL and the bodies to anthropomorphic test dummies dropped in the 1950s -- an explanation requiring witnesses to have misdated events by up to a decade.

The internal record is the same service, described by its own units. AFR 200-2 of 26 August 1953, signed by order of the Secretary of the Air Force by Nathan Twining, designated ATIC as the analysis authority, directed that 'All evidence will be forwarded to ATIC', instructed units to safeguard materiel against 'defacing or alteration which would reduce its value for technical analysis', and limited public release to objects 'conclusively identified by ATIC as a conventional aerial device'. JANAP 146 was still listing unidentified flying objects as a reportable category in 1977.

None of that establishes anomalous material. All of it establishes that the collection channel the public was told had closed was not the only channel, and that the service said so in documents it classified rather than published.

## Roswell Incident

Roswell Incident is a event on the board; board tier A, named in sworn testimony or primary documents as directly inside the programme; dates 1947; axes C3 A2 D3; 15 connections; in the retrieval band.

In one line: Recovery and retraction, 7-9 July 1947.

### What it is

On 8 July 1947 the Roswell Army Air Field public information office announced that personnel of the 509th Bomb Group had recovered a crashed "flying disc" on a ranch in the Roswell region. The following day the Commanding General of Eighth Air Force at Fort Worth announced the object was a radar-tracking weather balloon. The Air Force issued two further explanations decades later: a 1994 report attributing the debris to a Project MOGUL balloon train, and a 1997 report attributing reports of "alien bodies" to anthropomorphic test dummies and to two later aircraft and balloon mishaps.

### Why it is implicated

The argument here is not about what fell. It is about the file. When the General Accounting Office audited the record in 1994-95 at the request of Rep. Steven Schiff, it found that Roswell Army Air Field's administrative records for March 1945 through December 1949 and its outgoing messages for October 1946 through December 1949 had been destroyed, and that "the document disposition form does not indicate what organization or person destroyed the records and when or under what authority." The unit records of the 1395th Military Police Company (Aviation), the unit that would have cordoned any site, could not be shown ever to have been retired to a records depository at all. Only two documents originating in 1947 survived the search. The FBI teletype that survives states the recovered object was being sent to Wright Field for examination, and GAO's review of Air Materiel Command records for 1947 to 1950 found nothing about the receipt or examination of any Roswell debris. A government that recovered a weather balloon does not lose four years of a bomb group's message traffic without a disposal authority, and does not fail to log the arrival of the item at the laboratory it says received it.

### The longer account

Roswell is the origin node of the entire concealment argument, and the strongest evidence for it is not testimony. It is an audit report signed by a GAO director in 1995.

The sequence of public statements is short and well documented. On 8 July 1947 the RAAF public information office reported the recovery of a "flying disc," credited to personnel of the 509th Bomb Group. Within a day the story was replaced: Eighth Air Force at Fort Worth announced a crashed radar-tracking balloon. The July 1947 combined history report of the 509th Bomb Group and RAAF, one of only two surviving 1947 documents, records that the public information office "was kept quite busy . . . answering inquiries on the 'flying disc,' which was reported to be in [the] possession of the 509th Bomb Group. The object turned out to be a radar tracking balloon." The commanding officer certified by signature that the report was a complete and accurate account of RAAF activities for that month. The second surviving document is an FBI teletype from the Dallas field office dated 8 July 1947, whose authenticity an FBI spokesperson confirmed to GAO, reporting that an Eighth Air Force headquarters official had telephoned to describe the recovery near Roswell of "a hexagonal-shaped disc suspended from a large balloon by cable," and that "the disc and balloon were being sent to Wright Field . . . for examination."

That is the entire 1947 documentary record. Everything else was gone by the time anyone looked.

GAO looked hard. Its team worked from March 1994 to June 1995 across the National Archives in Washington and at College Park, the records center at Suitland, the National Personnel Records Center in St. Louis, the Air Force Safety Agency at Kirtland, the Air Force History Support Office at Bolling, NSA at Fort Meade, the Army Central Security Facility, CIA at Langley, FBI headquarters and elsewhere, and queried the National Security Council, the Office of Science and Technology Policy, the Department of Energy, the FBI, DOD and CIA in writing. It reviewed classified material. What it reported was a hole shaped exactly like the event.

The Chief Archivist of the National Personnel Records Center documented for GAO that RAAF records covering finance and accounting, supplies, buildings and grounds and other general administrative matters from March 1945 through December 1949, together with RAAF outgoing messages from October 1946 through December 1949, had been destroyed, and that "the document disposition form did not properly indicate the authority under which the disposal action was taken." GAO's flat summary sentence is the one that matters: "although some of the records concerning Roswell activities had been destroyed, there was no information available regarding when or under what authority the records were destroyed." The Chief Archivist offered the mitigating view that many Air Force organizational records of that period were destroyed without a disposition citation, and GAO noted that its own review of the destruction forms for RAAF's 1950 outgoing messages supported that view. That is a fair caveat and it is in the record. It does not restore the messages.

A separate gap sits underneath it. GAO specifically sought the records of the military units assigned to RAAF in 1947 - the 509th Bomb Group, the 1st Air Transport Unit, the 427th Army Air Force Base Unit, and the 1395th Military Police Company (Aviation). Disposition forms from St. Louis show a Walker Air Force Base records officer transferring the first three unit histories to the Army's Kansas City depository in 1953. Of the fourth, GAO wrote: "We could not locate any documentation indicating that records of the 1395th Military Police Company (Aviation) were ever retired to the National Personnel Records Center or its predecessor depositories." The military police company is the unit that secures a crash site. Its paper is the only unit paper that does not exist.

The third gap closes the loop. Because the surviving FBI teletype says the material went to Wright Field, GAO tried to find the regulation governing how such debris should be handled and reported: "We were unable to locate any applicable regulation." It then reviewed Air Materiel Command records at Wright Field from 1947 to 1950 and "found no records mentioning the Roswell crash or the examination by Air Materiel Command personnel of any debris recovered from the crash." The item arrives nowhere. GAO also established that in 1947 Army regulations required air accident reports to be maintained permanently, that four air accidents were reported in New Mexico in July 1947 and all occurred after 8 July involving military aircraft, and that Air Force officials said no requirement existed to report a weather balloon crash - which is a clean explanation for the absence of an accident report and no explanation at all for the absence of the message traffic.

CIA's answer is its own artifact. In March 1995 the Agency's Executive Director told GAO that earlier UFO record searches had produced nothing on Roswell but that "it was unclear whether the CIA had ever conducted a search for records specifically relating to Roswell," and ordered one. On 30 May 1995 he reported that a search on the term "Roswell, New Mexico" across all CIA databases produced no documents. Forty-eight years after the event and one year into a congressional audit, the Agency was not sure it had ever looked.

The two Air Force reports arrived on either side of the GAO audit and moved the explanation twice. The 1994 report, announced by Secretary of the Air Force Sheila Widnall on 8 September 1994, concluded that the most likely source of the recovered wreckage was a Project MOGUL balloon train, a then-classified acoustic effort to detect Soviet nuclear tests; DOD told GAO that this report "represents the extent of DOD records or information concerning the Roswell crash." The 1997 report, The Roswell Report: Case Closed, addressed the body reports and concluded that "Air Force activities which occurred over a period of many years have been consolidated and are now represented to have occurred in two or three days in July 1947," that the "aliens" were anthropomorphic test dummies carried aloft by high-altitude balloons, and that claims of bodies at the RAAF hospital most likely combine a 1956 KC-97 accident that killed eleven airmen and a 1959 manned balloon mishap that injured two pilots. The dummy drops and both mishaps postdate July 1947 by between seven and twelve years. The Air Force's answer to that is that witness memory compressed the decade. It is a testable claim and it is on the record under signature, which is what makes it useful here.

The chain begins with Silas Mason Newton, a Denver oil promoter whose name first reached the FBI on 24 September 1938 on a suspected wire-fraud referral, and Leo (Leonard) A. GeBauer, a Phoenix electronics merchant who appears in the literature under the pseudonym 'Dr. Gee', the 'magnetologist'. On 8 March 1950 Newton, billed only as 'Mr. X' or 'Scientist X', lectured a room of science students at the University of Denver. He told them he had seen two recovered flying saucers held in a secret government facility; one had come down near Aztec, New Mexico, brought down by a 'magnetic fault line', the other near Durango, Colorado. The occupants of the first had been burned dark brown when a porthole blew out; the crew of the second had suffocated from a change in atmosphere. He described the beings as between 38 and 42 inches tall, dressed in cloth that could not be torn, probably from Venus, flying on magnetic propulsion at twice the speed of light.

Frank Scully, a Variety columnist, had already begun retailing the story in his 1949 columns. In 1950 Henry Holt published 'Behind the Flying Saucers', which turned Newton's lecture into a bestseller and into the template. Scully's version put the event in March 1948, a 99-foot craft - the largest UFO reported to that date - containing sixteen humanoid bodies, recovered by the military after a controlled landing in Hart Canyon roughly twelve miles northeast of Aztec, and hauled away for secret research. Scully added that the craft ran on 'magnetic principles', that the crew carried concentrated food wafers and heavy water, and that every dimension of the vehicle was divisible by nine. Martin Gardner, in 'Fads and Fallacies in the Name of Science', took the book apart as 'wild imaginings' and 'scientific howlers'.

The monetisation is the part that matters. Through the late 1940s and early 1950s Newton and GeBauer were travelling the Four Corners selling 'doodlebugs' - devices that in the oil business claim to locate deposits by remote sensing - and their sales pitch was that the doodlebug was built from, or reverse-engineered out of, the alien technology recovered at Aztec. Herman A. Flader, a Denver industrialist, put in $50,000 for a partnership in GeBauer's Colorado Geophysics & Development company, on the represented understanding that each unit cost around $800,000 to build. Dr. Alfred D. Kleyhauer, a Denver optometrist, lost $15,000. When investigators opened the devices they found surplus military radio parts; the estimated build cost was about $3.50 each. When J. P. Cahn of the San Francisco Chronicle demanded a sample of the miracle metal, the sample tested as ordinary aluminium. Cahn published the exposure in True magazine in September 1952 and returned in August 1956 with a second piece cataloguing further victims. Flader pressed charges. Newton and GeBauer were convicted of fraud in 1953 and also faced federal charges for moving the proceeds across state lines. Neither did jail time; the Denver Public Library's account records Newton still on probation and in violation of its terms by June 1955, and on 30 April 1969 he pleaded guilty again, this time to salting the Buckhorn Mine at Silver City, New Mexico. He died in 1972, probably still on probation.

# 1970-1999: Post-Blue Book

## Rendlesham Forest Incident, RAF Woodbridge/Bentwaters

Rendlesham Forest Incident, RAF Woodbridge/Bentwaters is a event on the board; board tier B, documented channel and capability, programme role indicated but not yet nailed to a primary source; dates 1980 to 1981; axes C2 A1 D3; 9 connections; in the retrieval band.

In one line: 26-28 December 1980; USAF-occupied NATO base, Suffolk, England.

### What it is

A series of encounters in and around Rendlesham Forest, Suffolk, England, in late December 1980, between the twin USAF bases RAF Bentwaters and RAF Woodbridge, in which USAF security police and, on a later night, the deputy base commander reported an unexplained illuminated object described as metallic and triangular, together with ground depressions and elevated radiation readings. The primary document is a one-page unclassified memorandum from Lt Col Charles I. Halt to the UK Ministry of Defence dated 13 January 1981 and headed "Unexplained Lights."

### Why it is implicated

Rendlesham is the cleanest specimen on the board of an official, contemporaneous, signed U.S. military document describing a landed object of unknown origin at a nuclear-capable forward base -- and of the institutional machinery that made it go away. The memo was never classified, which is itself the tell: a document that is not secret cannot be leaked, and a document that is not investigated cannot generate a record. The USAF then threw away its own copy and had to obtain another from the British MoD when a FOIA requester asked for it in 1983. On the British side, a defence minister's briefing paper concluded in writing that "no additional action was required," reasoning from Halt's two-week reporting delay and "relatively low-key manner" rather than from the physical evidence the memo describes. Rendlesham is therefore the paper trail of a non-investigation: everything that would have been generated by taking it seriously is absent, and the absence is documented.

### The longer account

The Halt memo is short enough to quote nearly in full, and its brevity is part of the case.

Paragraph 1: "Early in the morning of 27 Dec 80 (approximately 0300L) two USAF security police patrolmen saw unusual lights outside the back gate at RAF Woodbridge. Thinking an aircraft might have crashed or been forced down, they called for permission to go outside the gate to investigate. The on-duty flight chief responded and allowed three patrolmen to proceed on foot. The individuals reported seeing a strange glowing object in the forest. The object was described as being metallic in appearance and triangular in shape, approximately two to three meters across the base and approximately two meters high. It illuminated the entire forest with a white light. The object itself had a pulsing red light on top and a bank(s) of blue lights underneath. The object was hovering or on legs. As the patrolmen approached the object, it maneuvered through the trees and disappeared. At this time the animals on a nearby farm went into a frenzy. The object was briefly sighted approximately an hour later near the back gate."

Paragraph 2: "The next day, three depressions 1.5 inches deep and 7 inches in diameter were found where the object had been sighted on the ground. The following night (29 Dec 80) the area was checked for radiation. Beta/gamma readings of 0.1 milliroentgens were recorded with peak readings in the three depressions and near the center of the triangle formed by the depressions. A nearby tree had moderate (0.05-0.07) readings on the side of the tree toward the depressions."

Paragraph 3 describes the third night: a "red sun-like light" that "appeared to throw off glowing particles and then broke into five separate white objects," then three star-like objects at about ten degrees elevation, two north and one south, moving "in sharp, angular movements" and displaying red, green and blue lights, the southern object "visible for two or three hours" and beaming "down a stream of light from time to time." Halt closes: "Numerous individuals, including the undersigned, witnessed the activities in paragraphs 2 and 3." Signed, Charles I. Halt, Lt Col, USAF, Deputy Base Commander.

This is a deputy base commander of a major forward USAF installation putting his name to a report of a landed object, physical ground traces, radiological readings taken with an instrument, and multiple military witnesses including himself. The memo was written on official USAF letterhead and was not classified in any way. It went to the British MoD. It was released under the U.S. Freedom of Information Act in June 1983 to Robert Todd of Citizens Against UFO Secrecy -- and the covering letter to Todd explains that the USAF had discarded its own copy and had to obtain another from the British MoD to satisfy the request. A U.S. Air Force base commander's report of a landed unknown craft was, by the institution's own account, not worth keeping.

The British disposition is documented with equal precision. In October 1985 the defence minister Lord David Trefgarne held a private, off-the-record meeting with Lord Peter Hill-Norton, the former Chief of the Defence Staff, who had been pressing the MoD on the case. The briefing paper prepared for Trefgarne survives as pages 115-116 of file DEFE 24/1925 at The National Archives, released in May 2008. Trefgarne's recorded view: "Our own view also was that no additional action was required. We believe that the fact that Col. Halt did not report these occurrences to MoD for almost two weeks after the event, together with the relatively low-key manner in which he handled the matter, are indicative of the degree of importance in defence terms which should be attached to the incident." The reasoning is worth isolating: the MoD assessed the significance of a reported landing not from the reported facts but from the reporter's delay and demeanour. Trefgarne wrote "Oh, dear" at the top of Hill-Norton's letter of 26 July 1985, which survives at pages 208-210 of the same file and in duplicate at pages 259-261 and 280-282 of DEFE 31/174.

Simon Weeden, the MoD desk officer who received the Halt memo, later described the disposition to journalist David Clarke: "once we had been through all the basic checks and found there was nothing seen on radar, no obvious explanation, no obvious threat to air defence, we decided no further action was needed." No witness was interviewed. No further official report was made or action taken by the U.S. or U.K. authorities beyond the memo.

In June 2010 Halt wrote and signed a two-page notarised affidavit committing his then-current memory to paper. It goes materially beyond the memo. It says one of the objects moved overhead and sent a laser-like beam down to their feet; that the same object then moved back toward Bentwaters and sent beams of light down into the Weapons Storage Area; and that several airmen present reported seeing the beams. Neither the beam-to-their-feet event nor the Weapons Storage Area beams appear in the 1981 memo or on Halt's contemporaneous field tape.

The denials attached to the affidavit are themselves catalogued artifacts. Airman Tim Egercic, on duty at the Bentwaters Weapons Storage Area, has specifically denied that any beams came down into the WSA. Halt's superior officer Col Ted Conrad, who says he was watching from outside his quarters at Woodbridge and saw nothing resembling Halt's descriptions, said of him: "He should be ashamed and embarrassed by his allegation that his country and England both conspired to deceive their citizens over this issue. He knows better." Conrad's statement is a dated, attributable denial by a named commanding officer -- which makes it evidence about the chain of command's posture, whatever it establishes about the lights.

### What it has denied

The British position, set out in the Trefgarne briefing paper of October 1985 (DEFE 24/1925 pp. 115-116) and defended for decades as the MoD's 'no defence significance' line, is that no additional action was required and that the incident's importance should be inferred from Halt's reporting delay and low-key handling. The U.S. position is expressed by omission: the Air Force generated no further report, took no further action, and did not retain its own copy of the memo. Col Ted Conrad's on-the-record repudiation of Halt supplies a named commanding officer's denial dated decades after the event.

Roles the board assigns it: retrieval, concealment, custody, research.

The method is the weakness and also the reason the corpus is worth auditing. Stringfield presented cases as numbered abstracts summarizing accounts from unnamed informants, most described as military or medical personnel. Some abstracts are explicitly composites; the widely cited 'Abstract XXII: Biological data relating to retrieved alien bodies' is described as a composite assembled from anonymous medical and military sources. No abstract in the published series can be independently verified from the text alone, because the text withholds the identifying information that would permit verification. That is a disqualifying defect for any individual claim and simultaneously the exact behavior one would expect from a researcher protecting serving personnel.

Status Report IV is the outlier and the best single entry point for an analyst. It abandons the abstract format for a single named case study, the alleged January 1978 incident at Fort Dix and McGuire Air Force Base in New Jersey, with a specific installation, a specific date window, and identifiable military police and security police units. That case is checkable against Army and Air Force records in a way the composite abstracts are not.

The physical archive survives. MUFON's Project Aquarius Library lists the Stringfield Files as a private collection. The underlying correspondence, informant notes and case files in that collection are the actual evidentiary object; the published monographs are only the redacted output. Whether the collection contains unredacted informant identities is the single most consequential unanswered question about this entity.

What the record shows: a well-documented researcher with an unusual official reporting relationship who, over sixteen years, published seven monographs compiling anonymous military testimony about recoveries and autopsies, and who left an archive. What the record does not show: any named source, any document, any physical item, or any independent corroboration of a single abstract. Tiered honestly, the existence, authorship, dating and archival survival of the Status Reports are Tier 1 or 2; every substantive claim inside them is Tier 3 pending identification of sources.

### The longer account

The Task Force on the Declassification of Federal Secrets is a creature of the 119th Congress, chaired by Rep. Jeffrey Nuccetelli, a former Air Force military police officer with sixteen years of active duty, testified to five UAP incidents at Vandenberg Air Force Base between 2003 and 2005 - the home, as his statement notes, of the National Missile Defense Project, during launches described by the National Reconnaissance Office as 'the most important in twenty-five years.' ## IAA FY2024 Sec. 1102, contractor turn-in of 'non-earth origin or exotic' UAP material

IAA FY2024 Sec. 1102, contractor turn-in of 'non-earth origin or exotic' UAP material is a legislation on the board; board tier A, named in sworn testimony or primary documents as directly inside the programme; dates 2023; axes C3 A3 D3; 9 connections; in the retrieval band.

In one line: Division M (Intelligence Authorization Act for FY2024) of S. 2226 as passed by the Senate, 27 Jul 2023; the same text is Sec. 1104 of S. 2103 as reported by SSCI, 22 Jun 2023. Turn-in mandate STRIPPED in conference; enacted as Sec. 7343, Div. G, P.L. 118-31, a funding limitation only.

### What it is

The contractor turn-in provision of the Fiscal Year 2024 Intelligence Authorization Act: a Gillibrand amendment adopted unanimously by the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence on 14 June 2023, which required any person currently or formerly under federal contract holding government-derived UAP material to notify AARO within 60 days and, within 180 days, to hand over "a comprehensive list of all non-earth origin or exotic unidentified anomalous phenomena material" for assessment, analysis and inspection. It carried a safe harbor, a sweeping funding ban on unreported UAP special access programs, and a Sense of Congress. It was numbered Sec. 1104 in S. 2103 as reported on 22 June 2023, renumbered as the bill moved, and was enacted in drastically narrowed form as Section 7343 of Division G of P.L. 118-31 on 22 December 2023.

### Why it is implicated

This is the single most important statutory hook on the board because it is the only text Congress has ever voted out of committee that presumes, on its face, that private contractors are holding non-earth-origin material derived from federal programs and that treats getting it back as an administrative problem with a deadline. A safe harbor is not written for a hypothetical. Congress does not draft immunity from criminal and civil action "for receiving" material unless someone plausibly received it. The definition of covered activities is equally load-bearing: it contemplates government or contractor personnel with "a primary, secondary, or contingency mission of capturing, recovering, and securing unidentified anomalous phenomena craft or pieces and components of such craft," and it defines the reverse-engineering perimeter as work on "any aerospace craft that uses propulsion technology other than chemical propellants, solar power, and electric ion thrust." Seventeen senators on the Intelligence Committee voted this text out. Then it was cut in conference.

### The longer account

On 14 June 2023, nine days after The Debrief published David Grusch's allegations, the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence adopted by voice vote a UAP amendment sponsored by Senator Kirsten Gillibrand (D-NY) and cosponsored by Senators Mike Rounds (R-SD), John Cornyn (R-TX) and Marco Rubio (R-FL). The committee then approved the underlying Intelligence Authorization Act unanimously. The bill was filed and numbered on 22 June 2023 as S. 2103; the UAP text appeared as Section 1104, titled "Funding limitations relating to unidentified anomalous phenomena." Section 1102 of that same reported text was an unrelated provision on defense sensitive support; the board's identifier tracks a later renumbering as the IAA moved into the NDAA vehicle, and the substance, not the number, is what matters.

The operative mechanism had three parts.

First, a notification and turn-in mandate. The text reached "any person currently or formerly under contract with the Federal Government that has in their possession material or information provided by or derived from the Federal Government relating to unidentified anomalous phenomena that formerly or currently is protected by any form of special access or restricted access." Such persons had 60 days from enactment to notify the AARO director, and 180 days to provide "a comprehensive list of all non-earth origin or exotic unidentified anomalous phenomena material" in their possession and to make it available to the director for "assessment, analysis, and inspection." The AARO director then had 30 days to notify designated congressional committees and leaders of any notification, information or material received. That last clause is the accountability trigger: it would have created a dated congressional paper trail for each turn-in.

Second, a safe harbor: "No criminal or civil action may lie or be maintained in any Federal or State court against any person for receiving" the covered material or information, contingent on meeting the deadlines. Douglas Johnson, who first reported the text, read this as implying that a private entity holding non-human technology outside democratic oversight, and possibly profiting from it, would otherwise be in a legally tenuous position, and that the provision functioned as a time-limited amnesty to smooth an orderly return. Nothing in the provision created new criminal offenses, and nothing immunized threats, violence or perjury.

Third, a funding ban. No future federal funds, directly or indirectly, for any UAP-related special access program activity unless the program had been "formally, officially, explicitly, and specifically described, explained, and justified to the appropriate committees of Congress, congressional leadership, and the Director" of AARO. The designated recipients were the intelligence, armed services and appropriations committees of both chambers plus the Speaker, House minority leader, and Senate majority and minority leaders, 58 senators and 137 House members in total. The categories of covered activity were drafted in six expansive paragraphs and included recruiting, employing, training, equipping, operating and providing security for government or contractor personnel with a retrieval mission; managing and providing security for activities and information relating to UAP "from disclosure"; and reverse-engineering work reaching any aerospace craft using propulsion other than chemical propellants, solar power and electric ion thrust.

The Sense of Congress attached to the provision is the most candid language Congress has produced on the subject. It asserted that "due to the increasing potential for technology surprise from foreign adversaries and to ensure sufficient integration across the United States industrial base and avoid technology and security stovepipes... the Federal Government must expand awareness about any historical exotic technology antecedents previously provided by the Federal Government for research and development purposes." Read plainly: the government previously provided exotic technology antecedents to industry for R&D, secrecy has stovepiped that work, and the stovepiping is now a national security liability. Whether or not one accepts the premise, the drafters wrote it into a bill that a Senate committee approved unanimously.

The legislative arc from there is the concealment story in miniature. The Senate Armed Services Committee carried parallel but non-identical language at Section 1546 of S. 2226, without the safe harbor and without the Sense of Congress, and with the funding limitation confined to FY2024 DoD programs. On 20 July 2023 SSCI Chairman Mark Warner filed a revised version, SA 994, narrowing the IAA's funding limitation to FY2024 as well. The House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence approved its own FY2024 IAA (H.R. 3932) on 13 July 2023 containing no UAP provisions at all, and the House Rules Committee blocked a floor vote on Rep. Mike Gallagher's parallel amendment to the House NDAA, reportedly because of objections from HPSCI leadership, whose chairman Michael Turner and ranking member Jim Himes had made dismissive public comments about secret alien technology on 6 June 2023. The Senate passed a combined NDAA-IAA on 27 July 2023. In conference, the turn-in mandate and safe harbor did not survive. What was enacted on 22 December 2023 as Section 7343 of Division G of P.L. 118-31 is a funding limitation only, conditioned on the Director of National Intelligence providing program details to the appropriate committees and leadership, which the Intelligence chairmen explained "provides for funding limitations relating to unidentified anomalous phenomena because of perceptions of insufficient transparency in this area."

The deletion is the finding. A unanimous committee wrote a hand-back-the-material provision with an amnesty clause, and the amnesty and the hand-back both vanished behind closed doors.

### What it has denied

The executive branch answer to this provision was delivered in advance and in writing by Pentagon spokesperson Sue Gough, who stated on 5 June 2023 that "AARO has not discovered any verifiable information to substantiate claims that any programs regarding the possession or reverse-engineering of extraterrestrial materials have existed in the past or exist currently," and on 22 June 2023 that "AARO has been rigorously investigating alleged programs mentioned by individuals who have come forward as part of the congressionally-mandated historical review. To date, AARO has not been denied access to any United States government program, past or present, during the course of its work." Johnson noted that the second statement is carefully scoped: Grusch did not come forward as part of the historical review, so the sentence may not have covered his allegations at all.

Roles the board assigns it: custody, retrieval, reverse-engineering, oversight, funding, disclosure.
