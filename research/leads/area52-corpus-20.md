## 2. STRUCTURAL CLAIMS

Numbered for reference. All are the speaker's claims as recorded, not board fact.

**S1 (file 1) — A claimed CIA search for negatives.** Some years after Rivera's 1988
photographs, four men entered his family home, the lead one presenting documents headed
"CIA" and demanding the photo negatives; searched every room methodically over an
afternoon and found nothing (Rivera had pre-emptively hidden them). Rivera's own text
flags the CIA identification as unverified. Family subsequently sent the negatives out of
Puerto Rico to a relative in Japan and used code language ("the baby") on the phone,
believing their line was tapped. (1 / 0:26:11–0:40:38)

**S2 (file 1) — A claimed December 1988 F-14 intercept and government denial.** Ramsay,
sourcing UFO researcher Jorge Martin: a giant triangular craft (~1 mile wide) over the
Sierra Bermeja mountains near Cabo Rojo, Puerto Rico, was approached by at least two F-14s
that circled it and then vanished mid-air; a third F-14 was allegedly chased off by three
red orbs the object deployed, also vanishing; over 100 witnesses, some of whom drew
pictures. Ramsay says an airfield "in the Bahamas" (his own uncertainty: "I believe") was
the deployment point, that the FAA confirmed multiple F-14s were deployed there "for
testing" at the relevant time, and that when asked, "the Americans" denied it. (1 /
0:07:02–0:10:38)

**S3 (file 2) — A NIDS-to-present government-interest lineage.** Woodruff's framing: the
ranch had informal government-contracted research via NIDS in the 1990s (Bigelow,
partially funded, with Sen. Harry Reid securing funding; Puthoff also involved), the
government "took over completely" through the 90s/early 2000s, then formally stepped back
("officially, the government isn't officially involved") while remaining privately
interested — flights, helicopter overflights including "Black Hawk helicopters," alleged
phone/email surveillance of ranch staff ("they told me when I started... it's possible
that you might have the government listening to your phone calls"), and a Utah congressman
unable to get direct answers when following up. (2 / 0:06:38–0:24:13)

**S4 (file 2) — A DoD NDA-break claim.** Woodruff: in the show's first season, Travis
Taylor broke his NDA to alert DoD to a possible hack "by either foreign entities or foreign
governments." Woodruff separately says "yeah, absolutely" when asked if there are national
security implications to ranch activity. (2 / 2:02:47–2:03:39)

**S5 (file 2) — Colossal Biosciences DNA result and timing.** A jawbone/tooth sample from
an alleged oversized canid found on the ranch, sent to Colossal Biosciences ~3–4 years
before the interview, returned as ~90% "a non-existent wolf species" and ~10%
non-native-to-Utah gray wolf lineages (Eurasian and Greenland); Colossal logged it as
"mystery" rather than naming it, then some years later announced a genetically engineered
"de-extinct" dire wolf. Woodruff and Ramsay both flag this as suspicious; Colossal's stated
position (per Woodruff) is that the dire-wolf project used other source material. (2 /
0:31:23–0:34:51)

**S6 (file 3) — A Bigelow/MUFON funding-for-access claim.** Vendittelli: Robert Bigelow
gave MUFON money on condition of receiving case information before anyone else — offered
as one data point in a broader "would intelligence agencies infiltrate a UFO-report
clearinghouse" discussion, which both speakers otherwise treat as speculation with "no
evidence." (3 / 2:41:12–2:42:09)

**S7 (file 4) — A self-described AFOSI/DoD investigator's authentication claim.** An
unnamed man identifying only as "Joe," described by Horn as a retired USAF Office of
Special Investigations/DoD case supervisor and investigator, spent roughly 5–6 months
skeptically interrogating Horn about the Meier case (Jan.–Aug., year not given in the
excerpt), showed credentials once (a tabletop of citations, on video call, then had Horn
delete the screenshot), and concluded "your Billy Meier case is 100% ironclad authentic,"
offering to rebut ufology skeptics on Horn's behalf before later withdrawing that offer
("these people don't understand investigation"). This is a single, unverified,
first-hand-to-second-hand claim with no document, name, or agency file offered — record
the vagueness. (4 / 0:38:52–0:43:22)
