### 3.3 The collision, stated in both directions

**Against the credulous reading.** Nothing about moscovium is stable, machinable, storable or
available in gram quantities. It is made one atom at a time, at cross sections of about one
picobarn, and it is gone within a second. Lazar's claim is not "element 115 exists", which was
never controversial; it is "a stable isotope of element 115 exists and I handled half a pound of
it". Nothing in the 2004 paper, the 2015 priority decision or the 2016 naming touches that
claim. *Skeptic*'s April 2026 treatment puts it correctly: IUPAC recognition "does not validate
Lazar's specific claims about a stable isotope or gravity propulsion".

**Against the hostile reading.** It is also not true that Lazar invented a number out of nothing
and got lucky. The island of stability near element 114 was mainstream, popularised physics well
before 1989. Mahood's own element-115 page cites **Glenn Seaborg in *Scientific American*, April
1969, pages 57 to 67**, with graphics of expected half-lives, and a 1970 textbook describing
element 114 as "an island of stability in a sea of instability". A person reading popular science
in the 1970s and 1980s had 114 and 115 available to them. The correct finding is neither
"prescient" nor "fabricated from nothing"; it is **"available prior art, used".**

## WHAT I COULD NOT ESTABLISH

This section is not empty and should not be made empty by a build session filling it in from
memory.

1. **The 1989 KLAS air dates.** The most-cited pair of dates in this entire subject, and I could
   not open a 1989-dated artefact for either. 8newsnow.com, which is KLAS's own site and carries
   Knapp's retrospectives, returned HTTP 403 on three separate URLs. The May 1989 "Dennis"
   broadcast and the 10 or 11 November 1989 named broadcast rest on Tom Mahood's timeline and on
   Wikipedia's summary of a 2023 NewsNation segment. **Tier 3. A later session should try a
   television listing, a newspaper TV page, or an archived KLAS page.**

   own 1995 synopsis. Friedman, who saw the form, describes only "under $1000.00" and the phrase
   "Department of Naval Intelligence". **The EIN, the zip code, the box contents and the
   "E-6722MAJ" string that circulate in the literature are NOT asserted anywhere in this file.**
   The one page promising a box-by-box reading returned HTTP 403.

   existence and its scope statement come only from Friedman's description of it. This matters,
   because the scope statement is the whole hinge of the Los Alamos question.

   no docket number came through the fetch. The conviction, the plea date, the sentence date and
   the 1996 denial are all sourced; the case number is not.
