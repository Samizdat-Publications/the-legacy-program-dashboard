**The six findings that matter, in order:**

1. **The roster hole is 2022, not 2023 to 2025.** Fourteen of the sixteen people who have given
   testimony at a US congressional UAP hearing since 2022 are already on the board. The two who
   are not, Under Secretary Ronald S. Moultrie and Deputy Director of Naval Intelligence
   Scott W. Bray, are the two most senior serving officials ever to testify in open session on
   this subject, and the hearing they testified at has no node.
2. **Herrera's relationship to Congress is documentary, not testimonial, and the record is
   precise about it.** He did not testify. A member moved a document about him into a record.
   The distinction is exactly the sort the board exists to keep, and a node that blurs it would
   be worse than no node.
3. **AARO's Volume I page 29 is the single most useful artefact in this pass for Herrera**, and
   it names nobody. It reads: "An interviewee who is a former U.S. service member said that in
   2009, while participating in a humanitarian and security mission in a foreign country, he
   encountered 'U.S. Special Forces' loading containers onto a large extraterrestrial
   spacecraft." The wording is AARO's summary of an interview, not a quotation of the
   interviewee, and that matters because the dispute over this sentence turns on its wording.
4. **Fravor's sworn testimony contains the case's provenance chain and the board does not carry
   it.** Under oath he named Jay Stratton as the person who contacted him in 2009 to
   investigate, placed Stratton inside "the ATIP program in the Pentagon led by Lue Elizondo",
   and said Alex Dietrich contacted him later. That is a documented chain of custody for how
   the 2004 case reached the public, in the words of the man it happened to, under oath.
5. **Dietrich is thin on the board because the board has her as a quote source rather than as a
   documented officer.** Her employer publishes a detailed career biography; two separate
   sworn congressional testimonies name her; CBS dates the start of her public record to the
   day. None of that is on her node.
6. **Jason Sands is the honest hard case of this pass.** He is real, identifiable, and has
   spoken at length in artefacts anyone can open. Nothing about him is documented. The node is
   proposed at the weakest footing the board allows, with the condition for dropping it stated
   in the open.
