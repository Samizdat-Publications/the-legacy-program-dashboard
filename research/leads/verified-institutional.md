## 1. Research and Development Board (RDB)

**VERDICT: PARTLY CONFIRMED.** The board existed and its statutory life is fully documented.
The transcript's 1941 date is contradicted by six years. The Sarbacher consultancy could not
be verified from any primary record.

The National Archives' own finding aid for Record Group 330 states, at section 330.11:
"Established as the Research and Development Board by the National Security Act of 1947
(61 Stat. 495), July 26, 1947. Abolished by Reorganization Plan No. 6 of 1953, effective
June 30, 1953, and superseded by Office of the Assistant Secretary of Defense (Research and
Development). Redesignated Office of the Director of Defense Research and Engineering by DOD
Reorganization Act of 1958 (72 Stat. 514), August 6, 1958." Section 330.11.3, "Records of the
Research and Development Board", lists surviving textual records including "Minutes of
meetings of the Executive Council, 1946-50" and "Records of the Committee on Technical
Information, 1946-53".

Truman's letter to Bush of 25 September 1947 gives the predecessor relationship in the
President's own words: "I am happy to know that you are willing to continue in the public
service as Chairman of the Research and Development Board created by the National Security
Act of 1947 ... Since V-J Day you have continued to render valuable service as chairman of
the Joint Research and Development Board set up by the Secretaries of War and Navy."

That settles the date question against the transcript in both directions. The RDB is 1947,
statutory. Its predecessor the JRDB is a post-V-J Day creation of the two service
secretaries, so no earlier than September 1945. **1941 is wrong.** The commonly cited
June 1946 date for the JRDB is consistent with Truman's wording but I did not pin it to a
primary source and do not assert it.

**Sarbacher: NOT VERIFIED.** Nothing found. An archive.org full-text search for
"Sarbacher" together with "Research and Development Board" returns exactly one item, an
anonymously uploaded MJ-12 compilation, which is not usable. A separate archive.org item
titled "Robert Sarbacher archive from Georgia Institute of Technology archives" was opened
and its OCR read in full: it is a set of newspaper clippings from Sarbacher's Georgia Tech
years and **contains no mention of the Research and Development Board**. It does record that
"from 1943 to 194[4] he was employed by [the] Navy Department as consultant research and
development" and that he was Dean of Graduate Studies at Georgia Tech from 1 July 1945. Note
also that the item's uploader is an anonymous individual, not the institution, so its
provenance is weak even where it is informative.

The RDB consultancy claim traces, in every secondary account found, to two sources that are
themselves recollection: Wilbert Smith's handwritten notes of a 15 September 1950 meeting
(held in Canada, at Library and Archives Canada and in the Arthur Bray Fonds at the
University of Ottawa) and Sarbacher's own 1983 letter to William Steinman. I opened neither.
**Do not put "RDB consultant" on the board at T1 or T2 on the strength of this pass.**

## 2. 1127th USAF Field Activities Group

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED**, at primary-document level, on every element of the lead.

Two Air Force FOIA releases were downloaded, rendered and read. Both are image scans with no
text layer, so the quotations below were read off the rendered pages.

The unit's own semiannual history has a title page reading: "HISTORY / 1127 USAF FIELD
ACTIVITIES GROUP (AFNIA) / 1 January - 30 June 1967 / Prepared by Anita H. Beasey, Policy
Branch, Programs Division, Headquarters, 1127 USAF Field Activities Group / Approved By:
RUDOLPH C. KOLLER, Jr., Colonel, USAF, Commander". It carries the document number
**AFNIABA 014-67**, was classified SECRET, and bears the struck-through markings "RODCA
MATERIAL / SPECIAL ACCESS REQUIRED / NO FOREIGN DISSEMINATION".

Its contents page lists, under **FOREIGN MATERIEL PROGRAM** (p.29): "Project BLUE FLY" (29),
"MIG-17 Exploitation" (29), "Project MOON DUST" (30). That is the Moon Dust / Blue Fly
relationship the transcript asserts, stated by the unit about itself.

Page 29, Project BLUE FLY: "As a result of the 7 April 1967 air battle between Israeli and
Syrian Air Forces, three MIG-21 aircraft downed by the Israelis crashed in Jordan. The USDAO,
through his official contacts, received permission for personnel of Detachment 3, FTD, to
visit the crash sites for the purpose of exploiting the aircraft. The Staff Operations Branch
(AFN...) coordinated requirements necessary to deploy the FTD exploitation personnel and to
airlift personnel and parts from site of acquisition to Wright-Patterson AFB. The take
consisted of approximately three tons of aircraft parts and armament." And: "In February, the
Joint Chiefs of Staff approved a one-year extension of the authority granted the USAF to apply
Force/Activity Designator I (F/AD-I) priority to short-notice BLUE FLY airlift contingencies.
This authority will terminate on 1 February 1968 unless sooner modified by JCS."

Page 30, Project MOON DUST: "The volume of MOON DUST actions remained fairly constant. The
1st Aerospace Control Squadron (SPADATS), Ent AFB, Colorado, notified AFNIAA of the deorbit
of **37** Soviet space objects consisting of rocket bodies, payloads, and space platforms."
A redaction follows, then: "SPADATS also advised AFNIAA of deorbit of [figure illegible in
the scan] US space objects, considered of special importance." The US figure was zoomed to
340 dpi and still cannot be read; it is not reported here.

**The transcript's 49 and 17 are in the companion document, and mean something different.**
The second release has a cover reading "HISTORY OF THE ASSISTANT CHIEF OF STAFF,
INTELLIGENCE / July -- December 1967", and at its page 62: "MOON DUST (U) / (Secret/No
Foreign Dissem) During the last half of 1967, the Operations Plans Branch (AFNIAAB) received
from the 1st Aerospace Control Squadron (SPADATS) at Ent, AFB, Colorado, notifications of the
deorbit of 49 Soviet space objects (rocket bodies, payloads, and space platforms), and of 17
US space objects considered to be of special importance."

**Two corrections to the transcript, both material:**

- The figures are **notifications of deorbit received**, not objects **recovered**. Deorbit
  notification is the tasking trigger; recovery is a separate act the document does not count.
  Anything that reaches the board must say "notified of the deorbit of", not "recovered".
- The document is the Assistant Chief of Staff, Intelligence's own semiannual history, which
  covers the 1127th as a subordinate. It is not "a review of the functions of the 1127th".

Page 47 of the same release also states: "Colonel Rudolph C. Koller, Jr., was Commander of
the 1127 USAF Fld Acty Gp during the entire period of this report", with an authorised
strength table rising from 220 to 249 over the half-year.

## 3. AFR 200-2 (1953)

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED** as to existence and text. **The transcript's central claim about it
is CONTRADICTED.**

A four-page scan of the actual 1953 regulation was downloaded and read. Its face reads:
"AIR FORCE REGULATION NO. 200-2 / DEPARTMENT OF THE AIR FORCE / WASHINGTON, 26 AUGUST 1953 /
INTELLIGENCE / (Unclassified) Unidentified Flying Objects Reporting (Short Title: UFOB)".
It is marked RESTRICTED / SECURITY INFORMATION, struck through on the release copy, and
stamped "REC'D. SEPTEMBER 1953". The printer's line reads "U. S. GOVERNMENT PRINTING OFFICE:
1953". It is signed "By Order of the Secretary of the Air Force: N. F. TWINING, Chief of
Staff, United States Air Force", official K. E. THIEBAUD, Colonel, USAF, Air Adjutant
General, distribution D.

**Its own footnote settles the supersession question the corpus map flagged as needing
verification: "*This Regulation supersedes AFL 200-5, 29 April 1952."** The corpus editor's
guess that the transcript's garbled "AFL 205" meant AFL 200-5 is correct, and the date is now
known. The release copy also carries two handwritten annotations, "Sup by AFR 200-2, 12 Aug
54" and "amended by 200-2A", against paragraph 6.

Paragraph 4a: "The Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC), Wright-Patterson Air Force Base,
Ohio, has been designated the Air Force activity responsible for conducting analysis of
information and evidence."

Paragraph 6c, addressees: electrical reports go in multiple address to the Commander, Air
Defense Command, Ent AFB; the Commander, ATIC, Wright-Patterson; the Director of Intelligence,
HQ USAF; and the appropriate major air command headquarters. Written reports go to the
Director of Intelligence, HQ USAF, "for ozalid-type reproduction and dissemination, with an
information copy forwarded directly to ATIC."

Paragraph 7, Evidence: "All evidence will be forwarded to ATIC." On materiel: "(1) Safeguard
the materiel carefully to prevent any defacing or alteration which would reduce its value for
technical analysis. (2) Notify ATIC immediately and request shipping instructions or other
special instructions as may be appropriate."

Paragraph 8, Release of Information: "Inquiries concerning specific local sightings will be
referred to the activity public information officer. The reporting officer may furnish the
latter all raw information except names of principals, Air Force comments, details of
intercept procedures involved, and classified radar data." The releasable list ends: "f. The
fact that the object has been conclusively identified by ATIC as a conventional aerial device
or other familiar object and no further evaluation is contemplated."

**CONTRADICTED: "routed reports to ATIC rather than Blue Book" is a category error.** Project
Blue Book was a project inside ATIC, at Wright-Patterson. The 1953 regulation does not mention
Blue Book at all, in either edition, because naming ATIC is naming Blue Book's own host. The
board's existing `atic` node already states this correctly. Anything built from the transcript
must not reproduce the dichotomy.

What the regulation **does** do, and what is worth carrying, is paragraph 8: identified cases
could be discussed publicly and unidentified ones could not. That is a real release
restriction and it is on the face of the 1953 edition, seven months after the Robertson Panel
reported, not only in the 1954 edition the board currently cites.

The 1954 edition's own footnote, read in transcription, closes the chain: "*This Regulation
supersedes AFR 200-2, 26 August 1953, Including Change 200-2A, 2 November 1953." Its short
title changed from UFOB to FLYOBRPT, and it added the 4602d AISS to the investigation chain,
which the 1953 edition does not name.

## 4. JANAP 146

**VERDICT: PARTLY CONFIRMED.** The revisions, dates and reporting requirement are confirmed.
**The penalty claim is right about the law and wrong about the document.**

Three revisions are now pinned:

| Revision | Title | Date established |
|---|---|---|
| JANAP 146(B) | "Communication Instructions for Reporting Vital Intelligence Sightings from Aircraft (CIRVIS)" | named as superseded in (C)'s letter of promulgation; its own date not established |
| JANAP 146(C) | "Communication Instructions for Reporting Vital Intelligence Sightings from Airborne and Waterborne Sources" | 10 March 1954, Joint Chiefs of Staff, Joint Communications-Electronics Committee. "is an unclassified publication." |
| JANAP 146(E) | "Canadian - United States Communications Instructions for Reporting Vital Intelligence Sightings (CIRVIS/MERINT)" | complete with Change No. 2, promulgated 17 May 1977; released 28 February 1994 by the Assistant to the Secretary of Defense under FOIA ref 93-FOI-2428/L |

(C)'s letter of promulgation states it "supersedes JANAP 146 (B) ... and all other conflicting
instructions. JANAP 146(B) shall be destroyed by burning."

"Unidentified flying objects" is a listed reportable category in both, at Article 201 (CIRVIS,
while airborne) and Article 301 (MERINT, immediately) in (C), and at Article 201 in the later
Canadian-United States version. The 1977 change still carries it, eight years after Blue Book
closed.

**On penalties: JANAP states no dollar figure and no term of imprisonment, in any revision
read.** What it does is incorporate other law.

(C), Article 308a: "All persons aware of the contents or existence of a MERINT Report are
governed by the Communications Act of 1934 and amendments thereto, and Espionage Laws. MERINT
reports contain information affecting the National Defense of the United States within the
meaning of the Espionage Laws, 18 U.S. Code, 793 and 794. The unauthorized transmission or
revelation of the contents of MERINT reports in any manner is prohibited."

(E), Article 208: "Transmission of CIRVIS reports are subject to the U.S. Communications Act
of 1934, as amended, and the Canadian Radio Act of 1938, as amended. Any person who violates
the provisions of these acts may be liable to prosecution thereunder. These reports contain
information affecting the national defense of the United States and Canada. Any person who
makes an unauthorized transmission or disclosure of such a report may be liable to prosecution
under Title 18 of the US Code 793, Chapter 37, or the Canadian Official Secrets Act of 1939,
as amended. This should not be construed as requiring classification of CIRVIS messages. The
purpose is to emphasize the necessity for the handling of such information within official
channels only."

The transcript's "$10,000 fine" is therefore **correct as to the incorporated statute and
wrong as to the document**. 18 U.S.C. 793 derives from the Act of June 25, 1948, ch. 645,
62 Stat. 736. Its 1994 amendment note reads: "Pub. L. 103-322 substituted 'fined under this
title' for 'fined not more than $10,000'". So from 1948 until 1994, including the whole period
in question, the penalty carried by the statute JANAP points to was a fine of not more than
$10,000 or imprisonment of not more than ten years, or both. **A claim on the board must
attribute that figure to 18 U.S.C. 793, never to JANAP 146.**

## 6. Howard C. Cross memo of 9 January 1953 (the Pentacle Memorandum)

**VERDICT: PARTLY CONFIRMED.** Date, addressee, reference number and content confirmed from
the circulating text. **Authorship is an attribution, not a signature. No scan was found.**

The text as published reads: "SECRET / SECURITY INFORMATION / G-1579-4 / cc: B. D. Thomas /
H. C. Cross/A. D. Westerman / L. R. Jackson / W. T. Reid / P. J. Rieppal / V. W. Ellsey/R. J.
Lund / January 9, 1953 / Files / Mr. Miles E. Goll / Box 9575 / Wright-Patterson Air Force
Base, Ohio / Attention Capt. Edward J. Ruppelt".

Substance, quoted verbatim: "we feel that agreement between Project Stork and ATIC should be
reached as to what can and what cannot be discussed at the meeting in Washington on January
14-16 concerning our preliminary recommendation to ATIC." And on provenance of the panel: "The
CIA-sponsored meeting is being held subsequent to a meeting of CIA, ATIC, and our
representatives held at ATIC on December 12, 1952." The experimental proposal follows: observation
posts "with complete visual skywatch, with radar and photographic coverage, plus all other
instruments necessary or helpful in obtaining positive and reliable data on everything in the
air over the area."

**Three things this pass establishes that the board should be precise about.**

- **The letter is unsigned.** H. C. Cross appears in the **cc list**, paired with A. D.
  Westerman, not as a signatory. Vallee codenamed the author "Pentacle" precisely because he
  would not name him; Cross was identified later, and the sources found in this pass state
  that identification without explaining how it was made. The board's `pentacle-memorandum`
  node already tiers the authorship claim at T2, which is right.
- **The memo does not name Battelle either.** The employer attribution runs through Project
  Stork, which was Battelle's contract, established independently at T1 in lead 5. That is a
  two-document inference and is sound, but it is an inference.
