## 3. STRUCTURAL CLAIMS — funding, tasking, custody, oversight

Numbered so the board can cite them. All are the **guest's or host's claims**, several
explicitly their own inference.

**S1 — Remote viewing tasked across 19 US intelligence agencies, unevenly.** Dellis,
attributing the figure to Ed May: 19 agencies came back for taskings a combined ~500 times;
DIA alone accounted for 172 of those; CIA and FBI "once or twice" each. No other agency
breakdown given. 99% of the specific missions are described as still classified. (1 /
0:42:16–0:43:07)

**S2 — A capped, rolling "read-in" roster.** Ed Dames told the guest he was "read in," with
only 100 people ever read into the project at one time — a one-out-one-in cap. Caption
renders the term itself as **"the billot structure"** [PHON? — a likely corruption of the
standard classified-access term "bigot list"; verify before using this string as a search
term]. (1 / 0:27:43)

**S3 — Candidate vetting funnel.** Dr. Ed May: SRI reached out to roughly 600 candidates
(a mix said to include intelligence-agency personnel), narrowed to about 10 with "notable
psychic ability," then to 4–5 considered genuinely strong performers — all of whom, per May,
showed synesthesia. (1 / 0:35:23–0:37:10)

**S4 — Judging methodology.** SRI used a double-blind "Fuzzy Set / Clear Set" scoring
protocol: a monitor scored what percentage of a remote viewer's drawing matched the true
target, rather than a binary hit/miss. Presented by Dellis as evidence of scientific rigor
in the programme's own design. (1 / 0:48:41–0:50:17)

**S5 — DIA sent a remote viewer to a civilian research institute for extended study.**
McMoneagle is said to have spent 14 months, long weekends only, at the Monroe Institute
under DIA sponsorship, having his brain monitored during out-of-body-state testing. (1 /
0:29:25–0:30:16)

**S6 — A foreign military recovery operation with no public acknowledgment.** Venditelli's
account of Varginha, Brazil (Jan 1996): a plane, described as USAF, is said to have flown
into Brazil, a helicopter ferried whatever was recovered to the plane, and it departed with
no diplomatic notice reported at the time. He treats the absence of any contemporary press
coverage as itself the notable fact. **Note the transcription artifact:** captions in this
passage repeatedly render "Varginha" as "Virginia" — this is almost certainly faster-whisper
mishearing the Brazilian place name, not a claim about the US state. (2 / 0:50:44–0:52:26)

**S7 — A witness's death attributed to contact with recovered material.** The Varginha
police officer named only "Marco" is said to have died within a day of contact with the
recovered object, his official cause of death recorded as "general infection." Venditelli
treats his choice to bring the material into a hospital, rather than handle it himself, as
evidence he believed it was dangerous. (2 / 0:55:41–0:56:28)

**S8 — Public visibility credited to the 2023 Senate hearing.** Both Donderi and Ramsay
treat the mid-2023 US Senate hearing (and the witnesses who appeared at it) as the pivot
that took the subject from a niche interest to mainstream public conversation, more so than
any UAP Task Force or AARO activity before it. (3 / 0:04:43–0:05:38, 0:37:14–0:38:54)

**S9 — Modern-era oversight framed as denial.** Donderi's characterisation, unsourced beyond
his own reading: AARO's director "still says it's a lot of [expletive]" and the office was
"hired... to denounce all this." No document or specific AARO statement is quoted. (3 /
0:38:05–0:38:54)
