## 4. STRUCTURAL CLAIMS — compartmentalisation, funding, custody, oversight

Numbered so the board can cite them. All are **Gerb's claims**, several of them his own
stated inference rather than anything he sources.

**S1 — Nested compartments with non-correlated connections.** From the Wilson memo as Gerb
reads it: seven program managers called, four of whom referred Wilson to the same program;
those four were "part of it in different compartments placed in different layers of the
compartments pyramid, split up to do different things or parts of it". They sit in the same
records group but with **non-correlated connections**. Gerb says he cannot tell whether the
adjacent compartments are reverse engineering or material exploitation, and says so. (01 /
11;23, 11;43)

**S2 — A special subset of unacknowledged carve-out/waived programs, organised in 1994.**
Wilson was directed to "records group files" at OUSD(A&T) and found a "special project
records group not belonging to usual SAP divisions", described as a subset of unacknowledged
carve-out waived programs organised in 1994 by Perry himself. Gerb treats the 1994 date as
the reorganisation event. (01 / 09;30, 10;20)

**S3 — Funding hidden by audit avoidance.** "No budget info was listed for the program,
purposefully kept secret for audit avoidance." Gerb links this forward to Grusch's 2023
account of programs nested within each other and funded through **IRAD** [caption `IRAs` —
Independent Research and Development] rather than appropriated lines. (01 / 12;12, 12;40)

**S4 — Corporate, not government, custody.** The bigot list Wilson was shown (dated
1992–93) contained **only civilians** as far as he could recognise — scientists,
technicians, engineers, managers. No politicians, no White House, no Clinton or Bush-senior
administration names. Gerb's reading: the program sits with a contractor, and the government
names on the list were Pentagon, State, NSC staff rather than political leadership. (01 /
15;23, 15;51)

**S5 — The contractor can refuse statutory oversight, and the oversight body will back the
contractor.** Wilson invoked his regulatory authority as DDIA/J2 and was refused; he
complained to the SAPOC Senior Review Group at the Pentagon, and the SRG **sustained the
contractor's denial** and told him to drop it, with a career threat attached. Gerb treats
this as the structural core: the denial was ratified, not merely committed. (01 / 15;02,
18;24)

**S6 — Access criteria set by the program, not by the government.** After a past audit
nearly outed the program, a formal agreement was struck with SAPOC and "special criteria"
established; **no USG personnel may access the program unless they meet criteria the
committee refused to disclose**. (01 / 14;15, 15;02)

**S7 — The program is categorically outside the normal taxonomy.** The Watch Committee told

flags this as the reason it does not appear where an oversight officer would look. (01 /

**S8 — Isolation as a deliberate cost.** The program's progress was "agonisingly slow" with

**S9 — Nuclear classification as the disclosure firewall.** The recurring mechanism: the AEC
