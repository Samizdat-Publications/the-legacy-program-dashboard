**The five findings that matter, in order:**

1. **The board's `sapoc` node dates the committee sixteen years too late.** The node says the
   SAPOC "was established by DoD Directive 5205.07 on 1 July 2010". A Deputy Secretary of
   Defense SAPOC Information Bulletin of November 1994 says the SAP management and control
   structures were changed on **5 January 1994**, that the plan formalised SAP approval and
   revalidation "through the Special Access Program Oversight Committee (SAPOC)", that "A
   Senior Review Group (SRG) was established to support the SAPOC principals", and that the
   SAPOC was then "chaired by the Honorable John Deutch, DEPSECDEF". The Joint Security
   Commission's report of 28 February 1994 independently calls it "The recent formation of
   the DoD Special Access Program Oversight Committee". The 2010 directive's "hereby
   established" re-established a body that had existed for sixteen years, and the board
   currently reads the re-establishment as the creation.
2. **The Cosford file was explained, and the board's route in assumes it was not.** The
   triage row says the released file "is said to carry an internal assessment of an
   unexplained intrusion into UK airspace". The National Archives' own highlights guide for
   the August 2009 tranche says the Assistant Chief of the Air Staff was briefed that there
   was evidence a UFO had evaded UK defences, **and then** says: "It soon emerged that the
   majority of sightings were caused by the Russian rocket that launched the Cosmos 2238
   satellite, re-entering the earths atmosphere." Both halves belong on the node.
3. **The 902nd MI Group question in `area52-triage-2.md` row 14 is settled, and neither side
   was wholly wrong.** The CENTER LANE project office wrote on the letterhead "DEPARTMENT OF
   THE ARMY / SECURITY SUPPORT DETACHMENT / USAINSCOM, 902D MILITARY INTELLIGENCE GROUP /
   FORT GEORGE G. MEADE, MARYLAND 20755", office symbol IAGPA-F-SD. So the unit sat inside
   the 902d. The same memorandum dates INSCOM's involvement from "Project GRILL FLAME in
   1977", and a 1978 talking paper records the Army Assistant Chief of Staff for Intelligence
   agreeing that INSCOM would do the work. The board's `stargate-program` prose and the
   guest's account are describing two ends of one chain, not contradicting each other. What
   the released files do not support is the detachment name: the letterhead says **Security
   Support Detachment**, not Systems Exploitation Detachment.
4. **CENTER LANE was a Secretary of the Army designated special access programme, not an
   INSCOM one.** `center-lane`'s own text says INSCOM "ran the unit as a special access
   programme with its own security classification guide". The 2 March 1985 letter from the
   Commanding General, INSCOM, asks HQDA to "officially discontinue CENTER LANE as a
   Secretary of the Army designated Special Access Program". The designation authority is a
   level above INSCOM, and that is the more useful fact for this board's argument.
5. **The Belgian officer's name and office are both wrong in the triage row.** The row says
   "Maj. Gen. Wilfred De Brouwer", "Deputy Chief of the Air Staff". The National Archives'
   guide says "General Wilfried de Brouwer, Chief of Operations, Belgian Air Staff". No
   record opened here gives him a rank of Major General or the post of Deputy Chief of the
   Air Staff. That is why no person node is drafted.

## 1. Belgian Air Force F-16 intercept of 30-31 March 1990, and the 1989-90 wave

**VERDICT: PARTLY CONFIRMED.** Two government records were opened. Both are British. They
establish that the scramble happened, that the Belgian Air Force sent the MoD an official
statement about it, that a four-page account by the Belgian Air Staff's Chief of Operations
sits in a named MoD file, and that the UK Defence Intelligence Staff put Belgian imagery of
31 March 1990 into its own classified study. No Belgian record was opened, and neither of
the two MoD files that hold the Belgian material was opened.

### What was opened

| URL | What is at it |
|---|---|
| https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/aug-2009-highlights-guide.pdf | "Highlights Guide", The National Archives, for the tranche of MoD UFO files opened on 17 August 2009. Names the bookmarked stories in each released file and gives file and page references. Extracted with `pdftotext -layout`. |
| https://archive.org/download/condign-vol-2-1-258/Condign_Vol_2_1-258_djvu.txt | OCR text of Project Condign Volume 2, the working papers and appendices, in the same archive.org item the board already cites for `doc-condign-vol3`. |
| https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/API/records/v1/details/C10340416 | The National Archives Discovery catalogue record for DEFE 24/1959/1, used below for the closure pattern. |

### What the documents establish

**The scramble, from The National Archives.** Under the heading "UFOs reported over Belgium
- 1989-1990" the guide says the files "reveal how in March 1990 the Belgian Air Force
scrambled F-16 fighters to intercept UFOs reported by police officers and others."

**The Belgian Air Force put it in writing to the MoD, and where.** "An official statement
from the Belgian Air Force sent to the MoD in November 1993 is at DEFE 24/1970 p86-87."

**The officer's account, his name and his office.** "DEFE 24/1960 (p320-324) - a four-page
account of the UFOs detected by NATO radars and F-16 fighters over Belgium in 1990, which
remain unexplained. The account, from General Wilfried de Brouwer, Chief of Operations,
Belgian Air Staff confirms that F-16 pilots obtained 'lock-ons' with their radars but were
unable to explain the phenomena."

**The British reaction, which is the part this board should want.** "A letter from Malcolm
Rifkind to Lord Hill-Norton explains the MoD was not informed of the incident at the time and
concluded there was no threat to the UK DEFE 24/1970 p58-59." A NATO ally scrambled fighters
and the UK Secretary of State for Defence later told a former Chief of the Defence Staff that
the MoD had not been told at the time.

**Further page references given by the guide**, none of them opened: DEFE 24/1970 p66,
p70-71, p75-76 and p86-87; DEFE 24/1962 p177-179; DEFE 24/1965 p272.

**The Defence Intelligence Staff used Belgian imagery.** Condign Volume 2's figure list
carries "FIGURE 34: VIDEO FRAME - BELGIUM 31 MARCH 1990", "FIGURE 35: SHAPE UK, RUSSIA,
BELGIUM", and a figure captioned "BELGIUM LOCATION EUPEN (2 SECOND AT ZOOM SETTING 100-150MM
AT F4) 30-31 MARCH" whose year the OCR renders as 1970 and which I am therefore not quoting
as a date. Volume 2 also places Belgium among the "20 prime general locations" of
concentrated events, noting these "have airspace adjacent to the UKADR" and that "reports are
sometimes received of UAPs entering from, or leaving towards these nations."

### What the transcript and the triage row claim that these documents do NOT establish

- **No rank, and a different office.** Map 14's own text and the triage row give "Gen.
  Wilfred De Brouwer" / "Maj. Gen. Wilfred De Brouwer", "Deputy Chief of the Air Staff".
  The only record opened that names him spells it **Wilfried de Brouwer** and gives his post
  as **Chief of Operations, Belgian Air Staff**. Nothing opened supports a two-star rank or a
  deputy chief of staff appointment.
- **No public statement rejecting mass hysteria.** The triage row and map 14's S7 both rest
  on De Brouwer's public rejection of the mass-hysteria explanation. Nothing in either opened
  record says that. What the guide records is narrower and better: an account, inside a
  government file, saying the F-16 radar lock-ons could not be explained.
- **No radar tape analysis is described.** The guide says NATO radars detected objects and
  F-16 pilots obtained lock-ons. It does not describe an analysis of the tapes.
- **The Belgian side of the record is untouched.** SOBEPS and the Belgian Ministry of Defence
  archives were not reachable and were not tried beyond noting that the row names a printed
  book in a research library. `doc-condign-vol3` chapter 4, the route the triage row proposed,
  does **not** cover Belgium: chapter 4's country sections are the Former Soviet Union, China,
  Spain, and the United States and Canada. The Belgian material is in Volume 2, and it is
  imagery, not a survey.

## 2. Cosford Incident, 30-31 March 1993

**VERDICT: PARTLY CONFIRMED on the file and the date, CONTRADICTED on the conclusion.** The
National Archives' catalogue and its own release guide both exist and were opened. The file
itself was not: the paper piece is closed until 2078 and the open redacted digital copy could
not be downloaded from this environment.

### What was opened

| URL | What is at it |
|---|---|
| https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/aug-2009-highlights-guide.pdf | As above. Carries a section headed "Cosford incident, 31 March 1993". |
| https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/API/search/records (query "DEFE 24 2086") | The National Archives Discovery catalogue records for DEFE 24/2086 and DEFE 24/2086/1. |
| https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/API/records/v1/details/C10340416 | Discovery detail record for DEFE 24/1959/1, opened as the pattern check on the release. |

### What the documents establish

**The file, and its own title.** Discovery gives **DEFE 24/2086**, "UFO reports of sightings,
30-31 March 1993", covering dates 1993 Mar 01 to 1993 Jun 30, held at The National Archives,
Kew. The catalogue title supplies the event's date without recourse to any broadcast.

**The release is a redacted surrogate, and the original is shut for another fifty-two years.**
The paper piece DEFE 24/2086 carries closure status D with a record opening date of
**01/01/2078**. The item actually open is **DEFE 24/2086/1**, "Digital copy of DEFE 24/2086:
UFO reports sightings, 30/31 March 1993; with redactions", closure status O, opening date
**17/08/2009**. The same pattern holds for DEFE 24/1959 and DEFE 24/1959/1. This is a fact
about the release itself that `uk-defe24` does not carry.

**What the guide says the file contains.** "The file DEFE 24/2086 covers sightings of bright
lights seen across central England in the early hours by police officers and military
witnesses (including a police patrol at RAF Cosford, near Wolverhampton). The file includes
more than 30 sightings during a six-hour period. The MoDs UFO desk asked the RAF to replay
radar tapes but this found nothing unusual had been detected. However, the Assistant Chief of
the Air Staff (ACAS), Sir Anthony Bagnall, was briefed that there was evidence a UFO had
evaded UK defences. It soon emerged that the majority of sightings were caused by the Russian
rocket that launched the Cosmos 2238 satellite, re-entering the earths atmosphere."

### What the triage row claims that this does NOT establish

- **The row's characterisation is half the sentence.** Row 2 says Cosford "is the named case
  where the released file is said to carry an internal assessment of an unexplained intrusion
  into UK airspace". The briefing of the Assistant Chief of the Air Staff is real and is in
  the guide. So is the resolution, in the very next sentence, to the Cosmos 2238 rocket
  re-entry. A node built on the first half alone would be the board asserting an anomaly the
  releasing archive says was explained.
- **The radar tapes found nothing.** The guide says the replay "found nothing unusual had been
  detected". Any node claiming radar corroboration at Cosford would be contradicted.
- **No desk officer's loose minute was opened.** The row names one; the guide does not
  mention one; the file was not opened.
- **`nick-pope`'s connection is not in the record.** Map 14 attaches Nick Pope to Cosford
  through archival interview footage. The guide names only the MoD's UFO desk as an office and
  Sir Anthony Bagnall as an individual. Anything tying the node to `nick-pope` stays at Tier 2
  in the "On <show>, <date>, <speaker> said" form.

### What the documents establish

**The command, its address and its structure.** Two documents carry the letterhead
"DEPARTMENT OF THE ARMY / UNITED STATES ARMY INTELLIGENCE AND SECURITY COMMAND / ARLINGTON
HALL STATION / ARLINGTON, VIRGINIA 22212". The 16 Nov 79 disposition form records that a
milestone plan "established milestones for implementation of HQ INSCOM reorganization" and
the creation of a brigade at Fort George G. Meade, that "HQ INSCOM will be consolidated at
AHS, VA", and that this involves collocating ADCSOPS-HUMINT and DCSCL with the rest of
DCSOPS.

**Psychoenergetics from 1977, and the Army staff's part in starting it.** The 4 April 1984
GDIP memorandum opens: "Since the beginning of USAINSCOM's exploitation of psychoenergetics
(PE) technology with Project GRILL FLAME in 1977 through the present effort under CENTER
LANE, the project has been conducted on an ad hoc basis, using resources taken 'out of
hide.'" The 1978 talking paper records that "ACSI & MG Rolya agreed that INSCOM would 'check
out' parapsychology phenomen[a], and that Army Intelligence would obligate $75 K to support
an SRI Subject selection and training program for INSCOM personnel over the next year", with
the money to train a project manager and three INSCOM subjects and the contract let via
AMSAA.

**The office that ran it was inside the 902d.** The 4 April 1984 memorandum's letterhead is
"DEPARTMENT OF THE ARMY / SECURITY SUPPORT DETACHMENT / USAINSCOM, 902D MILITARY INTELLIGENCE
GROUP / FORT GEORGE G. MEADE, MARYLAND 20755", office symbol IAGPA-F-SD, addressed through
the ADCSOPS-HUMINT and the Chief of Staff to MG Stubblebine as Commanding General, INSCOM,
and signed by a lieutenant colonel of Military Intelligence as CENTER LANE Project Manager.
The 13 Jul 84 information paper carries the same office symbol.

**The 902d as the programming vehicle.** The programme development requirement package short
titled "902d MI Gp S&IA OPSEC Sppt CENTER LANC Init" proposes to "Establish a new
intelligence program utilizing psychoenergetics (parapsychology) to enhance existing
intelligence collection and OPSEC programs, and an organizational infrastructure to manage
administrative, training and operational functions for the program", with resources across
FY86 to FY89. Its rationale paragraph states that "INSCOM's efforts in the intelligence
application of PE have been on an ad hoc basis since 1977" and that "From 1978 through 1983
intelligence collection [taskings were] completed for the U.S. Army, DIA, JCS, CIA, NSA, FBI,
and the Secret Service".

**The special access designation, and whose it was.** Every one of these documents carries
the warning notice "CENTER LANE SPECIAL ACCESS PROGRAM RESTRICT DISSEMINATION TO THOSE WITH
VERIFIED ACCESS TO CATEGORY THREE (3)", classified by the Commanding General, INSCOM. The 2
March 1985 letter then asks the Department of the Army to "officially discontinue CENTER LANE
as a Secretary of the Army designated Special Access Program". The designation authority sat
above INSCOM.

**The decision to end it, and the transfer.** The 17 July 1984 memorandum for the Assistant
Chief of Staff for Intelligence, signed by a Brigadier General as Commanding, records the
Commanding General's own opinion "that INSCOM, who has supported this project with S and IA
funds and personnel assigned without spaces, should discontinue our involvement with
psychoenergetic research and operations", states an intent "to cease INSCOM CENTER LANE
Project operations on 30 Sept 1984, but allow completion of outstanding contracts and
training by 31 December 1984", and asks for authority to permit "timely and undisrupted
transfer of (1) ICLP operations to a national agency such as DIA or NSA and (2) ICLP
phenomena validation and technology extension to the US Army Medical Research and Development
Command". It also records that "Intelligence users in the Army, DIA, NSA and CIA have all
tasked this methodology". The 2 March 1985 letter completes it: it notifies HQDA of "the
deactivation of the INSCOM CENTER LANE Project, the attachment OPCON of CENTER LANE assets
and resources to the Defense Intelligence Agency, and the retirement of CENTER LANE as an
active project nickname, effective as of the date of this letter", over the signature block
"E. SOYSTER / Maj General, USA / Commanding" as the OCR renders it.

**The classification guide exists and is withheld.** The reading-room item indexed as the
CENTER LANE security classification guide releases a human-use requirements annex and states
"Next 8 Page(s) In Document Exempt". The guide's own text has not been released.

procedures have not been followed for the project as a whole." A CI Staff branch chief advised

staff's recommendation for regular re-evaluation went unimplemented.
