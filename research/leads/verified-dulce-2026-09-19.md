1. **A named originator confessed, in writing, and the confession is quoted.** Levesque to

2. **Doty's record is two-sided and the board currently carries only one side.** Every existing
   claim on `richard-doty`, `paul-bennewitz`, `afosi`, `afosi-kirtland-file`, `d-psyop` and

   But on camera to George Knapp he says the driving-him-mad story is "false" in "every aspect"
   and that Bennewitz "did this to himself". Both statements are his. A man who says he ran a
   deception operation and who also denies its most damaging effect is not resolvable by picking
   the quote one prefers. The node should carry the contradiction as a claim in its own right.
3. **The documented hole in the ground near Dulce is a nuclear one, and it predates the rumour by
   twelve years.** Project Gasbuggy, 10 December 1967, 29 kilotons, 4,227 feet, 12 miles southwest
   of Dulce, on the Carson National Forest just west of the Jicarilla Apache Reservation, AEC with
   the Department of the Interior and El Paso Natural Gas. Production testing July 1968 to October
   1969. Liquid radioactive waste injected into the former cavity. Surface remediation completed
   September 2004; 5,562 cubic yards of contaminated soil removed. This is the strongest
   counter-explanation artefact in the file and it is a government fact sheet, not an argument.
4. **Cooper's own 1989 text does not say Dulce.** I extracted all 49 pages and searched. Zero

   underground for the use of the alien nation and that two bases would be constructed for the joint
   use of the alien nation and the United States Government ... These alien bases would be
   constructed under Indian reservations in the Four Corners area of Utah, Colorado, New Mexico and
   Arizona". The Dulce name attaches elsewhere. Any edge from `bill-cooper` to `dulce-base` must be
   worded as frame-propagation, not as naming the site, or it will be wrong on its own source.
5. **Bennewitz has a federal burial record and it is an ordinary one.** Paul Frederick Bennewitz
   Jr, 30 September 1927 to 23 June 2003, Santa Fe National Cemetery, Section 12 Site 402, marker
   inscribed "S1C US COAST GUARD WORLD WAR II". The man at the centre of the best-documented
   domestic disinformation operation in this subject was a Second World War Coast Guard seaman who
   ran a humidity-instrument company. That is worth one line on the node, against a literature that
   only ever describes him as a target.
6. **Schneider's claim is precisely dated and precisely unsupported.** 8 May 1995, Post Falls,
   Idaho. Seven levels, 2.5 miles deep, two aliens shot, roughly seventy dead. Against that: no
   opened record of his employment by anyone, no opened record of a 1979 incident, and a death

   date of discovery in circulation. **I did not open a death certificate or a medical examiner's
   report.** The suicide ruling is in WHAT I COULD NOT ESTABLISH, not in the claims.

### 4.2 What the documented record of the place shows

**A 29-kiloton nuclear device was detonated 12 miles southwest of Dulce on 10 December 1967, at
4,227 feet, in well GB-E.** That is Project Gasbuggy, on a DOE Office of Legacy Management fact

testing ran **July 1968 to October 1969**, producing 213 million cubic feet of measurably
radioactive gas. Testing stopped in **1976**. AEC decommissioned in **1978** and injected liquid
radioactive waste into the former cavity. Final surface remediation completed **September 2004**,
removing 5,562 cubic yards of contaminated soil. A permanent monument, a brass plaque in a concrete
base, marks the spot.

This is the documented underground thing near Dulce. It is federal, it is nuclear, it left
contamination, it required decades of government presence, monitoring, drilling and soil hauling,
and it is twelve years older than the rumour. **The board should carry it as a node and as the hub's
principal counter-explanation edge**, without asserting the causal claim: I have not opened anything
that demonstrates Gasbuggy generated the rumour, and Hayakawa's chronology does not say so either.
What is documented is that the government did a large, secretive, radioactive thing under the ground
next to Dulce, and then came back for forty years.

Tom Sharpe's 2013 report adds the adjacent documented figure: **Gabe Valdez, a New Mexico State
Police officer stationed in Dulce in 1976**, who ran the livestock investigation. **Tier 2 from a
named newspaper.** The thesis of his son's 2013 book, that the deaths related to contamination
testing after the nuclear work, is that book's thesis and is recorded here as such, not endorsed.
