### What the documents establish

**What the programme was.** The 1963 survey's cover memorandum: "The MKULTRA activity is
concerned with the research and development of chemical, biological and radiological
materials capable of employment in clandestine operations to control human behavior." It
adds that the cryptonym covers the research and development phase and that a second
cryptonym, MKDELTA, denotes the Deputy Director/Plans system controlling operational use.

**Who authorized it and when.** The same memorandum: "MKULTRA was authorized by the then
Director of Central Intelligence, Mr. Allen W. Dulles, in 1953." The Technical Services
Division was assigned a portion of its research budget, "eventually set at 20%", and "Normal
procedures for project approval, funding, and accounting were waived." The Church Committee
text reprinted as Appendix B of the hearing print dates the approval: "MKULTRA was approved
by the DCI on April 13, 1953 along the lines proposed by ADDP Helms." A 1955 invoice
certification inside the Subproject 43 file (section 3 below) independently cites "the
memorandum dated 13 April 1953 from the DCI to the DD/A".

**The destruction, and who ordered it.** Appendix B: "In January 1973, MKULTRA records were
destroyed by Technical Services Division personnel acting on the verbal orders of Dr. Sidney
Gottlieb, Chief of TSD. Dr. Gottlieb has testified, and former Director Helms has confirmed,
that in ordering the records destroyed, Dr. Gottlieb was carrying out the verbal order of
then DCI Helms."

**The surviving financial records: what they are and why they survived.** Admiral Stansfield
Turner's prepared statement reports "our recent discovery of seven boxes of documents related
to Project MKULTRA, a closely held CIA project conducted from 1953-1964". They were found at
the Agency's Retired Records Center by an employee responding to FOIA requests. The reason
they survived is an administrative accident, stated in the document: financial papers on
sensitive projects were normally kept by the branch under the project file, but this material
"was sent to the Retired Records Center in 1970 by the Budget and Fiscal Section as part of
its own retired holdings. The reason for this departure from normal procedure is not known.
As a result of it, however, the material escaped retrieval and destruction in 1973 ... as well
as discovery in 1975".

**What is in them.** "the recovered folders are finance folders", holding "149 MKULTRA
subprojects", two boxes of miscellaneous papers including audit reports and financial
statements from "cut-out" funding mechanisms, and 33 further subprojects unrelated to
behavioural work. Turner's oral testimony gives the new identities: "the names of 185
nongovernment researchers and assistants", and "names of 80 institutions", of which "44
colleges or universities, 15 research foundation or chemical or pharmaceutical companies or
the like, 12 hospitals or clinics ... and 3 penal institutions". He also reports "a
contribution of $375,000 to a building fund of a private medical institution" routed through
an intermediary so as to appear a private donation and thereby attract matching federal
funds.

**An internal discrepancy inside the one document.** The opening statement in the same print
says "Eighty-six universities or institutions were involved." Turner's testimony forty pages
later says 80 institutions and 185 individuals. Both figures are in the same hearing. A node
should give Turner's, which is itemised, and say the opening statement differs.

### What the documents establish

**Three offices, in sequence, each from a document.** In 1955 he signs invoice
certifications on a numbered MKULTRA subproject as "SIDNEY GOTTLIEB, Chief, TSS/CD", that is
Chief of the Chemical Division of the Technical Services Staff. In 1963 the Inspector General
writes: "The present Deputy Chief of TSD, Dr. Sidney Gottlieb, in his then capacity of
scientific advisor to the Deputy Director/Plans (DD/P), released a study ... on 21 April 1960,
covering his six-month investigation of 'Scientific and Technical Problems in Covert Action
Operations'." In January 1973 the Church text calls him "Dr. Sidney Gottlieb, Chief of TSD".

**What he certified.** The Subproject 43 file carries his certification that "performance is
satisfactory, that the services are being accomplished in accordance with mutual agreements,
that a detailed agenda of the payments and receipts is on file in TSS/CD, that this bill is
just and correct". Invoice No. 1 is dated 31 March 1955 and Invoice No. 2 is dated 28 June
1955.

**The Olson experiment, in the Church text.** "The drug had been placed in the bottle by a
CIA officer, Dr. Robert Lashbrook, as part of an experiment he and Dr. Sidney Gottlieb
performed at a meeting of Army and CIA scientists." The same text records that neither the
Chief of TSS nor the Deputy Director for Plans specifically authorized that experiment, and
that Gottlieb secured only his immediate supervisor's approval.

**What the 1977 committee could not get.** The questioning of Turner turns repeatedly on
Gottlieb's absence. Senator Kennedy: "But Gottlieb would know, would he not?" Turner's answer
in substance is that Gottlieb had been interviewed in 1975 and had not been re-interviewed on
the newly recovered material. The hearing closes without him.

## 6. Eric Olson and the death of Frank Olson

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED as an event with a paper trail on both sides. The triage row's court is
CONTRADICTED, and four opened records disagree with each other on the details.** All three

### What the documents establish

**The Rockefeller Commission does not name him, and adds a line about his stability.** Its
account: "on one occasion during the early phases of this program (in 1953), LSD was
administered to an employee of the Department of the Army without his knowledge while he was
attending a meeting with CIA personnel working on the drug project ... He developed serious
side effects and was sent to New York with a CIA escort for psychiatric treatment. Several
days later, he jumped from a tenth floor window of his room and died as a result." Its
footnote to that sentence: "There are indications in the few remaining Agency records that
this individual may have had a history of emotional instability." It then records that "The
General Counsel ruled that the death resulted from 'circumstances arising out of an experiment
undertaken in the course of his official duties for the United States Government'", ensuring
death benefits, and that "Reprimands were issued by the Director of Central Intelligence to
two CIA employees responsible for the incident."

**The compensation act.** Private Law 94-126, 90 Stat. 3006 (1976), quoted in full in the 2013
opinion, directs the Secretary of the Treasury to pay "the sum of $187,500 each to Alice W.
Olson, Lisa Olson Hayward, Eric Olson, and Nils Olson, in full settlement of all of their
claims against the United States arising out of the death of Doctor Frank R. Olson in November
1953, if all of them waive any and all rights arising out of such death." President Ford's
signing statement of 12 October 1976 gives the bill as S. 3035 and the total as $750,000, and
records that his administration had supported $1,250,000 and would support further relief
legislation to raise it.

**The suit, and the court.** ERIC OLSON and NILS OLSON v. UNITED STATES OF AMERICA, Civil
Action No. 12-1924 (JEB), District of Columbia, memorandum opinion filed 17 July 2013. Its
first paragraph: "In 1953, CIA employees slipped LSD into a scientist's drink ... According to
his children, the scientist's 'accidental death' was in fact a secret assassination, suppressed
by the CIA to this day." The Government's motion to dismiss is granted on three grounds: most
allegations are untimely, most are waived by the 1976 settlement, and any surviving claims fall
outside the waiver of sovereign immunity. The Court also records a judgement about the pleading
that a node should carry: "the skeptical reader may wish to know that the public record
supports many of the allegations that follow, farfetched as they may sound."

experiment at a meeting of LSD researchers called by CIA." It also records that the cryptonym
MKULTRA became publicly known during the Rockefeller Commission and Church Committee
investigations "when details of the drug-related death of Dr. Frank Olson were publicized".
