Read-only audit of the board against a canon of figures, places, events, documents,

## 1. Method, and its limits

Board state read from `app/public/data/index.json` (1,057 nodes; 311 person, 213 document,
191 institution, 81 event, 76 program, 69 location, 59 domain, 57 legislation) and
cross-checked against `data/the-program.json` (311 people, 746 entities, 2,460 edges).
`research/_removed.json` was read first: 53 removed ids and 21 aliases, none of which touch
any entry below, so no gap here is a reversal of a prior decision. Canon list: 241 entries,
each one I can describe accurately in one line, built from my own knowledge plus web checks
on the Disclosure Project 2001 witness panel and the 2013 Citizen Hearing roster.
Matching was case-insensitive regex against node `id`, `label` and `short`, with name
variants handled (Bob/Robert, Sorensen/Sorenson, Brazel/Brazil). On-disk counts come from a
single regex pass over all 855 files under `research/`, aggregated by corpus.

**Limits, stated plainly.** The dataset carries no alias field, so a figure recorded only
under an unexpected label could still be missed. Regex matching produced two false positives
I caught by hand (`ihedn` for "Haut", `jhuapl` for "Hopkins") and three false negatives
(Karl E. Nell, Christopher C. 'Kit' Green, Eric W. Davis, all present); more may remain.
Mention counts are raw string hits, so common surnames inflate and `research/.transcripts/`
double-counts material also cut into the corpus files. "Not triaged" means no row found in
the six triage files per pass, not that no agent ever considered it.

## 5. Proposed change to the triage rule

The present rule promotes a lead to a node only when an openable primary record anchors the
claim, which is why this board carries the officials and omits the claimants: an officer's
commission leaves paper and a claimant's story does not. Add a second, parallel criterion
**notability of the claim itself**: a person, place, programme or event belongs on the board
when a reader informed about this subject would expect to find it and would read its absence
as a gap in the map, whether or not any record vouches for what was said. Such a node is
graded honestly at the tier the evidence supports, usually Tier 2 for "X said Y on a dated
record" and Tier 3 for inference, its openable anchor is the dated artefact of the claim
itself (a recorded testimony, a broadcast, a book, a filing) rather than a record of the
thing claimed, and the case against goes in the same node's `conflicts` and `unverified`
fields, not in a separate file. Where no record of any kind exists, the node says so in its
own narrative, because "no employment, service or civil record has ever been produced for
this person" is itself a finding and is more useful to a reader than silence.
