## 4. STRUCTURAL CLAIMS

Numbered so the board can cite them. All are the named speaker's claims (or, for S6–S9, claims made by the fictional characters in the file 04 magazine article, which is explicitly labelled unverified fiction by its own byline and by Ramsay).

**S1 — PRP as the disclosure deterrent (Woods).** Woods states directly that the Personal Reliability Program is why he said nothing about part of what he saw that night: "had I said what I saw out that right-side window... my whole career would have been down to tubes." He frames PRP less as a safety measure and more as the reason nuclear-site witnesses self-censor. (03 / 1:27:05–1:27:54)

**S2 — Civilian in the debrief room (Woods).** Woods says an unidentified tall, thin, older man in a civilian grey tweed suit sat in on his commander's-office debrief alongside OSI officers, and that he was never introduced. Woods separately recalls that Bob Jacobs (a different, already-documented 1964 Vandenberg witness — cross-reference `gerb-corpus-01-12.md` §3, 1964 Vandenberg) also described "gentlemen in tweed suits" at his own debrief, and Ramsay raises the possible connection; Woods does not confirm it is the same person or practice. (03 / 1:48:02–1:50:41)

**S3 — Non-disclosure paperwork, no copy retained (Woods).** Woods says he signed a document read to him by Captain Reed that he now believes functioned as a non-disclosure agreement, was given no copy of it, no copy of his own written report, and no copy of his medical records from the same-day flight-surgeon exam, which included two tissue samples taken near burn marks on his hand and above his eye. (03 / 2:04:11–2:05:53, 1:56:19–1:58:37)

**S4 — Institutional non-follow-up (Woods).** Woods describes AARO's process as extractive rather than investigative: hours of sworn testimony, a promise to "call you if we have questions," and no further contact; he separately says his written report to Congressman Burchett's office went unanswered, and that Congressional testimony would require witnesses to pay their own travel costs. (03 / 2:50:59–2:56:02)

**S5 — Missing-time and a shared, later-recovered memory as corroboration (Woods).** Woods presents Michael Johnson's unprompted, independent recollection — weeks after the event, at Woods's apartment — of a specific detail (Woods's dropped right-hand glove on a "shiny floor," which Woods says was never recovered from his kit) as his strongest evidence that the missing-time gap was shared and real rather than individually confabulated. (03 / 2:12:36–2:16:03)

**S6 — [Fiction, file 04] Chain-of-command control over what a witness is allowed to remember.** The read article has an entire 67-man recovery detail develop an identical 90-minute memory gap after a "mothership" allegedly hovered over a smaller crash-retrieval operation; when polygraph/hypnosis/drugs are proposed to break through it, the commanding Captain refuses on the stated grounds that these are trusted, loyal personnel who should not be treated "like counterintelligence suspects." The article frames the refusal itself as protecting the cover story rather than the men. (04 / 0:40:05–1:13:31)

**S7 — [Fiction, file 04] A record-keeping practice for low-priority sightings.** The article has a fictional USAF Captain show "Masters" a records room ("the ballroom") holding "over two million files" of glowing-ball sighting reports collected since 1945, explicitly never followed up on individually and never computerised, kept "on the off chance" of future significance. (04 / 2:03:14–2:04:58)

**S8 — [Fiction, file 04] An inert recovered craft accidentally activated and now unable to be switched off.** The article describes a small single-seat "flying wing" craft, recovered in the Midwest in 1972 alongside a dead small-grey pilot, inert and unpowered under study for years until a researcher "abandoned protocol," pressed unrecorded touchscreen/proximity controls, and activated it; the article states the sequence could not be reproduced or reversed and the craft had been running continuously for "over a year and a half" as of the story's telling. (04 / 2:07:38–2:13:00)

**S9 — [Fiction, file 04] Foreign-government cooperation in a recovery.** The article states a large disc-shaped craft was recovered from shallow water off the Danish coast with the Danish government's cooperation, then trucked into Wright-Patterson under tarp, by night, under heavy security, and is depicted as still under study years later, including cryogenic ("supercool") testing that made the interior unsafe to enter at the time of the fictional visit. (04 / 2:16:38–2:19:16)
