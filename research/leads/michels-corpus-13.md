## 3. STRUCTURAL CLAIMS — compartmentalisation, custody, contractor role, disclosure posture

Numbered so the board can cite them. All are the **named speaker's claims**, several
explicitly flagged by the speaker himself as inference or guesswork.

**S1 — Compartmentalisation by building/worksite, not by rank.** Rogers: at Cape Canaveral,
personnel assigned to a specific worksite could enter that building and no other, "so we
didn't have cross contamination of people learning things they had no need to know." He
personally held about 12 different site ID cards because his medical role took him to ~80%
of the Cape's buildings. (3 / 0:34:06–0:35:48)

**S2 — Seniority does not equal access.** Rogers, generalising from the encounter: "a tech
sergeant enlisted... is read into a program, whereas the major is not." A three-star general
on vacation was refused entry at the gate by an enlisted guard, and the guard's decision
held. (3 / 1:09:34–1:15:46)

**S3 — A defense-contractor-owned prototype can carry USAF markings without USAF
ownership or inventory status.** Rogers' own reasoning, modelled explicitly on the Have
Blue → F-117 precedent: the craft he saw had "U.S. Air Force" painted on it and an Air
Force insignia, umbilicals feeding it power/control externally (which he reads as evidence
of an immature, non-flight platform), and was — in his own words — "probably owned by a
defense contractor... not part of the U.S. Air Force inventory." (3 / 0:45:27–0:48:04)

**S4 — Geographic laundering of classification.** Rogers: putting a covert project on Cape
Canaveral lets anyone who asks be told "it's just related to the space program," where the
same project in New Mexico would draw scrutiny. (3 / 1:15:46–1:16:35)

**S5 — Reporting incentives run against disclosure even absent a formal gag.** Rogers did
not report the sighting in 1992 because doing so would have forced him to report the
lieutenant colonel and Major up the chain, risked his credibility if the video "couldn't be
found," and threatened his career — a purely career-incentive account, not a claim of
coercion. (3 / 1:00:12–1:03:28)

**S6 — EG&G as the connective tissue across the alleged programme's history.** Narrator's
synthesis (not Rogers' own claim): from Manhattan Project photography, through AEC/DOE
contractor status, Nevada Test Site operation, Blue Gill Triple Prime filming, Area 51
Special Projects Division gatekeeping (badges, screening, the Janet flights), to Bob
Lazar's alleged S4 background check and the 2002 Wilson/Davis meeting occurring in EG&G's
own parking lot. Rogers, asked directly whether EG&G might have owned the craft he saw,
says no — "it would have been something like Lockheed or Northrop, Boeing... Raytheon" —
distinguishing EG&G's site-management/gatekeeping role from ownership of any recovered
technology. (3 / 0:22:54–1:14:47)
