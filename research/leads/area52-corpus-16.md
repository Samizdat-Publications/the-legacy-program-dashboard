Numbered so the board can cite them. All are the **named speaker's claims**, several explicitly
flagged by the speaker as their own inference or belief rather than as established fact.

**S1 — The control group is mostly private, not governmental.** Coulthart, asked directly
whether the "control group" running the legacy program sits outside government jurisdiction,
says most of its people work in private aerospace — Battelle, Lockheed Martin, Northrop
Grumman, Boeing, Raytheon — vetted through the CIA's science and technology directorate as
"a safe pair of hands," then periodically used to brief in trusted politicians. (76 / 0:39:00)

**S2 — A secret executive order, not statute, is the legal basis for withholding briefings
from the President.** Coulthart, citing unnamed "top constitutional lawyers," says there is
no legal basis under which a sitting president (he cites Clinton as an example of one who
asked and was refused) could be denied a briefing, except a still-secret executive order
"probably" signed under Truman, with a second signed under Eisenhower. (76 / 0:40:48-0:41:45)

**S3 — Congressional oversight of the legacy program is deliberately narrow and selectively
briefed.** Only some Gang of Eight members have been "read in"; Coulthart expects this
selective-briefing fact to be used later as an excuse ("we told the trusted few") when the
program becomes public. He separately says a senator he knows personally lied to the public
about what he had been told. (76 / 0:35:16-0:40:48)

**S4 — Audit gaps are a deliberate concealment mechanism, per David Grush.** Coulthart:
Pentagon "audit holes" that mean money cannot be accounted for are, per Grush's own
interviews, used to conceal the legacy program's funding — inflating routine contractor
invoices ("$5,000 for a bag of bolts") as the mechanism. (76 / 0:43:36-0:44:34)

**S5 — Contractor whistleblowers currently have no legal protection, unlike DoD/IC
employees.** Coulthart: proposed disclosure legislation would have extended whistleblower
protections to contractors (e.g. Northrop Grumman employees), which is why he says people
like Jake Barber "opted out" of further public testimony once it became clear the legal
protections were not going to pass. (76 / 1:16:07-1:19:33)

**S6 — A parallel, unacknowledged program can hide from even highly-cleared insiders.**
David Grush, cleared for 2,000+ SAPs and carrying the presidential daily brief, did not know
about the legacy program until colleagues gave him "a bit of a nudge" in the Pentagon
cafeteria; a senior former NASA/DoD official told Coulthart he genuinely does not know
anything about it, while a person three organisational rungs below that official is,
according to Coulthart, the program's "gatekeeper" inside NASA. (76 / 1:22:17-1:24:15)

**S7 — Trillions, not billions, have been spent without Congressional consent.** Coulthart's
own figure, given without a source beyond "I believe": trillions of dollars expended on the
legacy program "without the knowledge or consent of the American taxpayer... with very
