### What the documents establish

**The Board and what it was for.** The directive opens: "It is the purpose of this directive
to authorize and provide for the more effective planning, coordination and conduct, within
the framework of approved national policies, of psychological operations." It establishes the
Board "for the formulation and promulgation, as guidance to the departments and agencies
responsible for psychological operations, of over-all national psychological objectives,
policies and programs, and for the coordination and evaluation of the national psychological
effort." The Board reports to the National Security Council. It is composed of the Under
Secretary of State, the Deputy Secretary of Defense and the Director of Central Intelligence,
plus representatives of other departments as the Board determines, with a Joint Chiefs of
Staff representative sitting as principal military adviser.

**The Director's post, and Gray in it.** The directive establishes "under the Board a
Director who shall be designated by the President and who shall receive compensation of
$16,000 per year", responsible for preparing the Board's programs and papers, promulgating
its decisions, ascertaining how they are implemented across departments, and organising a
staff whose members, when detailed in, "shall be under the control of the Director". The
participating departments "shall afford to the Director and the staff such assistance and
access to information as may be specifically requested". Footnote 3 to that sentence reads:
"Gordon Gray, President of the University of North Carolina and former Secretary of the Army,
was appointed Director of the new board." Document 82 then shows him in the chair's company:
the minutes of PSB M-2 list "Mr. Gordon Gray, Director, Psychological Strategy Board" among
those present with the DCI, the Under Secretary of State and the Deputy Secretary of Defense.

**Four offices, with dates.** The FRUS persons list gives: "Gray, Gordon, Secretary of the
Army from June 20, 1949 to April 12, 1950; Director of the Psychological Strategy Board from
1951 to 1952; member, the President's Committee on International Information Activities from
January 20, 1953 to June 30, 1953; Assistant Secretary of Defense for International Security
Affairs from July 1955 to February 1957; Director of Defense Mobilization from March 1957 to
July 1958; Special Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs from July 1958 to
January 1961; member, Committee for Information Activities Abroad from February 17, 1960 to
January 12, 1961".

**A limit on the directive itself.** Its penultimate paragraph: "This directive does not
authorize the Board nor the Director to perform any 'psychological operations'." A node that
uses the PSB as an operating body would be wrong on the face of the founding paper.

## 2. Institute of Field Physics, University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED as an institution, and the transcript's framing of it is
CONTRADICTED.** The Institute was real, incorporated, industrially funded and tied to the
Glenn L. Martin Company by money. It also carried a standing written denial of any connection
to anti-gravity research, which is the opposite of the reason the corpus reaches for it.

### What the documents establish

**Incorporation, name and people.** "The IOFP received its certificate of incorporation on
September 7, 1955." It was nearly called "The Research Institute of the University of North
Carolina", which the University and Bryce DeWitt approved and the funder did not. It was run
by Bryce and Cecile DeWitt; by 7 December 1955 DeWitt was giving his position as "Director of
the IOFP".

**The funder and the money.** Agnew Bahnson, a Winston-Salem industrialist who made his
fortune in industrial air conditioning, was the primary funder. He first wrote to DeWitt on 30
May 1955 and met him on 9 July 1955 in Raleigh. He had originally planned to site the
institute at State College in Raleigh because it had a nuclear reactor which, in Bryce
DeWitt's words, Bahnson felt "might be useful for anti-gravity". Bahnson died in an aeroplane
crash on 3 June 1964, and a chair was established at UNC in his honour for Bryce DeWitt.

**The Glenn L. Martin Company connection, and its price.** Trimble and the Glenn Martin
Company "purchase[d] a 'Founder's Membership' for the Institute of Field Physics, for the
considerable sum of $5000", and offered to help solicit further funding. The same page records
that the Martin company was simultaneously setting up its own institute, which "would become
RIAS", that Louis Witten "was the first researcher hired by RIAS, of the Glenn Martin
Company", that Witten approached DeWitt to join RIAS and lost him to Bahnson, and that the
Martin company is "now Lockheed-Martin". The two institutes were deliberately different in
kind: the IOFP's plan "was to house an institute within an academic institution, so as to
avoid the conflict that physicists felt working in an industrial setting."

**The anti-gravity disclaimer, which is the finding.** John Wheeler, writing to the acting
president of the University of North Carolina on 25 November 1955, warned of "the absolute
necessity to avoid identification with so-called 'anti-gravity research' that may be today's
version of the last century's search for a perpetual motion machine." He had already proposed
attaching a disclaimer to every piece of IOFP publicity, and the page reproduces it as the
"Protection Clause": "The work in field physics and gravitation theory carried on at the
University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, and financed by the Institute of Field Physics,
as fundraising agency, has no connection with so-called 'anti-gravity research' of whatever
kind and for whatever purposes." Bahnson's own November 1955 draft brochure foreword said:
"In the minds of the public, the subject of gravity is often associated with fantastic
possibilities. From the standpoint of the institute, no specific practical results of the
studies can be foreseen at this time." The history's own summary: the message that
anti-gravity connotations must be avoided at all costs "runs through much of the
correspondence and foundation documents like a mantra."

**Government money did arrive, and the page says which government body.** Joshua Goldberg,
then handling parts of the Air Force's funding of general relativity in the "modern physics
research branch" at the Aeronautical Research Laboratory, Wright Patterson Air Force Base,
gave a $5,000 grant and secured Military Air Transport Service flights for foreign
participants, eventually from eleven nations. Note the laboratory: the page uses neither
"Wright Air Development Center" nor "WADC", a point the board already carries on
`george-trimble`.

**The inaugural conference.** The published report states that in January 1957 physicists from
several countries met at the University of North Carolina for the Conference on the Role of
Gravitation in Physics, and that "The Chapel Hill conference also marked the establishment of
the Institute of Field Physics, directed by Bryce and Cecile DeWitt. The conference was the
inaugural conference of this institute."

### What the documents establish

**The office, and the civilian bar.** Section 202(a) of the National Security Act of 1947:
"There shall be a Secretary of Defense, who shall be appointed from civilian life by the
President, by and with the advice and consent of the Senate: Provided, That a person who has
within ten years been on active duty as a commissioned officer in a Regular component of the
armed services shall not be eligible for appointment as Secretary of Defense." The same
section makes him "the principal assistant to the President in all matters relating to the
national security" and gives him "general direction, authority, and control" over the
departments and agencies of the National Military Establishment.

**The dates.** The FRUS persons list: "Forrestal, James V., Secretary of the Navy until
September 1947; Secretary of Defense, September 17, 1947-March 28, 1949."
