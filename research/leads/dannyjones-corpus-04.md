## 3. STRUCTURAL CLAIMS

Numbered so the board can cite them. All are the **speaker's own claims**, several explicitly
flagged by the speaker as their own inference.

**S1 — A live, non-human being was allegedly transferred through a Brazilian civilian medical
system before US personnel arrived.** Fox's chain: captured by two military police officers →
refused at a private clinic → admitted alive to Regional Hospital's ICU under Dr. Italo →
subsequently moved to a military base → "the Americans came in" at Campinas. Fox states this
is corroborated, independently, by five to six first-hand witnesses across a 16-year
investigation. (02 / throughout, esp. 0:34:22–0:36:03)

**S2 — An intelligence contact's own framing for extra-territorial access: "because we can."**
Fox recounts asking an unnamed intelligence contact why the US could act inside Brazilian
territory without apparent consequence; the answer given was simply "because we can," which
Fox treats as evidence of an established, unaccountable operational capability rather than a
one-off. (02 / 0:35:33–0:36:03)

**S3 — Compartmented access, self-reported by a sitting AARO director.** "I can't part my own
hair without approval from the DIA" — offered by "John Koskowski" as an explanation for why
determinations AARO reaches do not translate into public disclosure. Fox treats this as
confirmation that AARO's findings are gated above the office itself. (02 / 1:09:03–1:09:30)

**S4 — A steep, self-reported access hierarchy.** An unnamed DIA contact tells Fox that
roughly 10 people worldwide "have the bigger picture," ranks Fox's own access at "one of 30,"
and Hal Puthoff at "one of 50." Asked what makes the bigger picture "scary," the contact
answers with one word, repeated: **"intent."** No further elaboration was obtained before the
conversation was interrupted. (02 / 1:20:51–1:23:37)

**S5 — Named special access programmes, per an unverified secondary claim about Jason Sands.**
Fox's inside contacts reportedly told him Sands "named specific special access programmes,
dealing with the legacy UAP, you know, crash retrieval stuff" and that Fox himself "got in
trouble just for asking about those names." Fox does not name the programmes and flags Sands's
broader testimony as unreliable due to reported PTSD and possible implanted memories. (02 /
1:11:39–1:12:37)

**S6 — Ridicule as declared Air Force/CIA policy, dated to the Robertson Panel.** Fox's
retelling: the panel is said to have advised that outright denial was untenable given
"compelling first-hand testimony," so the recommended posture was ridicule instead —
discrediting witnesses and observers rather than the phenomenon's existence. (02 /
2:22:52–2:23:42)

**S7 — Disinformation used to protect classified US technology and mislead foreign
intelligence, via the Bennewitz case.** Fox's retelling of *Mirage Men*: AFOSI's Rick Doty
allegedly confirmed to Bennewitz that what he'd photographed near a Nevada base was US
technology, then the NSA allegedly fed Bennewitz fabricated "alien" transmissions — both, per
Fox's framing, to (a) protect the classified programme and (b) mislead Soviet infiltrators
inside the MUFON community who were fishing for the same secrets via Bennewitz. (02 /
2:13:52–2:17:55)

**S8 — A proposed immunity/disclosure bargain, offered by Fox as a hypothetical.** Fox's own
proposal, put to the audience rather than sourced to an official: keep the recovered
technology classified for national security, but grant immunity so insiders (naming Eric
Davis specifically) can testify under oath to a bipartisan congressional panel about the
biologics, with the reasoning that "disclosure...doesn't just have implications for the United
States" but is global. (02 / 1:32:25–1:34:10; 2:59:47–3:02:13)
