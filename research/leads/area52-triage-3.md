## What this group holds, in one paragraph

Batches 11 to 14 are the weakest structural slice of this corpus. Four of the sixteen files
are the host reading a published book, a magazine article or a Reddit post aloud, one is a
channel update, and most of the remainder is first-person abduction and contactee testimony
that the brief carves out. What survives is narrow but real, and it is almost all **foreign**:
a Belgian air force that scrambled interceptors and then published, a British MoD file on a
1993 intrusion, a Spanish air surveillance squadron whose case was sealed under a domestic
secrets statute. The board already carries GEIPAN, COMETA, CEFAA, DGAC Chile, Peru OIFAA, Sky
Canada, `uk-mod` and `spain-mod-ufo-files`, and this group's best candidates fill the two
holes in that comparative set. The batch 13 remote-viewing material is almost entirely
**section C**, because `stargate-program`, `grill-flame`, `center-lane`, `sun-streak`,
`sri-international` and `d-remote-viewing` are already nodes. The batch 14 material is almost
entirely **noise**, because its central artefact is a disputed 1996 videotape whose every
provenance claim is a person's reaction rather than a document, stated as such by the speaker
himself (map 14, S2).
