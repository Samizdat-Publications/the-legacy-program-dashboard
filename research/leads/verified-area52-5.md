## 1. Armed Forces Special Weapons Project

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED.** The organisation, its charter, its effective date, its founding
authority, its first Chief and its descent to DTRA were all read in the Department of
Defense's own agency history, including the charter reproduced in facsimile in its
appendix, and cross-read against the National Archives record group description.

### What was opened

| URL | What is at it |
|---|---|

| https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/374.html | "Records of the Defense Threat Reduction Agency", Guide to Federal Records in the National Archives, Record Group 374, 1943-73 |

The title page reads "Defense's Nuclear agency 1947 - 1997 / defense Threat Reduction
Agency / U.S. Department of Defense / Washington, d.C, / 2002". Capitalisation is the OCR's.
The book is an official publication of the DTRA History Series; its own preface says the
views are the authors' and editors' and do not necessarily represent those of the
Department of Defense, and that some official sources cited in its notes may remain
classified.

### What the documents establish

**The charter, in facsimile.** Appendix D reproduces War Department Memorandum No. 850-25-8,
Washington 25, D.C., 18 March 1947. Paragraph 1: "The Armed Forces Special Weapons Project
established by joint directive of the Secretaries of War and Navy, effective as of midnight
31 December 1946, will operate directly under the Chief of Staff and the Chief of Naval
Operations." Paragraph 2 gives it responsibility "for carrying on the military service
functions of the Manhattan Project retained under control of the armed forces". Paragraph 3
itemises those: training of special personnel, military participation in the development of
atomic weapons of all types in coordination with the Atomic Energy Commission, technical
training of bomb commanders and weaponeers, and joint radiological safety measures.
Paragraph 5 assigns to AFSWP, as of 31 December 1946, the Army units that had been assigned
to the Manhattan Engineer District. The memorandum issues "BY ORDER OF THE SECRETARY OF WAR"
over Dwight D. Eisenhower as Chief of Staff.

**The AEC paid for it.** Paragraph 6 of the same memorandum: funds for AFSWP other than
military pay "have been made available by working fund advances to War Department from the
Atomic Energy Commission, utilizing funds formerly available to the Manhattan Project."

**The narrative dates and names the founding memorandum.** Chapter text at page 30: a
memorandum issued by Secretary of War Robert P. Patterson and Secretary of the Navy James V.
Forrestal on January 29, 1947, "generally referred to as the AFSWP Charter", established
effective midnight December 31, 1946 "a joint Army-Navy atomic energy organization which
will discharge all military Service functions relating to atomic energy and will be known
as the Armed Forces Special Weapons Project." The head was to be appointed by, and report
directly to, the Army Chief of Staff and the Chief of Naval Operations, who would also
select a deputy from the opposite Service, and both the head and the deputy were directed
to serve as members of the Military Liaison Committee to the AEC. Forrestal underlined the
word "joint" in a further memorandum to Patterson the same day.

**No chief for the first two months, and headquarters in Washington.** Between the beginning
of January and the end of February 1947 there was no officially appointed chief; Groves's
aide Colonel Hasbrouck held headquarters command. The offices were first in the new War
Department Building at 21st Street and Virginia Avenue NW, where the Manhattan District's
Washington offices had been, and moved to the Pentagon on 15 April 1947. The chronology in
Appendix C: "Feb. 28, 1947. Maj Gen Groves becomes first Chief AFS WP, serving for one
year, until his retirement on February 29, 1948".

**Sandia and Z Division.** The book places Z Division and its facilities on Sandia Base and
records that the Commission wished to retain control of them, while the 2761st Engineer
Battalion (Special) under Dorland continued day to day work at the base and a Technical
Training Group under Lieutenant Colonel Ord was set up there in January 1947. In March 1947
the AEC Santa Fe Office and AFSWP at Sandia Base divided security and intelligence
responsibilities between them.

**The lineage, from the agency itself.** Preface and epilogue: MED to AFSWP to DASA
(1959-71) to DNA (1971-96) to DSWA (1996-98) to DTRA (1998). The epilogue's closing line is
"The nation's oldest defense agency thus lives on; MED to AFSWP to DAS A to DNA to DSWA to
DTRA."

**NARA independently confirms the establishment.** RG 374 section 374.2: "AFSWP established
as an interservice agency, effective December 31, 1946, by a joint letter of the Secretaries
of War and the Navy, January 29, 1947, with responsibility for discharging all military
functions relating to atomic energy in coordination with the Atomic Energy Commission".

| Gerb | The record |
|---|---|
| Formed between 1947 and 1948 | Effective midnight 31 December 1946; charter memorandum 29 January 1947; War Department memorandum publishing it 18 March 1947 |
| A joint venture of the Navy and an element of the intelligence community | A joint Army-Navy organisation under the Army Chief of Staff and the Chief of Naval Operations. No intelligence body appears in the charter |
| Founded under James Forrestal | Forrestal signed as Secretary of the Navy, jointly with Robert P. Patterson as Secretary of War, and the memorandum issued by order of the Secretary of War over Eisenhower's signature as Chief of Staff |
| Formed at Los Alamos Z Division, which is Sandia Base | Z Division was at Sandia Base and the AEC wanted to keep control of it. AFSWP headquarters was in Washington from the start and moved to the Pentagon on 15 April 1947 |

The history says nothing about recovered material, non-human craft or any programme of that
kind. What it documents is the military organisation that inherited the Manhattan Project's
service functions and was funded by AEC working-fund advances.

## 2. Defense Threat Reduction Agency

**VERDICT: PARTLY CONFIRMED.** The agency, its establishing directive, its date and its
descent from AFSWP were opened and are documented three times over. What DTRA itself
publishes about the records it holds from the AEC and DNA eras was also opened, and it is
nuclear test participation and human radiation experimentation records, not what the
speaker describes. The AFSWP, DASA and DNA era textual records are at the National Archives
in RG 374, and the AEC's are in a separate record group.

`dtra.mil` refuses this machine: `https://www.dtra.mil/FOIA/` answers 403 to curl. That half

### What the documents establish

**Establishment.** RG 374 section 374.1: "Established: In the Department of Defense (DOD) as
a combat support agency, effective October 1, 1998, by DOD Directive 5105.62, September 30,
1998, consolidating the Defense Special Weapons Agency, the On-Site Inspection Agency, the
Defense Technology Security Administration, and elements of the Office of the Secretary of
Defense concerned with arms control programs management." The agency history's chronology
gives the same directive and date, and its epilogue records that Secretary of Defense
William S. Cohen formally established DTRA in a ceremony at Dulles International Airport,
that the first Director was Dr. Jay Davis reporting to USD(A&T), and that DTRA was
authorised 2,110 military and civilian personnel and a fiscal year 1999 budget authorisation
of $1.9 billion.

**The predecessor chain, from NARA.** Manhattan Engineer District 1942-47; AFSWP as an
interservice agency January to July 1947; AFSWP under the National Military Establishment
July 1947 to August 1949; AFSWP under DoD 1949-59; DASA 1959-71; DNA 1971-96; DSWA 1996-98;
plus OSIA 1988-98 and DTSA 1985-98.

**Where the records actually are.** RG 374 is 1943-73 and holds the AFSWP general records
1947-55, the AFSWP headquarters organisation records including Sandia Base, the AFSWP
special detachments assigned to Atomic Energy Commission facilities 1943-52, the joint task
force records for Crossroads and Greenhouse, and the general textual, motion picture and
still picture series. Its "Related Records" line is explicit: "Records of the Atomic Energy
Commission, RG 326." The record group note also states that RG 374 "may include material
that is security-classified."

**What DTRA says it holds from that era.** In its 1998 republication, DTRA lists fourteen
systems. Two reach back into the AEC and DNA period:

| System | What DTRA says is in it |
|---|---|
| HDTRA 010, Nuclear Test Participants | Military and DoD civilian participants of the US nuclear testing programs, the occupation forces at Hiroshima or Nagasaki from 6 August 1945 to 1 July 1946, and the Enewetak Atoll cleanup. Records are name, rank, service number, "dates and extent of test participation, exposure data, unit of assignment, medical data, and documentation relative to administrative claims or civil litigation." Authority includes the Atomic Energy Act of 1954, 42 U.S.C. 2013 and a "Tasking Memorandum from Office of the Secretary of Defense to the Director, Defense Nuclear Agency dated 28 Jan 78" |
| HDTRA 020, Human Radiation Research Review | Held at the "Radiation Experiments Command Center, Special Weapons Technology Directorate". Contains information abstracted from historical records, covering "human radiation experimentation conducted from 1944 to the present", transferred to DTRA on standing up from the OSD system DUSDA 05 |

The purpose stated for HDTRA 010 is preparation of the histories of nuclear test programs,
scientific and medical follow-up, and documentation for claims and litigation. The 2005
notice moves the system to the Nuclear Test Personnel Review Office at Fort Belvoir and adds
the Veterans Advisory Board on Dose Reconstruction as a routine user.

### What the transcript claimed that these documents do NOT establish

Gerb (batch 17, file 03, 0:43;33 to 0:44;22, repeated at 1:27;27 to 1:28;19), relaying Dylan
Borland's testimony, says "you could look at old Atomic Energy Commission records held
within DITRA, the Defense Threat Reduction Agency" and that such records concluded some UFOs
were of non-human origin. The lead map itself grades this "No title, number or date given; a
class of records, not a specific document."

Nothing opened supports it, and two things cut against it. First, the AEC's records are a
separate National Archives record group, RG 326, named as "related records" to RG 374
precisely because they are not in it. Second, DTRA's own published inventory of what it
holds from that era describes personnel, participation, exposure and experiment records, and
names its authority for holding them. Neither is a denial that some other body of records
exists; both are the answer to the question of what is documented, which is the only
question this pass can settle. The claim remains what the triage row called it, request
shaped, and the FOIA layer is where it belongs.

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED as an institution. The triage row's date is CONTRADICTED, and so is

### What the document establishes

**The date.** The Act's own approval line is "Approved August 1, 1946." The running head,
the marginal date caption and the approval line agree.

**Creation and placement.** Section 1: "there is hereby created and established in the
Office of the Secretary of the Navy an Office of Naval Research". Its duties are the
encouragement, promotion, planning, initiation and coordination of naval research; the
conduct of naval research in augmentation of and in conjunction with the bureaus; and the
supervision, administration and control of Navy activities relating to patents, inventions,
trade-marks, copyrights and royalty payments. All duties are performed under the authority
of the Secretary of the Navy, and its orders "shall be considered as emanating from him".

**Its head is Senate-confirmed.** Section 2: a Chief of Naval Research appointed by the
President by and with the advice and consent of the Senate, for a term not to exceed three
years, from officers not below the grade of commander on the active list.

**The Naval Research Advisory Committee.** Section 4 authorises a committee of not more than
fifteen civilians preeminent in science, research and development work, one of them from
medicine, to consult with and advise the Chief of Naval Operations and the Chief of the
Office of Naval Research, at $50 a day plus travel, with the conflict of interest statutes
disapplied to them by reason of that membership alone.

**Contracting freedoms.** Section 6 lets the Secretary, and by his direction the Chief of ONR
and the bureau chiefs, enter into research contracts "without performance or other bonds"
and without regard to five named sections of the Revised Statutes, and make advance and
progress payments without regard to another, with cost-plus-a-percentage-of-cost expressly
barred. Section 5(b) lets obligated appropriations stay available for expenditure for four
years after the fiscal year for which they were appropriated.

**What it absorbed.** Section 7 authorises the Secretary of the Navy to transfer research and
development functions from the bureaus to ONR together with personnel, buildings, facilities
and other property, "including without limitation the Special Devices Division and the Naval
Research Laboratory."

### What the triage row claimed that the document does NOT establish

The row states "Public Law 79-588, 3 August 1946, 60 Stat. 779" and argues that ONR was
"Created two days after the Atomic Energy Act of 1946 the board built this morning."

The public law number and the Statutes at Large page are right. The date is not. The Act was
approved 1 August 1946, the same day as the Atomic Energy Act of 1946, which the board
already carries as `atomic-energy-act-1946` with `dateStart` 1946-08-01. The two statutes
are 24 pages apart in the same volume, 60 Stat. 755 and 60 Stat. 779, and share an approval
date. The row's structural point survives and gets stronger; its arithmetic does not.

Gerb's own claim (batch 17, file 01, 0:25;01) is narrower than the row: that people should
"take a good hard look at the Office of Naval Intelligence, Office of Naval Research, some
of their historically special projects", and that the Navy is left out of the four-element
picture. The statute establishes the office, its Senate-confirmed head, its civilian
advisory committee and its exemption from ordinary contracting law. It says nothing about
special projects of any kind, and Gerb names no document.

### What the document establishes

**The parties.** "The following agreement is made between the State-Army-Navy Communication
Intelligence Board (STANCIB) (representing the U. S. State, Navy and War Departments and all
other U. S. Communication Intelligence authorities which may function) and the London Signal
Intelligence (SIGINT) Board (representing the Foreign Office, Admiralty, War Office, Air
Ministry, and all other British Empire Communication Intelligence authorities which may
function)." Read from the OCR; the departmental list is legible, the abbreviations are not
uniformly so.

**The scope.** The agreement "governs the relations of the above-mentioned parties in
Communication Intelligence matters only". Communication Intelligence is defined in a
footnote as all processes involved in the collection, production and dissemination of
information derived from the communications of other nations. Foreign communications are
defined as those of the government or any military, air or naval service, faction, party,
department, agency or bureau of a foreign country, and foreign country is defined to include
any country whether or not its government is recognised, "excluding only the U. S., the
British Commonwealth of Nations, and the British Empire."

**What is exchanged.** Paragraph 3(a) commits the parties to exchange the products of six
operations on foreign communications: collection of traffic, acquisition of communication
documents and equipment, traffic analysis, cryptanalysis, decryption and translation, and
acquisition of information regarding communication organisations, practices, procedures and
equipment. Paragraph 4 extends the exchange to methods and techniques. Both exchanges are
unrestricted except where a party gives notice of a withholding, and the text says each
party intends "to limit such exceptions to the absolute minimum."

**The secrecy clause.** Paragraph 5(a): "It will be contrary to this agreement to reveal its
existence to any third party whatsoever." Third parties are defined as all individuals or
authorities other than the United States, the British Empire and the British Dominions.
Paragraph 5(b) and (c) require each party to seek the other's agreement to any action with
third parties and take none until its advisability is agreed.

**The Dominions.** Paragraph 6: the Dominions are not parties to the agreement but "will not
be regarded as third parties." The London SIGINT Board is to keep the United States informed
of arrangements with Dominion agencies; STANCIB is to make no arrangement with any
non-Dominion agency other than Canadian except through or with the prior approval of the
London SIGINT Board, and as regards Canada is to complete no arrangement without first
obtaining the London Board's views.

**Commercial exploitation barred.** Paragraph 9 requires that no dissemination of information
derived from Communication Intelligence sources be made to any individual or agency,
governmental or otherwise, that will exploit it for commercial purposes, without prior
notification and consent of the other party in each instance.

**Supersession, termination and signature.** Paragraph 10: "This agreement supersedes all
previous agreements between British and U. S. authorities in the Communication Intelligence
field." Paragraph 11 allows amendment or cancellation by mutual agreement at any time and
termination by either party on notice "should either consider its interests best served by
such action." Paragraph 12 makes it effective on signature by duly authorised representatives
of the London SIGINT Board and STANCIB. It is signed at the foot for the London Signal
Intelligence Board by an officer described as "Colonel, British Army General Staff", whose
name the OCR does not render legibly, and for the State-Army-Navy Communication Intelligence
Board by Hoyt S. Vandenberg, Lieutenant General, GSC, Senior Member. It is dated 5 March 1946.
The pages carry the NSA declassification exemption marking "NSA25X6" with "EO 1.4.(d)".

### What the transcript and the triage row claimed that the document does NOT establish

**The document says nothing about anomalous objects, and that was the point of opening it.**
A case-insensitive search of the extracted text for "flying", "saucer", "unidentified",
"aerial phenomen" and "anomalous" returns zero hits in all five. This is the finding the
triage row itself predicted and it is worth carrying: the instrument is a communications
intelligence sharing agreement with a total secrecy clause and a defined exclusion of third
parties. It is a real, documented mechanism for multinational compartmented collection. It is
not evidence of coordination on recovery matters, and it does not mention the subject.

**The row's attribution is wrong twice.** It calls the agreement "the instrument behind
Gerb's S10 claim of Five Eyes coordination on recovery matters". S10 in the batch 16 map is
Ross Coulthart's, not Gerb's, and it is a claim about a single still-secret overseas facility
that Coulthart will describe only as a building outside the continental United States, not
about Five Eyes coordination. What Coulthart actually says about UKUSA is at 2:35:06 in that
file and is entirely about ECHELON and about how he came to write letters to sources: he
describes a communications surveillance operation "run by the Yakuza, UKUSA", the caption's
mangling of the name, and says he revealed ECHELON as an official programme about 25 years
earlier for Channel 9 Australia with Duncan Campbell. Five Eyes appears elsewhere in the file
as an interviewer's question about where the facility is, which Coulthart declines, and as
Coulthart's own statement that the intelligence agencies "serve as part of the five eyes" and
that he does not want to jeopardise that. No speaker in the opened passages claims UKUSA
coordination on recovery.

### What the documents establish

**The email that joins the two nodes.** Podesta Emails id 3099, subject "General McCasland",
sent by Tom DeLonge from `[DeLonge's personal address]` to `[Podesta's personal Gmail address]`. The raw source gives
`Date: Mon, 25 Jan 2016 10:04:29 -0800`, `Message-id: <E8428167-CF1B-4CC3-95D2-977A277CC5CB@me.com>`
and `X-Mailer: iPhone Mail (13D15)`. Its body in full, apart from the signature block, is
three short paragraphs. It opens by disputing a description of McCasland as a sceptic, says

a very important man."

whom the calendar invitation renders as "Neil McCasland". The thread is copied to
`[DeLonge's personal address]`, `[a Clinton campaign staff address]`, `[Weiss's Lockheed Martin address]` and

**The release window.** The five McCasland hits carry WikiLeaks release dates of 10 October,
14 October, 26 October and 6 November 2016; the thirteen emails sent from `[DeLonge's personal address]`
carry release dates from 10 October to 8 November 2016. The triage row's "October to
November 2016" is right.

**The name.** The transcript renders it "McCaslin" and the lead map marks the surname
UNRESOLVED, warning against guessing a fix. The release settles the spelling: McCasland, and
the correspondent uses the first name Neil. The board's node label is already "William Neil
McCasland" and its Tier 1 Air Force biography claim names Maj. Gen. William N. McCasland as
Commander, Air Force Research Laboratory, Wright-Patterson Air Force Base. The identification
is a good one, but it is an inference from a first name and a surname and it is made here,
not by the email, which never gives a rank, a service or a middle name.
