## 3. STRUCTURAL CLAIMS

Numbered so the board can cite them. All are the **named speaker's claims**, several explicitly
flagged by the speaker as inference or unsourced.

**S1 — AATIP's FOIA exemption via private-sector routing.** Michels' narration: because AATIP
ran "under the auspices of Bigelow Aerospace, a private company," its underlying data is "both
classified and not subject to the Freedom of Information Act." (1 / 0:01:43)

**S2 — Shift from public programmes to private special access programmes.** A statement made
in the episode, in context following Michels' visit and conversation with Elizondo (attribution
to Elizondo is contextual, not a direct on-camera quote in this cut): official UFO tracking and
remote-viewing work has not gone dark or ceased but has "only been shifted into the private
sector into the special access programs," with aerospace corporations' UAP-relevant knowledge
"sequestered against FOIA because of proprietary labels which the whole thing was set up to be
that way on purpose." Likened to a puzzle divided into pieces held by mutually unaware, budget-
competing "highly territorial factions." (1 / 0:17:10–0:18:10)

**S3 — If anti-gravity progress happened, it would be invisible by design.** Cook's own framing,
stated as his inference: "if we have made any progress on anti-gravity, but that progress got
turned into special access programs or was militarized, it might look like we haven't made any
progress... You'd never be able to tell the difference." (2 / 0:30:51)

**S4 — A parallel, less-public physics research thread underlies the public 1950s anti-gravity
enthusiasm.** Cook: the 1971 Australian memo names Oppenheimer and six institutions doing
gravity research "at the CIA's Office of Scientific Intelligence['s]" behest, i.e., a
government-sponsored research base beneath the trade-press anti-gravity boom the 1956 article
described. (2 / 0:29:11)

**S5 — Career-ending fear as circumstantial evidence.** George Trimble's abrupt, "scared"
refusal to be interviewed decades after the fact, and the warning passed through a Lockheed PR
contact never to approach him "not now, not ever," is presented by Cook as the moment that
converted a filed-away curiosity into a serious inquiry. No further explanation is offered or
available. (2 / 0:01:44–0:11:12)

**S6 — Aurora as a "gap-filler" black programme, not a permanent asset.** Gen. George Muellner's
account, as related by Cook: a capability gap between SR-71 retirement and a new generation of
digital reconnaissance satellites was closed with a small-batch "deployable prototype"
hypersonic aircraft, used only until satellite coverage resumed, then stood down. Cook treats
this as the most credible single account he has for Aurora's existence and purpose. (2 /
1:16:09–1:19:08)

**S7 — Deliberate ambiguity as institutional strategy ("unfunded opportunities").** Cook, citing
Ben Rich's own phrase for UFOs, frames Skunk Works and similar organisations as playing a
"double-edged sword" game with the press: neither confirming nor denying, alert to "possible
advantage" in any topic including parapsychology and remote viewing, careful never to be caught
in an outright lie because that would be self-limiting. (2 / 0:58:54–0:59:56)

**S8 — Corporate succession as a channel for public-facing UFO advocacy.** Skunk Works
directorship passed from Kelly Johnson to Ben Rich to (eventually) Robert Weiss, who then
became involved with Tom DeLonge's To The Stars Academy — presented by Michels as an open
question rather than a settled claim: "is there anything there?" (2 / 0:52:26–0:56:00)

**S9 — WWII-era compartmented special-weapons authority as a precedent structure.** Kammler's
Kammlerstab is described as a single office consolidating authority over the V-2, remote-control
aircraft, atomic energy, jet propulsion and (per rumour, not sourced) disc-shaped-craft
development — a precedent Cook treats as the shape later US compartmentalisation would take,
though he draws no explicit institutional line from Kammler's office to any named postwar US
programme in this transcript. (2 / 0:19:36)
