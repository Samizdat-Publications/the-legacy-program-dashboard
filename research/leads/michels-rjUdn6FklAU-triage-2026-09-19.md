## 4. THE CENTRAL QUESTION: a second witness produced by an interested party

`CLAUDE.md` §2: *two agents agreeing is not corroboration when they share a method.* The
same objection applies here in a stronger form, because the two witnesses did not merely
share a method — one of them was located, briefed and reunited by the party publishing the
result. The four sub-questions are answered separately below, as findings with timestamps.
**No verdict is offered. §5 states what would settle it.**

### a. Who found him, and how

**The interviewer's own organisation found him.** Not the original witness, not a neutral
third party, not a records search.

- [00:02:35] Woods is said to have hired private investigators, consulted the FBI, and been
  told by AARO that it would look. All are said to have come up dry. ~$10,000 is claimed
  spent on the search [00:09:49].
- [00:03:06]–[00:03:16] The narration states plainly that "all it took for our fledgling
  operation at American Alchemy to beat a multi-agency manhunt was some online searching
  and basic outreach."
- [00:03:16]–[00:03:31] The route is specific and checkable: **a viewer of the show**
  (captions: "Jason Miller") sent the production **a Facebook page** belonging to a
  70-something Michael Johnson in **South Carolina**.
- [00:03:40]–[00:03:45] Michels then "tasked a very talented recruiter to find him and he
  did, in about a week." The recruiter is not named.
- [00:05:19] The contact call is dated on air: **26 June 2026**.

**Finding:** the locating party is the publisher, acting on a tip from its own audience,
via a social-media profile, executed by an unnamed contractor. Every step of that chain is
in principle documentable, and none of it is documented in the episode.

### b. What he had said, if anything, BEFORE being found

**This is the weakest link in the chain, and the episode does not strengthen it.**

- The only pre-contact artefact the episode identifies is a **Facebook post** by Johnson,
  quoted at [00:03:34]–[00:03:40] as words to the effect of "my boys in South Dakota on
  Ellsworth 75 to 77". **That post places him at the base in the right years. It says
  nothing whatever about the event.** Its date is not given on air.
- Johnson asserts *private* prior telling: at [00:08:44]–[00:08:51] he says he remembers it
  well and tells people, who do not believe him; at [01:32:29] and [00:10:32] the same
  theme. **No dated, public, pre-contact statement by Johnson about the November-5 event is
  identified anywhere in 2 hours 47 minutes.**
- Woods, by contrast, has been telling the account publicly **since 1996** [00:20:48], with
  the episode claiming over 40 media appearances [00:01:46]–[00:01:54], including History
  Channel, Discovery+ and NewsNation — and in those appearances he repeatedly named Michael
  Johnson on air and asked audiences to find him [00:01:54]–[00:02:01].

**Finding:** the asymmetry is total. One account is 30 years old and heavily published under
the witness's own name and the second witness's name; the other account did not exist in
public form before 26 June 2026. The absence of any pre-contact Johnson statement about the
event is the single most important fact in this file, and it is recorded here as an absence,
not as a disqualification.

### c. What he was told before he spoke

**Both things are true, and they must be held together.** The interviewer withheld the
frame. The original witness supplied it within ninety seconds, on the recording.

- **Withheld by the interviewer.** [00:05:24]–[00:05:41]: Michels states that he called
  Johnson at work and explained only part of his motivation — "Nothing about November 5.
  Nothing about a UFO," and that Johnson did not know his name or that he had a podcast.
  [00:05:46] Michels's pretext on the call is that a friend stationed with him at Ellsworth
  wanted to reconnect. **This is a deliberate blind-contact protocol and it should be
  credited as such.**
- **Supplied immediately by the original witness.** Woods is patched in at [00:06:40] and
  within the next two minutes supplies, unprompted: the site and date, "November 5, November
  the 18th, 1977" [00:07:05]; that Johnson was working vacation relief and did not know the
  68th area [00:07:11]; and then the following exchange, which is the pivotal passage in the
  episode:

  - [00:07:29] Woods asks whether Johnson remembers what they encountered at 01:00 on
    November 5. **Johnson answers "No."** [00:07:35]
  - [00:07:35] Woods then supplies the category himself, twice — asking whether it was a UFO.
  - After a pause, [00:07:46] Johnson assents: he remembers it.
  - [00:07:47] Woods asks how large it was; Johnson then gives "big" and "white".
  - [00:08:51] Woods asks **how far over the top of the "blaster" it was** — supplying the
    frame of an object above the site — and Johnson gives "under 50 feet. Maybe a little
    higher."

- **The on-camera retelling of that moment diverges from the recording of it.** At

  Michels then characterises it explicitly — that Woods "hadn't said anything else, no other

  [00:07:29]–[00:07:46] does not show that. It shows an initial "No", followed by Woods
  naming the UFO, followed by assent.** This divergence is internal to the episode, is
  timestamped on both sides, and is the highest-value observation in this file.

- **Leading continues through the studio interview.** [00:40:47] Woods asks whether Johnson
  was taken to "Colonel Spreaker's office" or to medical. [00:46:31] Michels asks Johnson
  directly whether he remembers "a tall man in a tweed suit"; Johnson says yes; Michels
  confirms "you do." The tweed-suited civilian is a fixture of **Woods's** long-told account
  — the narration itself introduces him at [00:52:27] as the man "nobody ever names". The
  detail was put to the new witness as a yes/no question rather than elicited open-ended.
  [00:46:49] the follow-up is "fat or skinny", again closed.

- **The hypnotic induction supplies place, date and expectation.** Johnson is regressed by

  remember". At [00:59:00]–[00:59:09] the subject is directed "back to the winter of 1977"

- **Ordering.** Johnson gives his waking "first memories" at [00:18:44]–[00:20:40], i.e.
  *before* Woods narrates the full account on camera (which begins [00:20:44]) — but
  *after* the 26 June phone call. Johnson's regression is at [00:57:51], i.e. *after* some
  37 minutes of Woods's on-camera narration in the same room. **The studio is not the
  contamination point. The phone call and Woods's thirty years of published accounts are.**

### d. Where the two accounts agree and where they diverge

- **The episode's corroboration claim is narrow and explicit.** At [01:17:08]–[01:17:27] the
  narration rests it on three words — a "do not fear" instruction — asserting "two men, two
  rooms, hours apart, neither in earshot of the other", and that Woods has attached those
  words to his memory since 1977.
- **Johnson produced that phrase in waking conversation at [00:20:26]**, before his
  regression and before Woods's full on-camera narration. That ordering is better for the
  claim than the narration's own framing suggests, and should be checked against the audio.
- **Against it:** the phrase is a staple of the wider abduction literature and, more
  pointedly, of Woods's own published accounts since 1996, which named Johnson and were
  addressed to finding him. The episode does not establish that Johnson had not encountered
  them. It also does not establish that the phrase went unmentioned in any contact between
  26 June and the Austin filming.
- **Genuine divergence on detail, which is the more interesting half.** Johnson's account is
  a *passive* one throughout: frozen to the steering wheel, unable to speak, hearing Woods
  shouting, eyes burning, unable to blink, fading out and back [00:18:44]–[00:20:07]. Woods's
  is an *active* one: out of the truck, calling Johnson to come and look [00:22:56], engaged
  with the object. They agree on substance (an object above the site, figures, missing time,
  separate interrogation) and diverge on posture and agency. **That reads differently from
  verbatim agreement and the board should see it as such.**
- Johnson's beings are "human form" with a "pointed head" [00:20:13]–[00:20:19]. Under
  regression his object becomes "a big building" / "a warehouse building" [01:00:04]. The
  narration's "massive glowing red orb" [00:55:23] matches neither of Johnson's waking
  descriptions ("big", "white", [00:07:52]). **Narration and witness do not agree on the
  object, and the narration is the production's voice, not a witness's.**
- One asserted physical sequela: Johnson's right arm is said to be weaker than his left
  despite being dominant [00:45:52]. Michels argues on air at [00:46:01] that this is the
  kind of detail "nobody would ever bother to invent, especially somebody that we had to
  seek out." **That is the interested party's own corroboration argument. The board should
  record that it was made; it must not adopt it.**
- **Material interest established after contact:** the production launched a **GoFundMe on
  the two men's behalf** for their retirement [02:37:05]–[02:37:24], and the episode is
  commercially sponsored. Recorded plainly, as the board already records for the outlet
  generally: a real analytic caveat, not a disqualifier.

## 5. What would settle it

Not offered as a verdict. These are the records that would convert the question from
testimony into documents.

1. **Johnson's presence at Ellsworth on the night, from service records.** His Official
   Military Personnel File at the National Personnel Records Center, and his **DD-214** —
   which would carry his separation date, the reenlistment code he describes as blocking
   re-entry [00:53:40]–[00:53:45], and his unit. He states on air that he received an
   **Article 15** (nonjudicial punishment) before an honorable discharge [00:53:19]. An
   Article 15 generates a paper record with a date. **That record, if it exists and is dated
   within months of 18 November 1977, is the strongest available independent anchor for his
   presence and for the claimed aftermath, and it is requestable by the veteran himself.**
2. **The duty roster.** The 44th Missile Security Squadron / 44th Strategic Missile Wing
   assignment record placing a two-man Security Alert Team at Launch Facility November-5 on
   the night of 17–18 November 1977, and placing Johnson on it as vacation relief. Unit
   histories for the 44th SMW are held at AFHRA.
3. **"Steve Welsh".** The team member whose marriage leave created the vacancy [00:21:49].
   A third party, independently locatable, whose leave dates are a documentary fact and
   would corroborate the substitution that puts Johnson at that site that night.

**Ranked verification candidates, beyond the three above:**

4. **The L9 hoax document and its provenance** [01:51:46]–[01:55:54]. The episode asserts a
   fabricated Air Force document describing an alien attack at **site L9**, **16 November
   1977, 20:59**, naming "A1C Jenkins" and "A1C Raiki", with a disintegrated M16, missing
   nuclear warhead components, and two identical social security number endings on page two.
   It attributes the forgery to **Richard C. Doty** via **Bill Moore**, reported by
   **Bruce Maccabee**, with radiation-figure analysis credited to **Brad Sparks**. Highest
   document density in the episode and it touches `richard-doty`, already on the board.
   Note the episode's own date arithmetic is loose: it calls 16 November "a day before" an
   18 November event.
5. **Doty's presence at Woods's debrief.** Already a Tier 2 board claim from the 5 July 2025
   episode. This episode restates it at [01:55:50] and adds that Doty later appeared on a
   podcast with Woods and walked details back [01:57:05]–[01:57:16]. That podcast is a dated
   artefact and should be identified.
6. **The pre-contact Facebook post** [00:03:34]. If it can be dated and captured, it is the
   only pre-contact Johnson artefact in existence. It does not corroborate the event, but it
   does corroborate the base and the years, and it is the hinge of the whole discovery story.
7. **AARO's claimed search for Johnson** [00:02:40]–[00:02:47]. Woods says AARO promised to
   look. If any tasking exists, it is a record. Tier 2 cap applies: AARO is an interested
   party under `CLAUDE.md` §2.
8. **Named base personnel** for the 44th in late 1977: "Colonel Spreer/Spreaker",
   "Master Sergeant Gray", "Sergeant Garza". Unit rosters would confirm or refute the
   spellings and the billets.
9. **The Malmstrom / Big Sur / Vandenberg block** [01:36:32]–[01:38:30], which restates
   Malmstrom 1967 and Salas, both already on the board. Enrichment only — and note the
   narration applies the identical "two men in two different rooms" formula to Malmstrom at

## 6. What NOT to take from this

- **Nothing produced under hypnosis.** The regression content ([00:57:51] onward for
  Johnson, [01:05:11] onward for Woods) is not evidence of events. It is evidence that a
  regression was conducted and recorded on 2026-09-07's episode. The induction supplied
  date, season and setting before the subject spoke.
- **Nothing that rests on the "nobody would invent that" argument** [00:46:01]. That is
  advocacy by the publishing party.
- **No caption spelling.** "Dodie", "Mcabe", "Salis", "Ivonne", "Raiki", "Spreaker",
  "Lacklin", "Bar", "Floren Mansman", "personal liability program" are all machine
  renderings. Several are demonstrably wrong against the video's own description. Treat
  "Jason Miller" (the tipster) and "Steve Welsh" as **too mangled to trust** until heard.
- **Speaker attribution in the captions is unreliable.** The auto-track does not reliably
  separate Woods, Johnson, Michels and Kandil; the `>>` markers move mid-answer. Attributing
  any line to a specific man requires the audio.
- **The narration's object description.** "Massive glowing red orb" is the production's
  language and conflicts with the witnesses' own waking descriptions.
- **Any of the duration figures** (nine hours / five hours / five and a half) and any of the
  elapsed-year figures (50 / 49 / 48.5) until one is pinned to the audio.
- **The episode's identification of the second witness as established fact.** Johnson
  himself raises the problem on the call — asking whether they have the right Michael Johnson
  [00:09:24]–[00:09:33], [00:17:39]. The episode treats the recognition as settling it. It
  is not settled by anything in the episode; it is settled by item 1 above.
