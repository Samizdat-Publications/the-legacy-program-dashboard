## 3. STRUCTURAL CLAIMS — compartmentalisation, funding, custody, oversight

Numbered so the board can cite them. All are the speaker's claims, several explicitly their
own stated inference.

**S1 — Funding through sole-source and IDIQ contracts, with an untraceable margin.** Gerb's
account: private contractors, FFRDCs and UARCs are funded through sole-source and IDIQ
contracts; he separately alleges siphoned or unaccounted funds (citing the 1980s Northrop
Grumman F-14 ashtray overbilling as a historical precedent for defense-contractor fraud, not
a UFO claim itself) and says some programme funding is "literally untraceable." (09-3, 0:06:04–0:08:38)

**S2 — Civilian, not military, custody of the legacy programme.** The Wilson–Davis notes, per
Gerb, showed the programme's bigot list was mostly civilian contractor names rather than
military or political ones — consistent with the equivalent claim already logged from the
existing gerb corpus (S4 there). (09-3, 0:14:00–0:14:56)

**S3 — Restructuring after a near-exposure event.** Gerb repeats the claim that a 1990s
Pentagon audit nearly outed the programme, after which it was restructured under SAPOC's
Senior Review Group, first under William J. Perry and then John M. Deutch. (09-3, 0:13:04)

**S4 — FFRDCs and UARCs as the deliberate custody layer.** Gerb's structural argument: FFRDCs
(MITRE, Battelle-run labs) and UARCs (e.g. Georgia Tech) are "not contractors, not
universities" — semi-private institutions the government can "keep their hands on" while
avoiding direct oversight; AARO itself told the Senate it works with FFRDCs/UARCs it "can't
name." (09-3, 0:52:45–0:53:37)

**S5 — Minimal congressional read-in, correlated with MITRE/SAIC ties.** Gerb: "encourage
people to take a long hard look at who in Congress has been on the MITRE payroll specifically
[and] SAIC." No names given. (09-3, 0:15:50)

**S6 — Deliberately small "bigot lists," with cross-utilisation of personnel.** Programmes
keep their read-in lists small by having the same few cleared scientists perform multiple
roles (e.g. a physicist also doing basic accounting), and industrial-security personnel
handling menial tasks instead; cleared engineers are said to rotate among Lockheed, Raytheon
and Northrop Grumman through shared shell-company arrangements with EG&G and others tied to
DOE work. (09-3, 1:33:19–1:35:02)

**S7 — Deliberate technology transfer to industry as a funding-avoidance mechanism.**
Attributed to Philip Corso: recovered-craft-derived technology is said to be used to
strengthen military capability and then handed to American industry (citing the Sperry Rand
divestment) so the government need not carry the R&D cost directly, echoing an alleged CIA
practice of releasing technology to industry to drive down price before buying it back. (09-3, 1:35:57–1:36:47)

**S8 — A very small "full picture" population.** Gerb estimates fewer than 30 people alive
have a full cross-service (Army/Navy/Air Force) picture of UAP-related USAPs; cites James
Fox's figure of 22 and an unspecified Bob Lazar-era figure of 20, alongside the traditional
"Majestic 12" figure of 12. Neither the president nor most elected officials are briefed,
per Gerb, because they are "temporary employees." (09-3, 1:37:42–1:38:37)

**S9 — Executive carve-outs pre-dating SAP oversight.** Gerb links Eisenhower-era "presidential

farewell speech to programmes he may himself have helped create. (09-3, 1:38:37–1:39:21)

**S10 — Confusion as a deliberate operating method.** Gerb's explicit thesis: "confusion is a
