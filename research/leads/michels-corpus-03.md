## 3. STRUCTURAL CLAIMS — compartmentalisation, funding, custody, oversight

Numbered so the board can cite them. All are the **named speaker's claims**.

**S1 — SAP taxonomy is contested even among practitioners.** Davis: there are "regular SAPs," waived SAPs, acknowledged, and waived-unacknowledged, but he is "not sure" the middle two are truly separate categories; he and Grusch independently concluded a "hidden SCI" is functionally indistinguishable from a SAP. (04 / 0:13:19–0:15:56)

**S2 — Stovepipe compartmentalisation prevents cross-service coordination, and this is a named, sourced complaint.** Davis: access to another compartmented programme must be justified on a DD-254 and usually is not, "because programs are not don't really overlap." He credits **Bill Sweetman**'s 1980s reporting (post-Reagan) with correctly identifying that this causes duplicated, unable-to-communicate programmes spending taxpayer money twice on the same problem. (04 / 0:16:48–0:17:40)

**S3 — SAP-level contracting bypasses competitive procurement by design.** Davis: no RFP or Broad Area Announcement is issued for SAP work, because doing so would expose the programme's specs to uncleared bidders. Instead the programme manager calls a known contractor directly. Payment is typically routed through a pass-through/front company, though Davis says this does not prevent contract oversight, since both parties know their real counterpart. Only two contract vehicles are used, per Davis: cost-plus-fee or firm-fixed-price — "nothing special just because of UAPs." (04 / 0:27:03–0:31:24)

**S4 — An IRAD-inflation allegation, attributed to Grusch and repeated by Elizondo, is one Davis says he has no independent insight on.** The claim as relayed: a contractor allegedly inflated a black-budget proposal so surplus profit could be diverted into Independent Research and Development spending. Davis declines to confirm or deny. (04 / 0:29:48)

**S5 — Davis states flatly, from a classified-level vantage, that no ARV (alien reproduction vehicle) exists and none has ever been built.** He says the CIA "shut it all down" in 1989 after decades without progress, and that Northrop Grumman/Area-51 ARV rumours are "all myth," originating with Bob Lazar-type storytellers "who believe their own bullshit" rather than disinformation planted deliberately. (04 / 0:36:24–0:38:00)

**S6 — Davis's own assessment of adversary crash-retrieval programmes is qualified and source-layered.** He says Russia had "at least one" crash retrieval, corroborated two ways: (a) George Knapp's post-Soviet KGB-file reporting, and (b) an independent classified source of Davis's own, which he says he cannot detail further; his personal exposure to the Russian material dates "to the end of the 80s." He says China is believed (via a Puthoff/DIA channel) to have "at least one" as well but offers no independent verification, and notes that neither has publicly or observably deployed anything beyond conventional weapons — citing the absence of any such reported capability in the Ukraine war as evidence against operational adversary ARVs. (04 / 0:39:38–0:41:27)

**S7 — The George H.W. Bush anecdote is presented as a first-hand account from Davis's own 2003 conversation with Bush**, not sourced to a document. Bush was briefed as incoming CIA director (1976) on a decades-old event via a Pentagon liaison officer who assumed, wrongly, that Bush's CIA leadership had already briefed him; Bush's request to see the evidence (a film plus documentation) was declined on "no need to know" grounds, the material being characterised as DOD rather than CIA intelligence. Davis explicitly separates this from his presidency (1989–93): "when he became president, he didn't hear about it anymore." (04 / 0:23:38–0:27:03)

**S8 — AARO is structurally incapable of declassifying on its own, per Graves, and the UAP Disclosure Act's review-panel mechanism was specifically designed to supply that missing authority — then removed from the first version of the bill.** (03 / 1:04:59–1:05:50)

**S9 — The Oceania radar-to-sensor corroboration sequence is the most detailed first-hand technical account in this batch.** Objects were first dismissed as false tracks from the new APG-79 AESA radar, then cross-corroborated by FLIR (IR return with no visible exhaust plume) and AIM-9X missile lock, at which point Graves's squadron treated them as physical objects requiring a safety report. A near mid-air collision (~50 ft) followed; the object was described as a dark grey/black cube inside a clear sphere, 5–15 ft diameter. Four objects were separately reported flying in a fixed "line abreast" formation about a mile apart, which Graves reads as implying some form of coordinated, likely networked, flight control. (03 / 0:12:15–0:19:30, 1:17:13)

**S10 — A NASA contact's "red-teaming our systems" theory is explicitly rejected by Graves on security-logic grounds:** exposing SAP-level technology to undocumented near-collisions with uncleared pilots would itself be "a massive security flaw," which he finds inconsistent with any legitimate red-team rationale. (03 / 0:23:04–0:24:39)
