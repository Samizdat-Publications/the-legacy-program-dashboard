## 4. STRUCTURAL CLAIMS

Numbered so the board can cite them. All are the **speaker's own claims**, several explicitly
flagged by the speaker as inference or hearsay rather than sourced fact.

**S1 — Multi-agency, multi-national contact list (Bledsoe).** Bledsoe answers a rapid "name an
agency" game from the host: yes to CIA, DIA, DoD, NASA, England's Ministry of Defence and
Australia's DoD, with two further agency names too corrupted to record (§5). He escalates the
seniority claim unprompted: not "some average low-level guy," but a two-star DIA general and a
Brigadier-General-rank CIA official, present as family witnesses. No document, date or contact
route is given for any of it. (01 / 1:18:57–1:19:48)

**S2 — A meta-material chain of custody ending in confiscation (Bledsoe).** Two independent
metal samples (his own find, and a friend's 15-year-old shoebox find) were flown to NASA
contacts, split with DeLonge and Elizondo, and approximately a year later declared classified
and taken back by the military — with an unspecified item, and later a triangle pin, given in
return. Bledsoe frames the pin as membership in an informal club of witnesses "elected" for
having interacted with the phenomenon while awake. No custody document, agency name or return
receipt is given. (01 / 1:14:21–1:18:57)

**S3 — Stargate's institutional footprint, per GSP.** Framed as established fact rather than
his own inference: 17 of 19 US intelligence agencies used the CIA-run Stargate programme at
SRI at least once; a joint task force alone re-tasked it 141 times over drug interdiction; a
named predecessor lineage (Center Lane, Grill Flame, Sun Streak) existed before Stargate under
the same "psionic programs" umbrella; and a general (almost certainly Stubblebine) was
retired out for excessive enthusiasm for the programme. GSP's own framing: he learned this from
a year of his own research and from named researchers, not from documents he can produce.
(02 / 1:53:15–2:07:09)

**S4 — Feedback-loop blindness as designed opacity.** GSP, again attributed to what he says he
was told: Stargate remote viewers were deliberately never shown their targets, because doing so
would let them "remote view" the target's later disclosure rather than the original event,
corrupting the data; the only signal a remote viewer had of success was whether the tasking
agency came back for another mission. Presented as a structural feature of how the programme
kept its own subjects blind to outcomes. (02 / 1:54:10–1:56:52)

**S5 — A stated reason for gatekeeping: weaponisation risk.** GSP: the reason remote
viewing/precognition capability is not made public is that it "could be used as a weapon" if
perfected and concentrated. He extends this into a broader claim that the government maintains
active "disinformation campaigns" specifically to discredit the *valid* psionic results, not
just the false ones. (02 / 2:00:29–2:01:18)

**S6 — Plausible deniability as the reason presidents are not briefed.** GSP, relaying what he
says he was told in Washington: sitting presidents are deliberately not briefed on the full
picture (with Bush Sr. as the last full exception, reasoned from his CIA background) so that if
pressed publicly, a president can truthfully claim ignorance — plausible deniability treated as
a designed feature, not an accident. (02 / 2:02:08–2:02:58)

**S7 — PSYOP's own tradecraft, demonstrated on UAP coverage as a live example (Isler).** Isler,
asked to construct a hypothetical PSYOP response to the 2023–24 drone/UAP flap: flood the
information space with mundane explanations (drones, plasma waves) precisely because each has
"a scientific foundation," which keeps the public confused and therefore unable to unify around
a demand for answers — "the larger motion hides the smaller motion." He is explicit this is a
hypothetical he is constructing, not a claim of an actual operation he ran or witnessed. (04 /
0:25:31–0:28:46)

**S8 — The domestic-use ban and its evasion.** Isler: PSYOP doctrine restricts the discipline to
foreign target audiences by law (see D4, Smith-Mundt), but personnel who leave the service carry
the same techniques into marketing and political-strategy work under other names —
"information operations," "marketing" — which he frames as the ban being real on paper and
routinely circumvented in practice. (04 / 0:38:36–0:39:27)

**S9 — Isler's own read on Elizondo.** Isler says he directly asked Lue Elizondo whether it was
possible Elizondo was himself being used by a government agency for its own agenda; Elizondo's
answer, as Isler reports it, was that it was possible — he knows the tradecraft and has not seen
a personal tell, but does not rule it out. Recorded as reported speech about a named third
party, not as Elizondo's own statement to this show. (04 / 0:18:59–0:19:46)
