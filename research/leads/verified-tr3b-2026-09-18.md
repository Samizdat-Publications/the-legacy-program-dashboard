**The seven findings that matter, in order:**

1. **The Library of Congress catalogues *Alien Rapture: The Chosen* as science fiction.** LCCN
   98002961, gsafd genre "Science fiction.", LCSH subject "Alien abduction - Fiction", LC class
   PS3556.O779 A79 1998 (American fiction), Dewey 813/.54. This is a Tier 1 cataloguing record
   from a primary bibliographic authority, created 4 February 1998 by the Library of Congress
   itself (`recordContentSource` DLC). The entire TR-3B narrative enters the world attached to a
   book that its own national library shelves as a novel.
2. **The presentation contradicts itself about the size of the craft, two slides apart.** Slide
   69: "The operational model is 600 feet across." Slide 70: "The 200 feet Prototype & the 300
   feet Operational TR-3Bs are Code named Astra." Both sentences are in the same document, in
   sequence. Nobody has to adjudicate this from outside; the artefact does it to itself.
3. **The mercury plasma figure is refuted by the freezing point of mercury.** Fouche's slide 72
   specifies plasma "pressurized at 250,000 atmospheres at a temperature of 150 degrees Kelvin".
   The NIST Chemistry WebBook gives mercury Tfus = 234.31 K and Tboil = 629.81 K. 150 K is 84 K
   below the melting point at one atmosphere, and pressure of that order raises a melting point
   rather than lowering it. The stated material at the stated temperature is a solid, not a
   plasma, before any question of superconductivity or gravity arises.
4. **One document does put him at the right place, and it is the best thing in the collection.**
   AF Form 77a of 24 August 1979 is a Letter of Evaluation on Air Force letterhead, signed by
   MSgt Brewer of 57 CRS, Nellis AFB Nevada, recording a temporary duty assignment to
   "AFFTC-DET 3, Nellis AF Range" from 1 June to 14 August 1979. Peter Merlin, the same
   historian whose analysis demolishes most of the rest of Fouche's account, writes on his own
   site that "Since the late 1970s, the Groom Lake facility has been operated by Detachment 3 of
   the Air Force Flight Test Center (DET 3 AFFTC)". The sceptic's own reference work is what
   makes Fouche's single best document mean something.
5. **And that document says nothing about what he did there.** Its own words: "His duties and
   responsibilities for this period have been verified via a separate report." The separate
   report is not in the fifteen pages. The rest of the same form describes F-15 avionics
   intermediate shop work, the TEWS-TITE bed-down, and cryptological asset training. A documented
   ten weeks of temporary duty at a documented secret airfield is a real and interesting fact,
   and it is not a claim about a craft.
6. **The "SAC/DARPA Classified Document" is not classified and probably not DARPA.** The opened
   page image shows no classification marking anywhere. It is signed "DALE E. HAGERMAN, TSgt,
   USAF, NCOIC, Amn Acft Maint Asgns Sec, Dir of Asgns, DCS/Pers", that is, the Airman Aircraft
   Maintenance Assignments Section of the Directorate of Assignments in the Deputy Chief of
   Staff for Personnel. Merlin's reading is that "REPLY TO: DARPA" is a five letter office code
   in the same personnel directorate, and that AAR is an After Action Review. Fouche's own
   caption misnames the agency as "Defense Advanced Research Agency". Every redaction on the
   page was applied by Fouche, which is stated in his own caption, so the document as published
   cannot be checked at precisely the points where he says the meaning lies.
7. **"SR-75 Penetrator" is the name of a model kit that went on sale five years before the
   presentation.** The *Baltimore Sun* of 25 December 1993 reports Testor Corp. releasing "the
   SR-75 Penetrator and XR-7 Thunderdart", designed by "John Andrews, an aviation historian in
   San Diego who designed the new models, as well as the F-19", and quotes Capt. Mary Dillon,
   Air Force spokeswoman: "We're not saying no comment, we're saying such a plane does not
   exist." Fouche's 1998 presentation devotes slides 62 onward to an "SR-75" "Penetrator" and an
   "SR-74 Scramp", presented as things he knows about from inside. This is the clearest
   available route by which commercially published speculation could have entered the account,
   and it is documented on both ends.

### 7.3 The TACIT BLUE conflation the brief asked about: NOT SUPPORTED

The brief asked me to look for "the widely-reported analysis that the TR-3B narrative may derive
from or be confused with the (real, documented) Northrop TACIT BLUE". **I did not find it, and the
primary record argues against it.**

TACIT BLUE's defining feature in the USAF's own fact sheet is *curved* surfaces; its planform is
a slab-sided, round-topped body with a straight wing and a V-tail, 48 feet across and 55 feet
long. It is not triangular, it flew 135 times over three years ending in 1985, and it has been on
public display since 1996. Fouche's craft is triangular, is said to be 200 to 600 feet across, and
is dated to the early 1990s. There is no shape, size or date correspondence, and no source
asserting one was opened.

The documented aircraft that actually is close to triangular in planform, and that actually did
fly out of Groom Lake in the period Fouche describes, is the F-117, which is the airframe Merlin
identifies in Fouche's own misattributed photograph at claim 48. That is the conflation the record
supports, and it is Merlin's, not mine.

**I am recording this against the brief rather than manufacturing a finding to fill the slot.**
