## 2. THE BLOCKER. This needs Stewart, and it is not a data question

**`test_packed_layout.js` is still red, and option 1 is now disproven by measurement.**

The failing half is `fitChipInk`, which requires `cellInkCells * 2 < cellInkChips`. Both
figures are class-coloured ink per ten thousand sampled canvas pixels, one with chips
drawn and one with cell mode forced.

| build | L0 | chip ink | cell ink | passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| master, before any of this | 137 | 25 | 9 | 18 < 25 yes |
| + ARV cluster | 139 | 30 | 16 | 32 < 30 **no** |
| + TR-3B cluster | 140 | **31** | 16 | 32 < 31 **no** |
| + Lazar cluster | 142 | **31** | 16 | 32 < 31 **no** |

**`notes/ARV-BUILD-2026-09-18.md` section 4 misdiagnosed the cause.** It said the
`witness x program` cell holding a single node was the problem and that filling it would
"stop it being a single numeral". Filling it does not remove the numeral. TR-3B joined
that cell, the cell count did not change, and cell ink stayed at exactly 16.

**And adding nodes no longer buys chip ink at all.** The Lazar cluster added two L0 nodes
and moved chip ink by zero. The likely mechanism is that the map packs into a fixed
extent, so more nodes means proportionally smaller chips and roughly constant total ink.
Whatever the cause, three data points say the same thing: **you cannot reach the threshold
of 33 by building more.**

So the remaining options are the ARV note's 2 and 3, and both are Stewart's or Design's:

**The check was not touched and must not be.** It carries a poison and it exists because
the regression it guards shipped to Stewart twice. The load-bearing half still PASSES: 8
of 8 fits draw chips and nobody is landed in the count matrix. Only the corroborating
ratio fails.

**The deeper finding, which outlives this check.** A new band-and-class cell costs about
+7 on the cell side; a chip buys about +1, and now zero. The hub rule exists to put new
KINDS of object on the board, and every first-of-kind opens a new cell. **The check is
structurally opposed to the modelling rule Stewart adopted on 18 September.** That is the
thing to put in front of Design, not the single failing number.

### 4a. A second scoring defect, same class as the one fixed this morning

**`tr-3b` is Tier A, and 30% of its score is the case against it.**

| node | score | from `denial` edges |
|---|---|---|
| `tr-3b` | 54 | **16 pts, 4 edges** |
| `arv-fluxliner` | 37 | 4 pts, 1 edge |

`denial` edges are weighted x4.0, and `regrade.py`'s own comment says why: "an entity a
government body bothered to deny is more interesting rather than less". **None of TR-3B's
four deniers is a government body.** They are an aerospace historian, a model kit, a
control-case aircraft and a misattribution. So the x4.0 weight meant for official denial
is paying out for scholarly refutation, and the better-debunked a claim is, the higher it
ranks.

This is structurally identical to `witness-chain` sitting in the x3.0 program set. **It was
NOT fixed**, deliberately: a second unmeasured scoring change in one session is exactly
what `notes/CENTRALITY-2026-09-18.md` section 4 argues against, and it would move tiers
across all 192 denial edges. The board already carries the material for a fix, since denial
edges have `denialKind` and `denier` fields that could separate official denial from
analysis. **Measure both before changing either**, the same way the witness-chain question
was handled.
