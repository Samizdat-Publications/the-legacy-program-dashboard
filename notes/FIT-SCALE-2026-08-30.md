# The packed map's arrival scale, measured — 30 August 2026

**This resolves open decision 1 in Claude Design's response of 30 August**, which was blocked on
whether the packed map opens at 0.59× or 0.74× on a 1920 window.

**Both numbers are real. Neither is the 1920 figure and the 0.59 was mine, misattributed.**

---

## 1. What was wrong

`notes/MAP-INVERSION-2026-08-29.md` §7 says the packed map "opens fitted at 0.59x". That figure
was measured at **1600×1000** — the window `runtime_check.js` uses for its screenshot pass, which
is where the first capture of the inverted board came from. It then travelled into both Design
briefs attached to the words *"at 1920"*, while the screenshot sent alongside it was a genuine
1920 capture showing **FIT 0.74×**. The brief contradicted its own attachment, and Design caught
it rather than building against it.

The rule this breaks is the file's own opening line: **do not trust any figure in this repo,
derive it.** A scale is a function of the map's box, and the box is a function of the window,
the rail and the dock. Carrying one across window sizes is carrying a different measurement.

## 2. The measurement

Cold arrival on the PROGRAM tab, entry surface dismissed, no selection. `view.k` read from the
renderer, not from the button label.

| window | map box | fit at rest | FIT ALL | with dock open (640px) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1366×768 | 1160×484 | **0.453** | 0.45× | **0.19×** |
| 1600×1000 | 1368×749 | **0.587** | 0.67× | **0.29×** |
| 1920×1080 | 1688×829 | **0.743** | 0.83× | **0.45×** |
| 2560×1440 | 2328×1189 | **1.055** | 1.14× | **0.76×** |

So: **0.59 is 1600×1000. 0.74 is 1920×1080.** Design's instinct that they were "measurements of
different window states" was exactly right.

## 3. What the measurement then says about change 4

Design specified a warm arrival at `L0_LABEL_DROP` (0.62) rather than at fit, reasoning that a
selection opens the dossier, the dossier takes width, and the fit collapses. **The numbers are
worse than the argument assumed.**

`L0_LABEL_DROP = 0.62`. With a selection open, the fit falls **below** it at every window size
under 2560:

| window | fit with dock open | below 0.62? |
|---|---|---|
| 1366×768 | 0.19× | yes, by a factor of three |
| 1600×1000 | 0.29× | yes |
| 1920×1080 | 0.45× | yes |
| 2560×1440 | 0.76× | no |

A warm arrival that simply fitted would land a reader on **110 unlabelled rectangles on three of
four common window sizes**. Design's 0.62 floor is not a refinement; it is the difference between
the feature working and the feature being the defect it was written to avoid.

## 4. A finding Design's spec does not cover

**The COLD arrival is already below the label threshold at 1366 and 1600.** Measured by sampling
the chip field for label ink at the cold-arrival scale:

| window | fit k | label ink in the chip field |
|---|---|---|
| 1366×768 | 0.453 | **0 px** |
| 1600×1000 | 0.587 | **0 px** |
| 1920×1080 | 0.743 | present |
| 2560×1440 | 1.055 | present |

Design's change 4 lists the cold arrival as "fit to whole, **unchanged**". That is a reasonable
call about scope, but it is made against an assumption the numbers do not support: on a 1366
laptop the packed map's rest state today draws no chip labels at all. The reader gets the
arrangement — bands, eras, occupancy, the absent-link crosses — and no names.

**This is not a regression from the inversion.** The same fit applied when the packed map was the
NETWORK rest state; `CLAUDE.md` §9 records the 1920 case ("misses by 24px") and the cell-mode
split that followed. What has changed is that the map is now a *destination*, so a reader arrives
there deliberately, having asked for structure — which makes an unlabelled arrival a worse
outcome than it was when the map was simply what the board happened to open on.

**Not fixed here, and deliberately.** It is a design question, not a bug: either the cold arrival
also floors at 0.62 and gives up showing the whole map on small windows, or small windows keep
the whole map and lose the names. That trade is Design's to make and Stewart's to approve.

## 5. How this was measured, so it can be re-run

`view.k` is read from the live renderer via `window.__board.MAP.view.k` rather than from the FIT
button's label, because the button reports the scale it *would* apply and the two diverge the
moment the camera is moved by hand. Label presence is read with `getImageData` over the chip
field — right of the 148px gutter, below the head row — because the label-drop rule is a
rendering decision and reading the source would only tell us what the code intends.

Both probes are throwaway; the figures above are what matter. Re-derive rather than cite this
file, and if you re-run it, state the window size beside every scale you write down. That is the
whole lesson.
