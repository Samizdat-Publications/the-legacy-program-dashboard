# Somebody looked at the map

**26 August 2026.** `CLAUDE.md` §9 had one item above everything else: *the packed-cell
layout shipped and no human being has seen it rendered.* That is no longer true, and the
way it stopped being true is the more useful half of this note.

---

## 1. A cloud session can look at the dashboard

`CLAUDE.md` §3 carried this row:

| | can build & test | can push | can **look** at the dashboard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **cloud session** (web) | yes | yes | **no** |

The row was wrong, and `notes/PACKED-LAYOUT-COST-2026-08-25.md` built a table of
"nothing here" properties on top of it. Chromium is installed at
`/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1194`, Playwright is at `/opt/node22/lib/node_modules`,
the board serves over HTTP, and `page.screenshot()` writes a PNG. A session can open
a PNG. Nothing in that chain needs a local machine.

This is the same shape as the `getImageData` discovery on 25 August, one rung further
out. That one said: the canvas can be *measured* from here. This one says: the page can
be *seen* from here. Six sessions carried the first assumption; the second was written
down as a fact in the authoritative document one day after the first was disproved.

**`tools/look.js` makes it permanent.** It is a camera, not a check -- it asserts nothing
and cannot fail:

```
node /opt/node22/lib/node_modules/http-server/bin/http-server dist -p 8899 --silent &
node tools/look.js
```

It sweeps four viewport sizes into `dist/shots/` (gitignored) because the map has two
different opening states and which one you get depends on how much room it has. One
screenshot at one size is not a look at the map.

**Read the screenshots knowing they flatter small text.** `look.js` captures at
`deviceScaleFactor: 2`, and a PNG viewed scaled-to-fit shows the labels larger than a
1x monitor does. Judge *arrangement* from these freely. Do not certify *legibility* of
the smallest labels from them -- that still wants Stewart's actual screen.

## 2. What the first look found

Four defects. The whole gate was green before and after each of them, which is the point.

### 2.1 Two of the five era column heads were painted underneath the controls — FIXED

The head strip is shared. The canvas draws the era labels on it; the map's own DOM
controls, `.l0crumb` (PROGRAM MAP / START / the node count) and `.l0ctl` (OVERVIEW /
1:1 READ / the three options), are absolutely positioned at `top:10px` *on top of it*.
Controls occupied y 10..33. `geo.headRow` was 46, and the era label drew at baseline 20
with its note at 33.

So `THROUGH 1969 / Estimate through Blue Book` and `NO DATE / no start date recorded` --
the first column and the last, the two the controls sit above -- rendered as an
unreadable smear **at every viewport size**. Only `1970-1999` was reliably clean.

The eras are the horizontal argument exactly as the bands are the vertical one. A head a
reader cannot read takes its column's claim with it.

`geo.headRow` 46 -> 76, and the label baselines now derive from `PAD.t` rather than being
written as constants, so moving the strip again moves the text with it. It costs 30px of
reserved top strip and changes **no** opening fit -- at every size in the sweep the fit is
bound by width, not height. `.l0crumb` also went `flex-wrap:wrap` -> `nowrap`, because the
strip reserves one control row and a breadcrumb that wrapped to two would drop straight
back onto the labels.

### 2.2 The absent-link cross printed on top of its own label — FIXED

`chip()` computed the label's room as `w - 12*view.k` regardless of whether an `iso`
cross was going to be drawn inside the same chip at a fixed right inset. Where the label
filled the chip the two collided: **University at Albany (SUNY), Dep✕** -- the ellipsis
gone, the mark unreadable as either punctuation or a finding. Room now reserves the
cross's own width plus a gap, and only when there is a cross to reserve for.

### 2.3 `NO DATE` was subtitled with a JSON field name — FIXED

The column note read `dateStart absent`, sitting in a row with `Post-Blue Book`,
`AAWSAP / AATIP` and `Post-disclosure`. Now `no start date recorded`.

### 2.4 ~~`PK.geo` was never read~~ — **WRONG, AND WITHDRAWN 26 August 2026**

This section claimed the renderer and the check both read an undefined `PK.geo` and fell
through to literal fallbacks. **That was not true, and it was published in a commit
message and a pull request before it was checked.**

`merge_packed` in `tools/l0_patches.py` copies `config.geo` onto `L0DATA.packed`, so
`PK.geo` is fully populated and always was. Confirmed at runtime:
`PK.geo = {gutter:148, headRow:76, ...}`, and `PK.config` is the thing that is undefined.
The renderer tracked the model correctly throughout, and so did the check.

The head collision in 2.1 had one cause and it was the simple one: `geo.headRow` was 46,
and the DOM control row that sits on the same strip occupies y 10..33, leaving no room for
a label at baseline 20. Raising it to 76 is the whole fix.

The mistake was mine and it is the same shape as the one in 2.1's own commentary: I read
`current.config.geo` in `data/l0-layout-model.json`, found no top-level `geo` there, and
concluded the renderer could not be reading it — without checking that the renderer reads
a *different file*, `l0-model.json`, where `merge_packed` has already moved it. Reading one
artifact and inferring about another is not verification.

The defensive `(PK.config||{}).geo || PK.geo` I added has been reverted to plain `PK.geo`
in both files, because it implied a source of truth that does not exist.

## 3. The check that was missing, and now exists

Nothing in the gate could see 2.1. The nearest thing, `headInkPanned`, asks whether the
head strip carries ink after a pan -- and it passed happily for the whole of the first
build, because overprinted text is still ink.

`head-row-clear` is the twelfth canvas assertion. It measures the **clearance** between
the lowest pixel any DOM control occupies and the highest pixel an era label reaches:

```
ok  no era column head is painted under the map controls
    :: 5 era heads · text starts 12px below the control row (strip 76px, controls end at 33px)
```

Per §5 it is poisoned two ways. The predicate poison drives `headClear` to -12. The
**page** poison puts the real control row back where it used to sit
(`.l0ctl{top:44px}`) and requires the clearance to collapse. It does, to -22px. That is
the defect itself, reproduced on the running page, and the check catches it.

## 4. Two things that are still open, and are Stewart's call

### 4.1 The whole map fits with labels only above ~1944px of window width

`mountL0` opens fitted when `fitScale()` clears `L0_LABEL_DROP` (0.62), and otherwise at
1:1 in the top-left corner. Measured, on the current build:

| viewport | map area | fit | opening state |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1366 x 768 | 884 x 293 | 0.232 | corner at 1:1 |
| 1600 x 1000 | 1092 x 556 | 0.452 | corner at 1:1 |
| **1920 x 1080** | **1412 x 636** | **0.608** | **corner at 1:1** |
| 2560 x 1440 | 2052 x 996 | 0.920 | fitted, labels kept |

On the commonest desktop size the map misses the fitted branch by 0.012 -- **24 css px of
map width**. The consequence is not small. At 1:1 the opening view shows roughly three of
seven bands and four of five columns, and the only control that shows the whole map,
OVERVIEW, is below the label threshold and so draws **110 anonymous rectangles**. Read it
and see a third; see all of it and read nothing.

At 2560 it fits and it reads well. The arrangement is good: the bands descend by measured
mean C, the 2017-2026 column is visibly the dense one in OVERSIGHT, and that is the
argument appearing in the shape.

Three ways out, and the choice is editorial:

| | |
|---|---|
| lower `L0_LABEL_DROP` | the label face is `9.5 * k`, so 0.62 already admits a **5.9px** label. Lowering it buys the fit by making the thing it is protecting unreadable. Probably wrong. |
| reclaim 24px of map width | narrow `geo.gutter` (148), or let the map borrow from the detail rail. Cheapest, and it flips 1920 into the good branch. |
| accept it | below 1944px the reader pans. Honest, but OVERVIEW stays a wall of blank chips. |

Related and probably worth its own decision: **OVERVIEW below the threshold should
arguably not be offered at all**, or should draw something other than nothing -- band and
column structure with no chip labels is a different picture from 110 empty boxes.

### 4.2 Label truncation at ~37 characters, as the comp predicted

Confirmed, and the diagnosis is unchanged. It is tolerable for institutions the reader
knows (`United States Department of Ener…`) and bad for the citations and programme names
that are the board's load-bearing objects (`FY2024 Intelligence Authorizatio…`,
`CRADA between U.S. Army Ground V…`, `SETKA-AN / SETKA-MO (later Galak…`). The chip is
205px at one size, and §4 forbids widening one chip for an important node, so the
options are a smaller face, a second line, or curated `short` labels. The model already
reads `n.short` and prefers it -- **that is the cheap route and nobody has populated it.**

### 4.3 The band with 3 nodes

`disclosure` still holds 3 and still looks thin, exactly as Design said in §6 it would.
Seen on screen it reads as deliberate rather than broken -- it is the last row of a
descending order, and the map ends on the weakest band because that is where the order
puts it. No change recommended. Worth Stewart's eye rather than a decision from here.
