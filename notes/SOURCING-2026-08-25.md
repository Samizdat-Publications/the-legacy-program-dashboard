## The rule that produced the corrections

Every source cited here was opened and read. Not summarised, not inferred from a
search snippet, not attached because both endpoints happened to share a URL.

The reason that rule earns its cost is that **sourcing an edge means reading the
document the note rests on**, and roughly one note in six turned out to assert more
than its document does. Fourteen notes were corrected this session. None of them could
have been caught by any check; all of them read as confident and finished.

### The corrections, in order of how badly they were wrong

1. **Blue Book Special Report No. 14 does not name Battelle.** The note had Blue Book
   contracting "the Battelle Memorial Institute of Columbus, Ohio". Full-text scan of
   all 313 pages of the CIA's released copy: no "Battelle", no "Columbus", no
   "Memorial Institute". It credits ATIC at Wright-Patterson and says the study was
   "prepared by a panel of scientists both in and out of the Air Force". The
   attribution is standard in the literature and may well be right; the primary record
   does not make it, and the released copy is written so as not to.

2. **Every DIRD redacts the author the board credits.** All seven relevant Defense
   Intelligence Reference Documents have "Author:" redacted under FOIA exemption
   (b)(6) and "Prepared by:" under (b)(3):10 USC 424. The crediting rests entirely on
   DIA's separate list of deliverables to Congress, which could not be obtained
   readable. Counting names in the body is no substitute: Winterberg appears nine
   times in *Advanced Nuclear Propulsion* and once in *Aneutronic Fusion Propulsion*,
   which he did not write, because prior work gets cited.

3. **Sec. 7343 requires disclosure by the DNI, not to the AARO Director.** The note had
   it backwards. The enacted text conditions funds on whether "the Director of National
   Intelligence has provided the details of the activity to the appropriate committees
   of Congress and congressional leadership". The same reading surfaced subsection (c),
   which the note had missed and which is the sharper half: UAP-related independent
   research and development funding "shall not be allowable as indirect expenses"
   unless disclosed. That one is aimed at contractors.

4. **NASA gives Anamaria Berea two different affiliations.** The Blue Marble quote is
   exact but comes from the team announcement of 21 October 2022. The final report of
   September 2023 lists her as George Mason alone and mentions neither Blue Marble nor
   Seattle in 36 pages. Both statements are NASA's.

5. **Frost never asked Graves about aviation safety.** The note had Frost asking about
   commercial aviation safety and Graves citing NASA's reporting system. Frost asked
   two questions, about scientists on the advisory board and about Congress
   facilitating partnerships; Graves answered citing AIAA. The aviation-safety exchange
   in that hearing is Grothman's.

6. **Knapp's Russia-files testimony says he shared them with the Task Force**, not with
   BAASS and the DIA. The printed hearing reproduces oral statements only; written
   statements live at docs.house.gov and are not in the CHRG document. Anything drawn
   from a written statement cannot be sourced to the transcript. Same cause for the
   Knapp/Reid "first person he told" sequence.

7. **COMETA records an intention, not a delivery.** The note had the report "delivered
   to the head of the French government". It says its authors believed "sufficient
   questions of national interest are raised for the Chief of State and the Prime
   Minister to be provided with this information". Paris appears nowhere in it either.

8. **Kit Green is adjunct, not professor.** Wayne State's own words: "an adjunct
   professor and Fellow in Clinical Neuroimaging", plus assistant dean for
   China/Asia-Pacific.

9. **David Clarke is Sheffield Hallam's Journalism Reader**, not an associate professor.

10. **CEFAA's founding resolution number and signatory were never verified.** The note
    asserted "Resolución Exenta N° 01599... signed by Director General Gonzalo Miranda
    Aguirre". The DGAC page gives the date and the hosting relationship and gives
    neither. Both removed.

11. **The Aztec landing site loses its canyon and its mileage.** "Hart Canyon, roughly
    twelve miles northeast" is not in the available source.

12. **Marik von Rennenkampff's Sol Briefing role is not on the Sol Foundation's site.**
    It carries him as a Fellow and the author of a named paper.

13. **The two Peru comparison edges are not equivalent.** Andina names Chile among the
    peers DIFAA's reopening brought Peru level with and names no European body at all.
    One is a comparison the source draws; the other is a comparison the board draws.
    Tier 2 and Tier 3 now. A comparison edge is still an assertion about the world.

14. **A summariser invented a military title.** The automated read of Ryan Graves's
    testimony returned his squadron as "commanding officer of the Advanced Aerospace
    Threat Identification Program". That is not Graves and is not in the document.
    Opening the PDF gives what he wrote. **This would have shipped as a sourced Tier 1
    claim if the PDF had not been opened.** Reading the source is not optional even
    when something is reading it for you.

## Negative findings, which are also results

- **Public Law 118-31 contains no "non-earth origin" and no "exotic"** — zero
  occurrences across 2.8 million characters. The contractor turn-in mandate did not
  survive conference. What did is Div. A, Title XVIII, Subtitle C, secs. 1841-1843. The
  board's node already carried that caution; it is now sourced rather than hedged.
- **Project Condign cannot source the Setka edges** despite having a chapter whose
  first section is FORMER SOVIET UNION. "Setka", "IZMIRAN" and "Migulin" each appear
  zero times. Condign names a later apparatus: a 1989 UFO Commission and SOYUZUFOSENTR
  under V. G. Azhazha.
- **The USAF's own Roswell records review contains no San Agustin.** "San Agustin",
  "Barnett" and "Maltais" each appear zero times. That absence, in the most
  authoritative primary review of the Roswell record, is the strongest available
  support for the note's own sentence that no primary document supports the site.
- **BAASS is not in the federal award system.** USASpending carries Bigelow Aerospace
  LLC at North Las Vegas on two NASA contracts and returns zero results for "Bigelow
  Aerospace Advanced Space Studies" and for "BAASS".
- **AARO's Historical Record Report Vol. 1 never says "Las Vegas", "NIDS" or
  "HHM402"** — which is why the Las Vegas cluster is not sourced to it.
- **`geipan.fr/fr/node/58787`**, cited on the GEIPAN node, is the classification
  methodology guide and carries nothing about the 2014 Chilean Navy footage.

## A near miss worth keeping

"Newton" appears 21 times in the USAF Roswell report and every occurrence is **Maj.
Irving Newton, USAF**, the weather officer who identified the debris — not Silas Newton
of the Aztec hoax. Matching on a surname would have produced a confident and completely
wrong citation.

The same shape bit me once already this session: `grep -i neurologica` matches the word
"neurological", which is all over the AAWSAP biomedical material, and I briefly believed
a removal had failed because of it.

## Host access from this environment — read before planning the next pass

This is not a list of sites that are down. It is a list of what this particular
environment can and cannot open, and it decides what is sourceable from here.

| host | status | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **web.archive.org** | **unreachable entirely** (WebFetch refuses; curl connection reset) | the usual fallback for a 403 does not exist here. The board carries many `web.archive.org` source URLs that **cannot be verified from this environment.** |
| archive.org (not `web.`) | works, with redirects followed | how Condign Vol. 3 and the USAF Roswell report were obtained |
| govinfo.gov | works, including 3.6MB public laws | the single most productive host this session |
| thehill.com | 403 on every URL | 3 edges unsourced |
| med.wayne.edu | 503, consistently, not transient | 2 Genik edges unsourced |
| cfa.harvard.edu, projects.iq.harvard.edu | 403 | worked around via arXiv |
| compassrosepllc.com | 403 | worked around via the Senate confirmation record |
| cbc.ca, washingtontimes.com | 403 | 2 thin-sourced people unsourced |
| dia.mil, aaro.mil | refuse **curl** at the edge, serve **WebFetch** | fetch the PDF with WebFetch, then read the saved file with pypdf |
| aforteanosla.com.ar | DNS does not resolve | the Setka source is gone from here entirely |
| raytheon.com | 404 on its own ATFLIR/UAP page | navy.mil covered the pod instead |
| newsnationnow.com | 403 | 3 edges unsourced |

**The pattern that worked when a host was blocked: find the primary record instead of
the secondary one.** Compass Rose's site is 403, so McCullough came from his Senate
confirmation hearing — a better source. Harvard's pages are 403, so the CfA/Galileo
relationship came from a peer-reviewed affiliation block — also better. Waiting for the
right document rather than attaching the nearest one turned two Tier-2-at-best edges
into two Tier 1s, one batch later.
