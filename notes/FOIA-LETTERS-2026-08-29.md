paste-ready records description. What it does not give is the letter around the description: the
fee-waiver request, the format request, the segregability request and the deadline citation that
turn a description into a filing.

This file is that letter, seventeen times. **The records descriptions below are generated from
`REQUEST-QUEUE.md`, not retyped**, so the two files cannot drift apart. Re-generate after editing
the queue rather than hand-editing here.

---

## Before filing: four decisions that are Stewart's, not the board's

**1. Requester category.** Every letter below asks for a public-interest fee waiver and, in the
alternative, asks that fees be limited. It does **not** assert that you are a representative of
the news media. That category is defined in the statute and asserting it is a claim about you,
not about the records; a category challenge is slow and it puts your standing rather than the
records in issue. If you want to assert it, add it yourself.

**2. Expedited processing is NOT requested, on purpose.** 5 U.S.C. 552(a)(6)(E) requires a
"compelling need", which the statute defines as either an imminent threat to life or physical
safety, or a request "by a person primarily engaged in disseminating information" with urgency to
inform the public. Neither is comfortably met here, and a denied expedite request costs time and
credibility on a first-instance filing. Do not add one.

**3. Your details.** Every letter has three placeholders in this form:
`[[YOUR NAME]]`, `[[YOUR POSTAL ADDRESS]]`, `[[YOUR EMAIL]]`. They are deliberately
unmistakable. Fill them before sending; do not send a letter that still contains double brackets.

**4. Fee ceiling.** Each letter states a willingness to pay up to a named figure. It is written
as `[[FEE CEILING, e.g. 25.00]]` rather than guessed, because agreeing to a number you have not
chosen is the one line in a FOIA letter that can cost you actual money.

## The statutory language these letters rely on, verified

Pulled from the United States Code on govinfo, 2023 edition, title 5 section 552, rather than
recalled:

| clause | what it says |
|---|---|
| 552(a)(3)(B) | records shall be provided "in any form or format requested by the person if the record is readily reproducible by the agency in that form or format" |
| 552(a)(4)(A)(iii) | "Documents shall be furnished without any charge or at a charge reduced below the fees established under clause (ii) if disclosure of the information is in the public interest because it is likely to contribute significantly to public understanding of the operations or activities of the government and is not primarily in the commercial interest of the requester." |
| 552(a)(6)(A)(i) | the agency shall "determine within 20 days (excepting Saturdays, Sundays, and legal public holidays) after the receipt of any such request whether to comply" |
| 552(b) | "Any reasonably segregable portion of a record shall be provided to any person requesting such record after deletion of the portions which are exempt under this subsection. The amount of information deleted, and the exemption under which the deletion is made, shall be indicated on the released portion of the record" |

# F13. GAO - and why it is not a FOIA request

The Government Accountability Office is a legislative branch agency and **is not subject to the
Freedom of Information Act**. Sending it a FOIA letter invites a correct rejection on a ground
that has nothing to do with the records.

GAO handles records requests under its own disclosure regulation at 4 C.F.R. Part 81. Address
the request to GAO's Chief Quality Officer / records request function, cite Part 81 rather than
5 U.S.C. 552, and drop the fee-waiver and 20-day paragraphs from the template above, which have
no counterpart in that regulation.

**Before filing this one, read Part 81 itself** rather than adapting the FOIA letter by analogy.
The records sought - the NSIAD-95-187 working papers and the section 6803 audit products - are
specified in `REQUEST-QUEUE.md` F13 and that specification is unaffected; only the instrument
changes. This is flagged rather than drafted because getting the instrument wrong is exactly the
kind of avoidable error that costs a year on a records request.
