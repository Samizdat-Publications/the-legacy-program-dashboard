# The shell pass — giving the map back the screen

**27 August 2026.** Six changes to the shell, all from Stewart on the live Cloudflare
deploy, plus one tool. Every cause was already located in
`notes/HANDOFF-2026-08-26-UX.md`; none had been fixed. Read that first for the diagnosis
and this for what was done and what it cost.

---

## 1. The baseline was the first problem

PR #5 held the whole of 26 August — the authored chip labels, the denial adjudication,
the image tiles, the first-paint fix, the gate at 46/40/28/26 — and it was still an open
draft. `master` was far behind it, so a session cut from `master` starts without the
handoff, without the corrected `CLAUDE.md`, and without Design's shell response. That is
how this session began, and it is worth stating plainly: **an unmerged PR is invisible to
the next session.** The gate was re-run green on that branch and it was merged before
anything else happened.

---

## 2. FIT showed numerals because one constant did two jobs

`dense()` was `view.k < L0_LABEL_DROP`. One number, 0.62, decided both *stop drawing
labels* and *collapse every chip into a count*, and every fit at a normal window lands
below it. So the board's answer to "show me the map" was a grid of numerals.

**Splitting the constant would not have been enough**, and this is the part worth
keeping:

| window | fit (dossier folded) | under a 0.30 cell threshold? |
|---|---|---|
| 1366x768 | 0.22 | **still cells** |
| 1600x900 | 0.41 | no |
| 1920x1080 | 0.62 | no |
| 2560x1440 | 1.04 | no |

A 0.30 threshold looks like a fix on a 2560 monitor and still ambushes a laptop. So cell
mode became an explicit opt — `L0_OPT.cells`, off by default, with a `CELLS` control and a
`CMD.l0opt('cells')` entry. **No fit and no zoom can reach it.**

### The five checks that would have gone on passing

`overview-is-never-anonymous`, `overview-draws-no-orphan-edges`, the edge-layer check,
`axis-type-readable-at-every-scale` and `selection-survives-cell-mode` all reached dense
mode by setting `v.k = 0.45`. The moment cell mode stopped being a function of scale, all
five would have kept passing **while measuring the chip renderer instead**. They set the
flag now.

The denial-marks probe inside the same block wanted the opposite — it needs chips, and had
been getting them for free by raising `v.k` above 0.62. It says so explicitly and sets the
flag off.

## 3. The map is what loads

`l0entry` defaulted true. Design settled it on 26 August: *"Entry is therefore 'here is
all of it', not 'here is where we think you should start'."* It defaults false; the six
cards are one click away on `START`.

Two camera fixes went with it. `openPath` lit its cells and left the camera wherever it
stood, so picking a path from a zoomed-in board highlighted seven chips the reader could
not see — it calls `MAP.fitAll()` now, which is the FIT ALL gesture named as a function so
the click handler and the command share one path. And `focus()` forced `view.k` to at
least 1.15 on every pick, throwing away the overview the reader chose the node from. It
keeps the reader's scale.

---

## 4. The dossier, and a transition that never ran

The dock shipped open with nothing selected, showing a paragraph of placeholder prose in
~290px the map wanted. It starts folded now and comes out on selection, keyed on a
*change* of selection so either tab overrides the rule until the reader picks something
else.

It gained the tab Stewart asked for — *"that tab needs to exist on the open version too"* —
matching the spine's label in type, letter-spacing and rotation. It is `position:fixed` and
pinned to `--dock-w`, because `.dock{overflow-x:hidden}` clips anything hanging off the
panel's left edge.

**The slide is the part to remember.** Three attempts:

| attempt | why it failed |
|---|---|
| `.dock{transition:width}` | the dock is a grid CHILD; its width comes from the `.body` track, so its own width property never changes and there is nothing to interpolate |
| `.body{transition:grid-template-columns}` | closer, and still wrong — `--dock-w` is an **unregistered** custom property, so it has no type and flips discretely |
| `@property --dock-w {syntax:'<length>'}` | a typed custom property animates; the track follows it and the map's edge follows the track |

Sampled every frame, the second attempt gave `30px -> 762px in one frame`, then a settle
from 762 to 800 over 140ms. **A 95% jump with a polite ending is not a slide, and it would
have shipped looking like one had nobody sampled it.** The third gives
`30 -> 681 -> 743 -> 762 -> 784 -> 797 -> 800`.

The analyst fold got the same curve on Stewart's instruction. It was `style.display =
'none'`, and display is not interpolable — one shell, two manners. It animates `max-height`
now: `359 -> 311 -> 270 -> 236 -> 191 -> 149 -> 104 -> 84`.

**Three controls became one.** The dossier had a `.dock-toggle` chevron and a `.dock-hide`
chevron, each an 11px glyph on the same edge the new tab occupies. `tools/walk.js` found
both overlaps on its first clean run. They are retired rather than moved: both duplicate
what the fold does, and `dock-hide` collapsed the panel to zero width, which is strictly
worse than the spine — no width back that the spine does not also give, and nothing left
to click.

## 6. The era heads printed under the buttons, and the guard could not have caught it

`.l0head` is pinned at `top:10px`. The reserve was `PAD.t = h + 20` and the label was drawn
at `PAD.t - 22`, i.e. `h - 2` — **twelve pixels above where the controls end, at every
width.** It only ever looked right because `HEAD_MIN` is 76 and a single-row strip is about
25px, so the floor dominated and bought accidental clearance.

Open an entry path (its chip widens the left group) and the dossier (it squeezes the row)
and `.l0ctl` wraps. Measured, `.l0head` height against window width:

| 2560 | 1920 | 1600 | 1366 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25px | 56px | 87px | 149px |

`measureHead` records the control row's **bottom edge** as `PAD.ctl` now, and the era band
is `ERA_BAND` (32px) below it, authored once and consumed by both the reserve and the
baselines so they cannot disagree.

`head-row-clear` was arithmetic that reproduced neither, and it passed throughout on a
build that was overprinting. It reads pixels under the buttons' own rects now, at four
widths, in the state that wraps hardest. **Its self-test was worse than the check**: it
re-ran `probe()` and asked the predicate about ink fields that only `headSweep` fills, so
it read the `-1` default and reported `ok` no matter what the page did. It measures now —
215px of era-head ink appears when the row is dropped 44px.

## 7. `tools/walk.js`

The harness `HANDOFF-2026-08-26-UX.md` §5 asked for. `look.js` photographs states somebody
chose in advance; every defect Stewart has reported was in a state nobody chose. This one
enumerates controls, clicks each, screenshots, and records overlaps, clipped text,
offscreen controls, the map scale and **the map's share of the window**.

A camera with a notebook. It asserts nothing and must never enter the gate.

**Its first run reported 33 overlaps in THE COVER-UP view and eight controls off-screen,
and every one was wrong.** They were the left rail's filter chips below the fold of
`.rail-scroll` — `scrollHeight 943`, `clientHeight 363`, working exactly as designed.
`getBoundingClientRect` reports where a scrolled-away element *would* be. Nothing was wrong
with the board.

That is this repo's own lesson pointed at its newest tool, and the fix was to make
"visible" mean visible: an element counts only if its rect still intersects every clipping
ancestor between it and the document. After that, 20 states, **0 overlaps, 0 clipped, 0
offscreen** — and the two real findings it had made (the buried chevrons) stood out instead
of being lost in the noise. A camera that cries wolf 33 times is worse than no camera.

## 8. What was NOT changed, and why — the label threshold

At 1600x900 the fitted map lands at **k = 0.587** and `L0_LABEL_DROP` is 0.62, so the chips
draw unlabelled. That is agonisingly close, and the obvious move is to lower the threshold.

**It would have been a mistake, and the screenshot said otherwise.** The chip label is
drawn at `8 * view.k` px — it *scales*. Rendering at k=0.63 and reading the PNG, the labels
looked perfectly legible. Measured on the page instead: `devicePixelRatio 1`, backing ratio
1, label font **4.69 CSS px** at the fitted scale. Unreadable on a real monitor. The image
had simply been displayed larger than life.

This is exactly the honest limit `look.js` and the 26 August handoff both record, and it
caught a change that was one command from being made.

**So 0.62 stays, and the real constraint is type size, not string length** — which is worth
noting because Design's §5 answered a different question, correctly: it addressed how a
label should be *cut* (middle, never end), and that pass is done. The threshold is about
whether there is any type worth drawing.

Where labels actually land:

| viewport | fit | label type | labels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1366x768 | 0.45 | 3.6px | dropped |
| 1600x1000 | 0.59 | 4.7px | dropped |
| 1920x1080 | 0.74 | 5.9px | **kept**, small |
| 2560x1440 | 1.06 | 8.4px | **kept**, comfortable |

On a window under about 1750px you get the lattice and not the names. Reaching the names
is `1:1`, or selecting a chip. **Whether that trade is right is Stewart's call to make with
his eyes, and it is the one thing in this pass that was deliberately left for him.**
