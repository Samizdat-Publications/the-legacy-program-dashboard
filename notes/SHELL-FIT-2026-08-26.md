# Fit, and the constant that was standing in for something that varies

26 August 2026. Porting Claude Design's shell response (`notes/design-2026-08-26/`).

## What Stewart reported

> "The map is never able to fit ... it really should auto fit until someone clicks
> something so they can see the whole thing." · "Clicking show on the map doesn't do much
> since the map is so obscured by the panels." · "It's also not clear how to collapse the
> right or left panel."

## What was actually wrong, in the order it was found

**1. The opening state.** `mountL0` ended with `if (fk >= L0_LABEL_DROP) centreOn(fk); else
cornerOn(1);`. Below about 1944px of window the map gave up on fitting and opened at 1:1 in
the corner: three of seven bands. Justified once, when below-threshold meant 110 anonymous
rectangles; the dense occupancy mode killed that justification and the default was never
revisited. Now `centreOn(fk)` at every width.

**2. The observer clamped but never re-fitted.**

```js
const ro = new ResizeObserver(() => { measure(); clampView(); repaint(); });
```

Open the dossier, the map narrows, the camera stays exactly where it was and shows less of
the board than a second earlier. This was most of what "the map is obscured" actually meant.
Design's own study hit the same class of bug and named it first in their port order:
*"Every fit must run against a measured rect, never a default. This was the study's own first
bug and it presented exactly as 'the map never fits'."* Ours measured fine and then declined
to act on the measurement.

**3. The head strip's reserve was a constant.** `geo.headRow: 76`, chosen when the control
row was one line of six buttons. Adding the two fit scales and the AUTO/HELD chip made the
row wider than the map at narrow widths, and it overflowed **under the dossier**: the BREATH
button, 15 dead sample points, blocked by `.dock-grip` and `.edoss`. Letting it wrap instead
would have dropped the second line onto the era labels.

Both failures are one mistake, and it is the third time this file has made it — the first two
were `w - 12*k` reserving chip room whether or not a mark was drawn, and the crumb's
`max-width: calc(100% - 24px)`. **A constant standing in for something that varies.** The
strip now reserves what the control row actually occupies, measured after render:

```js
const h = host.querySelector('.l0head').getBoundingClientRect().height;
PAD.t = Math.max(HEAD_MIN, Math.ceil(h) + 20);
```

Any future control can be added without anyone remembering to retune a number, and
`head-row-clear` keeps guarding the result.

## What was ported, and one thing that was deliberately not

| Design | here |
|---|---|
| FIT ROOM / FIT ALL, each printing its scale | **ported, re-derived** — see below |
| auto-fit on entry, released by the first gesture | ported: `AUTO` until the first drag, wheel or pick, then `HELD`. A fit button is the only way back, and its tooltip says so |
| a folded panel keeps its label, one click restores | ported: the dossier folds to a 30px spine reading `DOSSIER — CLICK TO OPEN` |
| chrome in screen space | **already shipped** — `axis-type-readable-at-every-scale` has guarded it since 25 August |

**The one re-derivation.** Design's pair is "fit what is left" against "fit everything and let
the panels cover the overlap". Their study overlays panels on a full-width canvas. This board
does not: the shell is `grid-template-columns: var(--rail-w) minmax(0,1fr) var(--dock-w)`, so
the panels are grid **columns** — they take the map's width rather than covering it. **There
is no overlap here to fit under.** Copying the labels would have shipped a FIT ALL that did
exactly what FIT did and printed a second identical number for ever.

Same information, honest for this layout: FIT fits the room there is; FIT ALL folds the
panels to spines and fits the width that frees. The difference between the two numbers is
still exactly the price of what is open.

## And it settles a question geometry could not

`CLAUDE.md` §9 asked whether to reclaim 24px from `geo.gutter` for a labelled fit at 1920.
Tried, and **the note's stated fix is wrong**: `fitScale` is
`(hostW - gutter - 16) / (worldW - gutter)`, so the gutter sits on *both* sides of the ratio
and shrinking it moves numerator and denominator together — 148 → 124 bought 0.608 → 0.613
against a 0.62 threshold. Taking the 24px off `worldW` instead was far worse, 0.379, because
narrower columns force taller cells and the vertical axis starts binding. Both reverted.

**FIT ALL reaches 0.62 at a 1920 window.** The labelled whole-board fit was never a geometry
problem; the map just needed its width back. The question is closed and no geometry changed.

## Design §4 and §6, and two places their letter had to bend

Both ported. Both needed a colour decision Design could not have made from outside the
build, because it turns on what the colours already mean here.

**The selection surviving cell mode (§4).** Below the label threshold there are no chips, so
a fit lost the selected node outright — pick a node, the map re-fits, and the thing you
picked is no longer on screen. The cell it lives in now carries the mark and the sub-line
reads `selection in here`.

Design asked for *the amber outline and an amber numeral*. **Amber is already taken in this
view**: it is the start-here flag, and a flagged cell already draws exactly that outline and
that numeral. Reusing it would have made a selected cell and a flagged cell
indistinguishable — worse than not marking the selection at all, because it would put a
false start-here pointer on the board, and pointers here are editorial claims about where to
look. The selection uses `#5ed4e6`, which is what selection means everywhere else on this
board: the chip stroke, the crumb count, the lit controls. Same treatment, in the colour
already reserved for it, and it composes with the flag instead of overwriting it.

**Band counts (§6).** Design's example runs the name and count together —
`DISCLOSURE · 3 nodes`. `REVERSE-ENGINEERING · 15 nodes` is about 162px of 9px mono and the
gutter holds 138, so appending would have truncated the longest band names to make room for
a number. The count goes on the second line instead, and that line now draws in **cell mode
too**: it used to be suppressed there to save vertical room, which meant the occupancy
view — the one view that exists to show the shape of the board — was the only one that would
not say a band was nearly empty.

Both checks assert the half that makes them checks rather than colour swatches:

| check | the half that matters |
|---|---|
| `thin-bands-print-amber` | amber beside every band under five **and beside no band of five or more**. A gutter painted amber top to bottom would pass the first half alone. |
| `selection-survives-cell-mode` | cyan in the selected node's own cell **and in no other cell**. Cyan somewhere on a grid of 27 occupied cells proves nothing; the claim is about *which* cell. The target cell is computed from the model's band and column for that node, never from where the renderer put the ink. |

## §3, SHOW ON MAP — and the control did not exist

Design specified three choreographies for a control this board had never had. There was no
SHOW ON MAP anywhere: selecting a node focused the map as a side effect of `selectNode`, and
that was all. So the port is the control **and** the choreography.

**A pick takes the camera, and that was a regression I had just introduced.** Design lists
drag, wheel and *pick* as the gestures that flip AUTO to HELD, and pick is the easy one to
miss. Without it, selecting a node zoomed to 1.15, the dossier opened a frame later, the box
changed, and the AUTO re-fit threw the focus away. Measured before the fix: pick a node at a
1920 window and the map settles at **k = 0.289** with the node nominally on screen and
nothing on it readable. `focus()` now holds, and remembers its target so a later box change
re-centres on that node rather than re-fitting the board — the dossier opening must not lose
the thing the dossier is about.

| | measured at 1920 |
|---|---|
| **CEDE** | dock 960 → 30, map host 758 → **1890**, k = **0.62** — the whole board *with labels*, target outlined |
| **PORTHOLE** | camera byte-identical before and after. Nothing moves, which is the whole promise |
| **TETHER** | camera only, nothing folds |

The choreography is a preference, stored per reader, and picking one runs it immediately
rather than arming it for next time — so the reader sees what they chose instead of being put
in a mode they have to get out of.

### Design was right about the inset and my reason for skipping it was wrong

Design §7: *"the selection bar carries SHOW ON MAP and CLEAR, so inset it by the panel width
and put it above both the dossier and the dim."* I skipped the inset, reasoning that this
board's dossier is a grid column and never covers the map.

True of the dossier. **`.l0ledger` is `position:absolute`, `z-index:6`, and sits on the map.**
Four of the bar's five buttons — CEDE, PORTHOLE, TETHER, CLEAR — hit-tested to `.l0row`
inside it. The reasoning that dismissed Design's instruction was checking the wrong panel.
The foot is now inset by whatever actually overlaps it, **measured** rather than given the
ledger's 306px, because that width is a CSS constant that has already changed once.

### The fourth collision, and the last one this shape

The selection bar and the start-here legend were both `position:absolute; bottom:12px`. All
five buttons dead. That is the fourth time this file has put two absolutely positioned things
on one edge and let arithmetic decide whether they clear each other:

| | |
|---|---|
| `w - 12*k` | reserved chip room whether or not a mark was drawn |
| `max-width: calc(100% - 24px)` | ran the crumb under the control row |
| `geo.headRow: 76` | a constant for a row whose width varies |
| `bottom: 12px` twice | the selection bar under the legend |

Bumping the legend to `bottom:68px` would have been the fifth, because the bar is 44px only
until someone changes it. They share a flex column and the layout does it.

**And the check was scoped to the wrong thing.** `every map control is clickable across its
whole face` was written against `.l0ctl` after the crumb collision. The selection bar shipped
into a different container and the check could not see it fail. It now covers the surface —
`.l0ctl`, `.l0selbar`, the legend toggle and the crumb's own chips: 14 controls, every sample
point across every face. Both poisons still bite (`POISON=head` → 6 of 14 overlapped,
`POISON=clicks` → 168 dead sample points).
