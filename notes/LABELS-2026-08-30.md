# Selecting a node did not name what it connects to

**30 August 2026.** Reported by Stewart from the live board, with a screenshot:

> when I click tom delong, I would expect to see connections to the stars academy,
> tom delong, mcCasland etc etc but instead it labels nothing. This defeats the purpose

He was right, and the measurement is worse than "labels nothing" suggests it might
be. On `tom-delonge`, which has ten neighbours, the map named **one of ten**. The
edges were drawn, lit and correct; every node at the far end of them was an
anonymous dot.

| | on-screen neighbours named |
|---|---|
| before | 1 of 10 |
| after | 10 of 10, no overlaps |

`notes/labels-2026-08-30/delonge-before.png` and `delonge-after.png`. The "before"
capture is not from an old build -- it is today's build with `POISON=neighbours`
set, which is the same thing and is the point of that poison.

---

## What was wrong: four causes, all in the label pass

The screen-space label pass was built on 30 August for Design's change 2 (labels
chosen by space, never by rank). It was correct at rest and wrong the moment
anything was selected.

**1. The renderer computed adjacency and threw it away.** `drawNode` derives
`const near = state === 'connected'` for its own stroke and glow, and then pushed
`{ n, text, rw, sel, hov, dim }` to the label pass. `near` was not in that object.
So by the time the pass ran, a neighbour of the selection was indistinguishable
from any of the other 900 nodes and was ordered purely on its distance from the
centre of the window.

**2. The ~900 dimmed nodes competed for the same budget and the same collision
boxes.** They draw at `globalAlpha` 0.16 in `PALETTE.faint`, which is to say
illegibly. A label nobody can read was taking the box from one they had just asked
for. A dimmed node is no longer a candidate while a selection is live.

**3. The outward walk started from the wrong place.** Design's algorithm walks
candidates outward from the pointer, or the viewport centre when the pointer is
off-canvas. A selection is a third origin and outranks the centre. Note the
first cut of this fix read the origin from `cand.find(c => c.sel)` and **silently
did nothing**: a selected person draws as a full plate and `return`s before the
label push, so the selected node is never a candidate. It fell back to the centre
without saying so. That is the shape of bug this repo keeps paying for, and it
survived one build.

**4. One slot per label.** `ttsa` and `steve-justice` sit at the *same* screen
point on this selection -- the force layout put them there. With one candidate
position each, only one of them could ever be named, whatever the ordering. A
neighbour now gets eight placements tried in order (below, above, the two sides,
the four diagonals) and takes the first that is free. This is still choosing by
space and still one size: it offers a label another *place*, never a smaller
*font*. It is scoped to the selection's neighbours, because extending it to the
whole map would densify the rest state Design deliberately kept sparse.

## And one thing that was wrong for a different reason

`LABEL_MAX` was 37, borrowed from the packed map. That is safe **there** because
all 110 L0 nodes carry an authored `short` already under 37. The network map has
no `short` at all -- 0 of 964 nodes carry one -- so it truncates raw labels, and
`ttsa` ships as a 69-character string ending in a rename note.

Dropping to 24 fitted more labels and produced `TO THE STARS…STARS INC.)` and
`MATERIALS AN…DY ANALYSIS`. That is not a label, it is two halves of the same
three words. The cap is back at 37 and `labelFit` now drops a **trailing
parenthetical** before truncating, because a parenthetical is disambiguation
rather than a name and the dossier still carries it. Once the multi-slot placement
was in, width pressure stopped being the binding constraint anyway.

## Is this selection by rank?

No, and the distinction is the one Design drew. **Degree is a global property**:
it names the same nodes for every reader on every frame, which is why it may not
decide who gets a name, and why `PLATE_DEGREE` and `LABEL_DEGREE` had to go.
**Adjacency to the selection is the reader's own action**, scoped to the node they
clicked. It is the same category as `sel` and `hov`, which have always jumped the
queue.

With nothing selected, `active` is null, no node is connected or dimmed, and the
ordering is the one that shipped. That is what keeps the two rest-state checks
meaning what they meant.

## The check

`runtime_check.js`, two assertions, and `MAP.cam()` is now published for the same
reason `labelBoxes()` is -- so a check reads the renderer's own camera instead of
re-deriving a transform that can drift away from it.

The fixture is **chosen at runtime**: the node whose connection count is nearest
six, so the check keeps meaning something after a research pass moves the graph.
The first cut took the most-connected node instead -- `d-coverup`, 88 neighbours
on screen -- and required 90% of them. It got 62, and **those 26 were not a
defect**: with the dimmed remainder already dropped the only remaining candidates
are the neighbours themselves, so they were colliding with each other, and 88
nine-pixel labels do not fit in that cluster. A correct renderer cannot pass that
bar without shrinking, which Design forbids. The check was demanding a bug.

`POISON=neighbours` sets `window.__LABEL_NO_CON`, which makes the **real
renderer** forget adjacency on a real frame -- the state the board shipped in this
morning. Covered fraction goes 5 of 5 to 0 of 5. The poison is on the page, not on
the probe.

Measured after, all at zero label overlaps:

| selection | neighbours | named |
|---|---|---|
| `tom-delonge` | 10 | 10 |
| `luis-elizondo` | 51 | 45 |
| `david-grusch` | 88 | 79 |

The hubs do not reach 100% and should not: past a certain density the honest
answer is that the labels do not fit, and the rule is skip, never shrink.

## Also fixed, and it is why this took a detour

`state_snapshot.py --gate` ran each check with `capture_output=True` and
**discarded the output on failure**. CI said `runtime_check FAIL(1)` and nothing
else -- not which of the 46 assertions, not what it saw. Reading it back meant
reproducing the whole run. `_why()` now prints the check's own `FAIL` lines, falls
back to the tail of stderr for a check that dies before asserting, and says "and
printed nothing" for one that exits silently. All three shapes demonstrated.
