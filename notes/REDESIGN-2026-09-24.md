# The redesign, 24 September 2026: from a graph to an atlas

**Status: DECIDED.** Written by the Opus 5.5 session of 24 September 2026 on Stewart's instruction to
"holistically rethink" the board, with full creative control and one constraint: nothing is sacred
except the dataset. **The implementation plan is `notes/PLAN-2026-09-24.md`.** The measurements behind
every number here are in `notes/redesign-2026-09-24/` (appendices A to F and T, listed at the end).

Stewart, 24 September 2026: *"We have a lot of data and are trying to figure out how to arrange it all
so it's discoverable and not just a sea of nodes connected without meaning."*

---

## 0. The decision on one screen

**The board's problem is not too much data.** The only structure a reader can navigate today is the
raw graph. The meaning is in the data: why two things are linked, which story a node belongs to, which
document a claim rests on. But the interface hides it, a layer of structure is missing, and some of the
data never ships at all.

The redesign adds the missing middle layer, **topics**, and makes every link explain itself:

| Layer | What a reader meets | Today | After the redesign |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Atlas** | 10 sections x 4 eras, about 75 topics | a 135-chip grid of nodes that fits only a viewport 1024px tall or more, so most laptops get anonymous count cells | topics, not nodes; every label drawn on a 1366x768 laptop |
| **Topics** | "Roswell, July 1947", "AAWSAP and BAASS", "Who gets told: SAP reporting law" | 8 threads touching 44 nodes (3.9%) | about 75 topics covering 90%+ of nodes, each showing what the record shows, what is claimed, and what is contested |
| **Entities** | people, institutions, programmes, documents, laws, places, events | 80 flat connection rows for Grusch, 65% of them labelled "member" | connections grouped by relationship, each with its sentence, dates, tier and sources |
| **Evidence** | claims, and the documents they rest on | 62% of claims show their citation only as hover text | a Library of about 700 source records; every claim links to its source, and every source lists what it establishes |

Cross-cutting: **Timeline** (lanes by section, event text shown), **Directories** (people,
organisations, programmes, laws, places, events, with facets), **Questions** (the big questions with the
evidence for and against), **Connect** (how is X related to Y, by meaningful paths only),
**Search** (everything), **Analyst** (unchanged, with new tools), **Patents** and **Reports** (kept).

**Retires:**
- the old single-file shell and its gate (it blocks the nuclear register and doubles CI);
- the 135-node L0 map as the front door (the Atlas replaces it);
- the starburst neighbourhood (a grouped view replaces it);
- the node tier letter in the interface;
- the 59 `domain` nodes as graph nodes (they become topics and explainers).

**Kept exactly:**
- every research record, claim, tier and source;
- the rules in `CLAUDE.md` sections 2, 2a, 5 and 6;
- size never encodes importance, and one label size;
- every check shown failing on its own poison;
- the analyst, the patents pages, the reports, the back button, and the terminal look.

## 1. What we learned

### 1a. The corpus is strong, and it has real structure

- 1,136 nodes, 2,685 hand-written sourced edges, 5,897 tiered claims (T1 3,701, T2 1,618, T3 578),
  1,059 timeline events (board generated 2026-09-19). 5,352 of 5,370 connection rows carry a human
  sentence explaining the link. [A, C]
- **The graph has topical structure a reader would recognise.** Louvain modularity is 0.67 (0.70 with
  hub edges damped), against 0.48 on a randomly rewired copy. There are 17 stable communities that read
  as topics a researcher would name:
  - "AAWSAP / AATIP / BAASS and the DIRDs", stability 0.91;
  - "Other nations' programmes", 0.94;
  - "Kirtland 1980: Bennewitz, Doty, Dulce and MJ-12", 0.85;
  - "SAP law and the congressional reporting architecture", 0.79. [B]
- **Every edge type carries signal.** Each keeps 61-91% of its edges inside one community, against
  about 9% by chance. The structural backbone (custody, funding, programmatic, institutional,
  legislative, research) is 63% of edges and reaches 86% of nodes. The assertion layer (denial,
  witness-chain, investigative, behavioral) is what lets every topic touch every other. [B]
- **Time is nearly universal.** 97% of edges carry a date signal and 89.5% of nodes are dated. Slicing by
  edge date gives readable eras before 2017; 2017-2026 holds half of all dated edges and is the hairball.
  [A, B]
- **98.2% of nodes already carry a topical research-file slug**, and case files are tightly knit. The
  share of a file's edges joining two of its own nodes is Dulce 1.00, propulsion 0.96, Lazar/S-4 0.91.
  [A]

### 1b. The interface hides the meaning

- **The node page lists connections flat.** On-map neighbours come first, then the rest, alphabetically
  by id.
  - Every off-map row is labelled "member" instead of its relationship (`app/src/pages/node.ts:244`):
    3,497 of 5,370 rows, 65%.
  - The edge's own sentence is only a hover tooltip, and edge sources are never shown.
  - Grusch has 80 rows; AARO's page is 2,477px tall. [C]
- **Claim citations:** 62% (3,643 claims) show only as text with no link, although their citations
  resolve to about 724 real documents and 1,692 of those claims cite a document that is already a
  node. [A]
- **Never rendered:** 664 of 780 open questions, `unverified` notes on 213 nodes, chronology on 243, the
  text of 1,026 timeline events, 694 person topic tags (overwritten before shipping,
  `build_app_data.py:458`), and 14 finished storyline essays. [A, C]
- **Search** indexes nodes and claims only. It promises events and finds none. [C]
- **The neighbourhood** is a starburst of equal-length lines; hops 2 and 3 are a roster of names. [C]
- **No phone layout:** at 375px the node page's main column is 0px wide. [C]

### 1c. The navigable structure is thin, and it does not match the data

- **Curated structures are thin.** Threads, highlights, entry paths, the L0 map and dossiers together
  touch 170 nodes (15%). 45.6% of nodes sit in no authored structure at all. 10 of 338 people are on any
  thread, and the L0 map holds no people by construction. [B, C]
- **The existing groupings miss the natural topics.** Against the measured communities, `cluster` scores
  a normalised mutual information of 0.17 and the L0 band 0.24 (0 is chance, 1 is a perfect match).
  Both cut across every story. [B]
- **59 `domain` nodes are topics stored as entities**: "Funding Channels", "Chain of custody", "The FFRDC
  Seam", "The four SAP reporting regimes". They distort degree, paths and communities. [T, A]
- **About 25-40 duplicate entity clusters.** Five nodes appear to be the RG 615 records collection;
  `gao-nsiad-95-187` and `gao-roswell-report-1995` are the same report. [T, A]
- **Two kinds of hub.** A provincial hub is its own topic: AAWSAP has 83% of its neighbours inside its
  community. A connector hub fans out: DoD reaches 15 topics. AARO's historical record sits on the
  shortest path for 7 of 8 test pairs, so a naive "how are these connected" answer is always "through
  AARO". [B]
- **Claims about one case are scattered across its members.** Roswell has 11 claims on its own node and
  79 on 55 other nodes. [A]

### 1d. The top-level map is at its limit by construction

- **Label mode needs every chip to fit one screen**: no scroll, no zoom, one 11px size. At 1920 wide it
  holds at a viewport 1024px tall and falls to cell mode at 1023.
  - A maximised browser on a 1080p screen gets 900-970px: 99 of 135 chips, 9 of 14 flags, 0 lines.
  - At 1366x768: 56 of 135 chips. [C]
- **The 135 budget calls itself a stopgap.** Membership is by degree, and people, places and domains can
  never qualify, so S-4, Dulce and Lazar can never reach the top map. [C, D]
- **The nuclear register (47 witnesses, verified) is parked** only because of a pixel check on the old
  shell, which no reader sees. [D, F]

### 1e. Some data never reaches the reader, and the process costs too much

- **Silent drops.** `merge.py` drops entity `conflicts`, `unverified` and `openQuestions`: 63 records,
  121 items, including "the case against" on S-4, Dulce and TR-3B. It also coerces 89 unknown edge
  verbs to `programmatic`, which regrade then weights x3. [E]
- **Sourcing rules skip whole surfaces.** They are not applied to the 1,811 dossier claims or to
  timeline events: 40 dossier T1 claims cite a secondary source and 22 cite AARO/DoD for an outside
  fact. [E]
- **Two shells, one of them deployed nowhere but still gated.** About 12,000 lines and 754 KB of old
  shell tooling, and an 18.6 MB build. CI takes about 24 minutes per deploy; `.git` grew from 146 MB to
  568 MB in four weeks, mostly from committed generated files. [E]
- **`CLAUDE.md` is 68 KB** and loads into every session rooted in the repo, about 17k tokens on every
  turn. [session]
- **The gate is two commands**, and running one without the other turned master red on 19 September. [D]
- **Em dashes ship in interface strings**, against Stewart's standing rule. [C]

## 2. The design rules

These define "discoverable and meaningful" for this board. Every phase of the plan is checked against
them.

1. **Group before you list, and name every group.** No list of more than about twelve items is shown
   ungrouped.
2. **Every link explains itself.** A connection shows its relationship, its sentence, its dates, its tier
   and its sources, never a bucket name alone.
3. **There is a middle layer.** Topics sit between the subject and the entity. A reader browses topics
   and drills into entities.
4. **The front door shows topics, never nodes.** The top-level map fits a 1366x768 laptop with every
   label drawn, and it grows by adding topics, not chips.
5. **Evidence is navigable.** Claims and sources are objects with pages. A reader can go from a claim to
   its source, and from the source to everything else it establishes.
6. **Both sides sit in the same place.** Every topic separates what the record shows (Tier 1), what is
   claimed (Tier 2 and 3, with who claims it), and what is contested (conflicts, denials,
   contradictions).
7. **Time and place are axes, not decoration.** Every topic has a span, every entity page has its
   chronology, and places are browsable.
8. **Hubs are indexes, not stars.** A connector hub's page groups its neighbours by relationship and by
   topic.
9. **Nothing authored is hidden, and nothing authored is dropped.** Every field a researcher wrote has a
   place on some page, and the pipeline rejects rather than silently discards.
10. **Every rule that protects the evidence stays:**
    - no invented sources;
    - the three claim tiers;
    - the AARO/DoD cap;
    - notability with provenance;
    - size never encodes importance, and one label size;
    - poisoned checks.
11. **Every new page works at 375px.** Stewart reads on his phone; so will his friends.

## 4. The data model: additive, with the dataset untouched

**Principle.** The authored research files stay the source of truth, and nothing in them is rewritten to
fit the new design. New structure lives in new authored files, or in side-cars keyed by stable ids that
the build joins in. Nothing is deleted: a duplicate becomes an alias in `research/_removed.json`, the
mechanism the board already has.

| # | Addition | File(s) | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stable ids** | computed in merge | claim id `c-` + 10 hex of sha1(node id + normalised text); edge id `e-` + 10 hex of sha1(a, b, type, normalised label). A citation to an id that no longer resolves fails a check, so a changed claim is re-cited deliberately rather than silently |
| 2 | **One vocabulary** | `schema/vocab.json` | classes, edge types with reader names, relationship predicates and families, topic kinds, member roles, source types, person roles, vital status. Read by merge, build_app_data, verify and the app (shipped as `/data/vocab.json`), and restated by the independent audit. Replaces six copies of the vocabulary scattered across the code [E] |
| 3 | **Topics** | `research/topics/_sections.json`, `research/topics/<id>.json` | the middle layer (schema in 4a) |
| 4 | **Relationships** | `research/_predicates.json` | per edge id: `predicate`, `subject` (`a` or `b`, which fixes the direction the board has never recorded), `by`, `confidence`, `reviewed`. The edge's type, tier, sources and sentence are untouched |
| 5 | **Sources** | `research/_sources.json`, `research/_claim_sources.json` | a registry of documents (type, title, issuer, date, identifiers, URLs, archive URLs, node link, interested-party flag) and, per claim id, the source(s) plus a locator (page, timestamp). The original source string is always kept and shown |
| 6 | **Claim facts** | `research/_claim_meta.json` | per claim id: `claimant` (who asserts it), `artefactDate` (when the claim was made, which section 2a requires), `eventDate` (what it is about), `bookkeeping` (the 39 placeholder claims, excluded from counts) |
| 7 | **People facets** | `research/_person_facets.json` | `roles` (official, military, intelligence, scientist, contractor, legislator, witness, claimant, researcher, journalist, media) and `vitalStatus`, split from `roleStatus`. This settles the open `status` question [E] |
| 8 | **Entity resolution** | `research/_removed.json` aliases | the 25-40 duplicate clusters collapsed; aliases also applied to entityInfo, topics and side-cars |
| 9 | **Domain nodes into topics** | `absorbs` in topic records | each of the 59 `d-*` nodes is absorbed by a topic or becomes an explainer topic. Its claims show on the topic page, its edges become membership, and its node page redirects. The node records stay in the research files |
| 10 | **Orphaned essays** | `essays` in topic records | the 14 storyline essays in entityInfo attach to topics as explainer sections, subject to the same quotation and URL checks |
| 11 | **Places** | `research/_places.json` | coordinates for location nodes from a named public gazetteer (Natural Earth or GeoNames, cited), and event-to-place links from existing edges |
| 12 | **Validation** | `schema/research.schema.json` | closed vocabularies for every enumerated field. An unknown value is a reject, never a coercion; an unknown field is a warning until phase 3 ends, then a reject |

### 4a. The topic record

```json
{
  "id": "roswell-1947",
  "title": "Roswell, July 1947",
  "kind": "incident",
  "section": "early-record",
  "span": { "start": "1947-07", "end": "1997", "focus": "1947" },
  "places": ["l-roswell"],
  "lead": "One sentence a reader sees on the Atlas card.",
  "summary": [ { "text": "One sentence of prose.", "cites": ["c-0123456789"] } ],
  "record":    ["c-...", "c-..."],
  "claimed":   ["c-...", "c-..."],
  "contested": [ { "text": "...", "cites": ["c-..."] } ],
  "members":   [ { "id": "jesse-marcel", "role": "witness", "note": "optional one line" } ],
  "absorbs":   [],
  "essays":    [],
  "questions": [],
  "threads":   [],
  "related":   [],
  "provenance": { "drafted": "YYYY-MM-DD", "by": "agent or person", "reviewed": "YYYY-MM-DD or null" }
}
```

**The rules that keep topics honest** are mechanical checks, each shown failing on its own poison:

- every member exists, after alias resolution;
- every cited claim id exists and belongs to a member or to an edge between members;
- every summary and contested sentence carries at least one citation;
- **every quoted string in topic text appears verbatim in a cited claim**, which enforces "no invented
  quotes" mechanically;
- **no URL appears in topic text**, because URLs live in source records only;
- a topic with fewer than 6 members fails unless it is marked `research-gap`.

### 4b. The relationship vocabulary

There are 12 coarse edge types today and 2,539 distinct edge labels. The redesign adds a controlled
verb on top of both. The draft vocabulary is about 40 predicates in 11 families:

- **role**: worked for, led, served on, founded, advised
- **organisation**: part of, succeeded, ran, contracted
- **money**: funded
- **custody**: alleged to hold material, analysed material
- **law**: created, governs, amended, must report to
- **oversight**: oversaw, investigated, briefed
- **testimony**: testified at, made claims about, witnessed, corroborated, contradicted, brought to the
  record, interviewed
- **denial**: denied
- **documents**: wrote, published, released, concerns, cites
- **media**: reported on, appeared on
- **place and time**: located at, occurred at, preceded

Each predicate carries a forward and an inverse reader label, and the side-car's `subject` field fixes
which end each one reads from.

The existing `type` stays, because regrade and the checks use it. Predicates are presentation and
navigation; they never change a tier. Plan phase 7 finalises the vocabulary against the 35 verbs
authors actually wrote (`contains`, `testimony`, `authorship`, `amends`, `codifies`, and others), which
merge currently throws away.

### 4c. The source registry

- **Seeding.** It starts from what is already structured: edge sources are 97.7% URL and 88% dated, and
  494 documents are cited by both claims and edges. Claim strings are normalised onto the registry by URL
  and by document identifier (GAO numbers, CHRG hearing records, FOIA case numbers, CIA-RDP numbers,
  public laws, U.S.C. citations) [A].
- **The standing rule.** The registry never contains a URL that is not already in the board's data. A
  check compares every registry URL against the set of URLs found in `research/`, and it is poisoned
  with an invented one. Attaching a new URL to a source is a verification task, done the way the board
  always does: by opening the record.

## 5. The reader experience, page by page

**Home.**
- Search first, then one paragraph on what the board is and how to read a tier.
- **Start here**: the 14 signed-off flags become a reading tour through their topics, in the same order.
- The ten sections with their topics, a handful of big questions, what is new (the latest research
  passes), and the counts.

**Atlas.** Sections are rows and eras are columns (through 1969, 1970-1999, 2000-2016, 2017-2026).
- One chip per topic, in its focus era, ordered by date. A thin tail shows a topic that runs on: Roswell
  1947 carries its record to 1997.
- It uses the same visual language as the program map Stewart and Design liked: orthogonal, one label
  size, chips of one height. The difference is 75 topics instead of 135 nodes, so it fits.
- Focusing a chip draws its links to related topics (shared members) with the animated directional line
  Stewart likes. At rest, no lines.
- Filters: kind, contested, has open questions.
- At phone width it becomes stacked section lists.

**Topic.**
1. Header: kind, section, span, places, and counts by tier.
2. Lead, then a short summary whose every sentence cites claims (rendered as numbered references).
3. **The record | The claims | The contest**, three blocks side by side, stacked on a phone.
4. **Who and what**: members grouped by role (witnesses, claimants, officials, investigators,
   institutions, documents, laws, places, critics, media).
5. Chronology: member events inside the span, with their text.
6. The documents most cited by the topic's claims.
7. A small map of the members: backbone edges at rest, with testimony and denials as a toggle overlay.
8. Open questions and FOIA.
9. Related topics, and threads or questions passing through.
10. All claims on the topic's members, collapsed and filterable by tier.

**Entity.**
1. **Appears in** chips: the topics this node belongs to, primary first. This single line answers
   "where does this fit".
2. **About**: `whatItIs`, the narrative, and the case against (`conflicts`, `unverified`, denials), now
   actually shipped.
3. **Connections, grouped by relationship family**, each row carrying the counterpart, the sentence,
   dates, tier and a sources toggle, one row per counterpart.
   - A **Group by topic** toggle turns a connector hub (Grusch, AARO, DoD) into an index.
   - Backbone relationships come first; testimony and denials are a separate block.
4. Claims about it by tier, each linked to its source record. For a person, **claims they have made**,
   once claimant extraction lands.
5. Chronology (merged chronology, sequence chain and timeline slice), affiliations, and all open
   questions, with the FOIA request where one is drafted.

**Library and source pages.**
- **Library:** sources filtered by type, issuer, era and interested party.
- **Source page:** what the source is, its links, and every claim that rests on it, grouped by topic and
  entity.
- This is the page that makes the board credible to "respectable individuals".

**Directories.** People (by role, vital status, era, topic, testified under oath, claimant),
organisations, programmes, laws and directives, places, events. They are tables with facets, sorted by
date.

**Timeline.**
- Lanes are the ten sections, so the timeline and the Atlas share one grammar.
- Clicking an event shows its text and its node.
- Scope by topic gives that topic's chronology.
- Filters: section, topic, tier.

**Questions.** Eight to twelve big questions:
- Has the US recovered non-human craft?
- How could a programme be hidden from Congress?
- Who has testified under oath, and what corroborates them?
- Where would the money come from?
- What does the government deny, and how?
- What physical evidence is claimed?
- Which famous documents are genuine?
- What happened at nuclear sites?
- What do other governments say?
- Where does the popular story go beyond the record?
- What would settle it?

Each page gives a short cited answer, the evidence for, the evidence against, the open questions and
FOIA requests, and the threads as chains of evidence.

**Connect.** Up to three distinct paths between two entities.
- Paths are weighted to prefer Tier 1, backbone relationships and specific nodes over connector hubs.
- Every hop shows its sentence, tier and source.
- When the only route runs through a connector hub, the page says so plainly instead of implying a
  story.

**Neighbourhood.** The entity in the centre and its relationships grouped by family in labelled
columns, with the orthogonal animated lines kept. Hop 2 is opened one family at a time, never all at
once.

**Places.**
- A static map of the United States with a world inset. It is a bundled public-domain basemap with no
  tile server, so nothing new is fetched at runtime.
- It shows location nodes, the events that occurred there, and their topics.

**Analyst.**
- New tools: `topic`, `topics`, `source`, `question`, and predicate filters on `edges`.
- Chips for the new route kinds, proposed to Stewart as a table.
- `TOOLS_VERSION` bumps, so this needs a Worker redeploy by Stewart.

## 7. Engineering and process

- **One build command and one gate command.** `python tools/build.py` runs the declared order.
  `python tools/gate.py` runs the build, every data check, the app build, starts and stops its own
  preview server, runs C1 onward, and runs every poison. `--quick` skips the poisons for iteration; the
  full run is required before a merge. This ends the two-command gap that turned master red on
  19 September. In CI, the poisons split across parallel jobs to cut the 20-minute sweep.
- **FOIA links become their own build step.** Today they hide inside the old shell's build.
- **Build-date stamps go.** A content-only fingerprint already exists, and dropping the stamps removes
  most of `check_rebuild.py`'s reason to exist.
- **`CLAUDE.md` drops to 20 KB or less.** It keeps the rules, the build and gate, the working agreements,
  where things are, and a pointer to the current state. The history moves verbatim to
  `notes/HISTORY-TO-2026-09-24.md`. At 17k tokens per turn today, this is the largest single saving
  available to every future session.
- **`notes/STATE.md` is the one current-state file**, overwritten, never appended, at the end of every
  session: master commit, what is deployed, open branches, next phase. Dated handoffs stay as history
  only.
- **Checks.** The C-series continues, and every new check ships with a poison that damages the real
  page. Layout checks retire in the same commit as the surface they guard, together with their
  replacements. The data checks that must stay independent are restated in the audit, not imported.
- **Review site.** Every phase that changes what a reader sees deploys its branch to
  `the-program-next` and gets Stewart's look before merging (`DEPLOY.md`, "the same three lines with
  `--project-name the-program-next`", run against `git archive <branch> app`).

## 8. Risks, and how the plan contains them

| Risk | Containment |
|---|---|
| Agent-drafted topic summaries state things the board does not | every sentence cites claim ids; quotes must match cited claims verbatim; no URLs in topic text; an adversarial verifier looks for the unsupported sentence; Stewart reviews the section and topic table |
| Two agents agreeing is not corroboration (`CLAUDE.md` section 2) | the verifier works from the claims alone, not from the drafter's notes, and the mechanical checks do not depend on either |
| Relationship predicates misclassified | a closed vocabulary, confidence scores, an Opus review of every low-confidence edge plus a 10% sample. Predicates never change tiers or sources |
| The source registry merges two different documents | only exact identifier or URL matches merge automatically; fuzzy candidates go to review; the original string is always kept and displayed |
| Deep links break | no route is removed; aliases resolve old ids; `#/map` redirects only after the Atlas ships |
| Layout checks churn | retired in the same commit as their surface, with replacements; poisons kept |
| Long programme, value arrives late | phase 1 ships visible meaning on every node page; each phase ships on its own and leaves the board better and green |
| Session cost | slim `CLAUDE.md`, `STATE.md`, self-contained phase sections, the model ladder, coordinators for fan-outs |
| The harness refuses a push to master | work on a branch, gate green, then ask Stewart for the push in one line |

## 9. Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **A. Repair the graph interface only** (group connections, trim L0, fix labels) | cheap, but it adds no middle layer and the top level stays a map of nodes that cannot grow. Kept as phase 1, rejected as the end state |
| **B. Topic-first atlas** (this document) | **chosen**: it matches the measured structure (17 real communities, 98% topical slugs), keeps the design language Stewart likes, and scales by adding topics |
| **C. Move to an off-the-shelf knowledge base** (a wiki, Obsidian publish, Kumu, Wikibase) | mature interfaces, but it loses the tier and provenance discipline the checks encode, the analyst's in-browser tools, and the design, at a large migration cost. Rejected |
| **D. Question-first argument map** | compelling for an evidence board, but most records (institutions, laws, places) are not arguments. Adopted as a layer (Questions), not the spine |
| **E. A pure faceted database explorer** | good for experts, poor for discovery. Adopted as the Directories and the Evidence table, not the spine |
